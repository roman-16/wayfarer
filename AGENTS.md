# Wayfarer

A personal modpack repository: it reproduces on any machine from `direnv allow` and `just`, and nothing outside it needs a manual step.

## The rules of the pack

- **The design decides.** `design/` holds what the pack is becoming, and `design/README.md` says the phase and what comes next. Build only what is Settled there; a gap found while building goes back to Open in its document, never decided in code. Design work runs through the `design` skill.
- **The lore decides.** `design/lore.md` is the source of truth. Every mechanic needs a reason there, and every quest, book and item text is written from it with the `lore` skill. A mechanic the lore cannot explain is a mechanic to drop or to give a reason first.
- **The world looks like Minecraft.** Every block, item, creature, skin, model, animation and structure is made with the `art` skill and judged in the game beside its vanilla relative (`design/style.md`, L8).
- **The interface wears the stamp.** Everything drawn over the world, the pack's HUD and screens and vanilla's and the mods' interfaces, is one design system, Ash and Ember (`design/style.md`): its tokens in `style.toml`, its sprites drawn by the generator, its fonts m5x7 and Alagard, every colour in the interface code taken from `Stamp`. Each look is made with the `art` skill and judged on the style board and where the player meets it.
- **Everything sounds like one game.** Every sound and piece the pack chooses is its own, new to the player and free to use, put to the user by ear with the `sound` skill, and heard in the game beside what plays around it, vanilla's and the pack's mods' alike, so the whole pack sounds coherent; the story's own music plays only at its moments (`design/sound.md`). The voice is recorded once, last, from the final texts, since every recording costs money (`design/narration.md`).
- **There is no Nether.** Nothing may bring it back: no lit portals, no Nether structures, no recipe that needs a Nether-only item without an Overworld source. `just test` audits every recipe in every jar against `generator/nether.toml` and every structure that still generates for a lit portal; a recipe finding is fixed by an Overworld source or accepted there as unobtainable, a portal by cutting its structure in `generator/owners.toml`, never ignored.
- **Every number lives once.** Balancing numbers are in `balance.toml`, and the mod, the generator and the boot test all read them from there. Eyes, their biomes, lore and hall spacing are in `generator/eyes.toml`. The interface's colours, alphas and timings are in `style.toml`, which the generator draws the stamp's sprites from and the mod reads through `Stamp`. A number that tunes something is a power of two, or a sum of two such as 128 + 64 = 192; a number that means something stays as it is, such as time people read, a share like two thirds, a count the story sets, or a fact about vanilla or a mod. A system's numbers move to this when it is rebuilt.
- **Hall spacing is measured, not guessed.** Biome layout and the sea decide how far the next hall really is, so each act's `spacing` (`generator/eyes.toml` `[acts]`, Acts 2 and 3, whose halls grow in bands) is tuned by `just survey apply`, which walks journeys from the inner edge of the act's band to the nearest hall not yet taken and compares the legs with the targets in `balance.toml` `[halls]`. One survey is noisy (about ±30%): apply, `just generate`, survey again, and stop once the medians sit near the targets.
- **Every mechanic has its edge cases.** What a player may do while it runs is part of its design: walk off, go underground, sit in a tent, die, log out, untrack its quest, reload the world mid-step, or a teammate doing the opposite. A design entry, a plan and a build name each of them and what happens, and the finish walkthrough tries them.
- **Singleplayer and server behave the same.** Rules live in the mod jar (code and data) or in `pack/config`, never in a world save or a client-only setting.

## Where things go

- `mod/src/generated/`, `pack/CREDITS.md`, `pack/config/EndRemastered-NeoForge/Eyes/`, `pack/config/iris.properties`, the shader's options in `pack/shaderpacks/` (from `generator/shader.toml`), `pack/config/nohostilesaroundcampfire.json5` and `pack/config/sparsestructures.json5` are written by `just generate`. Change the generator or its sources (`story/books.toml` for the books, `story/sounds.toml` for the music and the cues, `style.toml` for the stamp, `generator/fonts.toml` for the fonts), never the output.
- `generator/owners.toml` gives every structure kind of every mod an owner, or cuts it; the owners spaced in `balance.toml` `[structures.every_blocks]` share one placement each. `generator/removals.toml` cuts items, creatures, enchantments, loot tables and recipes (also those that name what it cuts or a mod the pack leaves out, so they never fail to load), and names the blocks that take no root and those that keep only in a tent. A mod added to the pack has its structures and anything that breaks a rule of the road entered there, and its keys, chat messages, HUD elements, starting items and random events weighed against the design: every key it adds is entered in `pack/config/defaultoptions/keybindings.txt`, unbound unless the user keeps it because a player really needs it, such as Combat Roll's roll or a key that acts only in the mod's own screen or tooltip, so the file holds every key of the pack; and a greeting, a starting item or an event the design does not have is switched off in the mod's config.
- The title's panoramas in `mod/src/main/resources/assets/minecraft/textures/gui/title/background/<time>/`, one set for each time of day, of which the title shows one at random each time the game starts, are photographs of the world, written by `python3 scripts/client.py panorama`, never drawn by hand; the title's logo is drawn by the generator with the stamp.
- `generator/noise.toml` lists the lines other mods log on every launch or world that are known and harmless, each with its mod and why; a line of the pack's own is fixed at its cause, never listed.
- `gallery/` shows the user every facet of what a player sees: its pictures and pages are written by `just gallery` from `gallery/shots.toml`, never by hand, as the `finish` skill says.
- `pack/` is managed by packwiz. Add a mod with `packwiz modrinth add <slug>` from `pack/`; use CurseForge only for a mod that is not on Modrinth. Every mod is `client` or `both`: singleplayer runs the server inside the client, so a `server` mod would be missing there.
- A new config for a mod starts from the default the mod writes on its first boot (`build/server/config` after `just test`, `build/client/config` after `just showroom`). Ship only the keys the pack changes when the mod fills in the rest itself, so machine-specific defaults (thread counts and the like) stay per machine.

## Reading the game and the mods

`just sources` writes Minecraft's and NeoForge's code with Parchment names and every mod decompiled with Vineflower, each with its assets and data and the jars it bundles (under `META-INF/jars/<jar>/`, where some mods keep all their code), into `build/sources/` (`neoforge-<version>/` and `mods/<jar>/`); a mod is decompiled again only when its version changes. Search it with `rg`, naming the path: `rg` skips `build/` and every other generated folder through `.gitignore`.

```console
$ just sources
$ rg "enter_when_safe" build/sources/mods
```

`just registries` writes what the running pack contains into `build/reports/`, from every mod: `registries/<namespace>/<path>.json` for every registry, a list of its entries for those made in code (blocks, items, creatures) and every entry's content for those made from data (structures, structure sets, biomes, loot tables and the rest); `tags/<namespace>/<path>.json` with every tag's members; and `recipes.json`. An entry the game cannot write back holds its error instead. It starts the dev server and stops it after, so run it while the dev server is stopped.

```console
$ just registries
$ rg --count '"trek:' build/reports/registries/minecraft/worldgen/structure.json
```

## Seeing the game

Never wait by guessing. No `sleep`, and no `wait` in a driver batch to give something time to finish: a guessed wait is either too short and flaky or too long and slow. Every step returns the moment its effect is done, by waiting on the event itself (the server's reply, a freshly drawn frame, the next compass reading) or by checking about every second until it holds, and a wait that does not finish in time fails and says where it stuck: a driver step that makes no progress for 60 s fails the batch, naming the step and the state of the game. When a step returns before its effect shows, fix the step so it waits for that effect, never the batch; `wait` is only for watching something that has no end, such as an animation. A run that must outlive an interrupted tool call, such as a long `just gallery`, starts detached (`setsid nohup just gallery ... > /tmp/gallery.log 2>&1 < /dev/null & echo $! > /tmp/gallery.pid`) and is waited on by its exit (`tail --pid="$(cat /tmp/gallery.pid)" -f /dev/null`), never by a loop that sleeps.

Never wait for a failure that is already known. Code that knows something failed says so the moment it knows, in one line `<what> failed: <why>` (`camp failed: no spot ahead is out of sight, ...`), and both drivers end the batch on that line at once, with it as the error, and exit non-zero; a refusal or a note on the way (`camp: the spot at ... falls short`) is worded otherwise and lets the batch go on. Gates and builds run in the foreground with a generous timeout and return when they finish; the dev client is driven through `scripts/client.py`:

```console
$ python3 scripts/client.py start            # open the showroom, in the running client or a new one
$ python3 scripts/client.py run 'fly on' '/tp @s 48.5 12 60 0 20' settle 'shot row'
$ python3 scripts/client.py stop             # returns once the game has exited
$ python3 scripts/client.py tour [eye|camp ...]   # open the showroom, photograph every hall and the Last Camp
$ python3 scripts/client.py panorama           # photograph the title's panoramas at the Last Camp, one for each time of day
$ python3 scripts/client.py bench [--minimum]  # measure every graphics step's frames, or Low and Medium on the minimum machine
```

One client stays up between batches, so a test pays for a launch only when it must. `start` first swaps changed code into the running client, then opens the world it was asked for in place: the showroom, a fresh world (`--fresh`, or `--seed <n>` to grow it from that seed) or a state (`--state`), in seconds. It launches a new client only when none runs, when the pack's mods or configs, the mod's build or a file the game reads while it starts (the stamp's tokens in `style.json`) changed, when the screen or mode is another (`--visible`, `--far`, `--lean`), or when a change needs a restart, and says why. Changed textures, models, fonts, sounds or lang are reloaded in place, and changed data or story opens the world again: the mod reads its own data files (the story, `balance.toml`, the gear, the plans of Tollbridge and the rest) each time a world starts. The client, the dev server and `just test` run beside each other, so none is stopped for another. Between batches a hidden client draws at 5 frames a second, and after 30 minutes without a batch it quits on its own, as the dev server does; `stop` ends either at once. `just showroom`, `just play` and `just devserver` stop the driven client or dev server first, since they use the same folders.

The game runs on a hidden screen from `start` until `stop`: gamescope's headless screen from the system (`programs.gamescope.enable`), 1,920 by 1,080 and drawn by the graphics card like the user's own, so it never takes over the user's display and runs whether anyone is logged in or not; `start --visible` and `tour --visible` put it on the user's display, drawn by the graphics card. A hidden game is silent (master volume 0, which Reactive Music's own player follows too); a visible one plays at half volume. A launch that meets a dialog on the way into its world fails at once and names it, since nobody can click it on the hidden screen; the one-time warning that a world uses experimental settings, which every showroom save shows because of its extra worlds, is confirmed by the driver and never comes again.

`start --lean` on either driver, and `just showroom lean` or `just play lean`, load only the Wayfarer mod and what it needs (Curios and Easy NPC, from the dev classpath) with an empty mods folder, for work that does not need the rest of the pack: the client starts in about 23 s instead of 34, the dev server in about 11 s instead of 16. They play in worlds of their own (`showroom-lean`, `play-lean`, `world-lean`), since opening a world without the mods it was made with damages it.

Every dev client (`just showroom`, `just play` and the driver) runs with test settings that `scripts/instance.py` lays over `pack/config` in `build/client`: no shaders, no Distant Horizons (it and its SeedGen stay out of the mods folder), 8 chunks, in a window. `start --far` on the client driver, and `just showroom far` or `just play far`, keep Distant Horizons on, its far view cut to 1,024 blocks; `just play shipped` keeps the far view, the shader and the full screen as the pack ships them; the `far` step waits until it is drawn, which around a new spot takes one and a half to two minutes, hidden or visible. The shipped pack keeps its shaders, its far view and 12 chunks, so those are judged in the exported pack (`just pack`, imported into Prism Launcher).

gamescope comes from the system, never from devbox, since it loads the graphics driver's own libraries and devbox's packages are older than the system's. The justfile sets `JAVA_HOME` to devbox's Java for every task, and both drivers launch the game and compile through just recipes (`just showroom`, `just play`, `just devserver`, `just compile`), so a shell's own `JAVA_HOME` never matters.

`run` prints one line of JSON per step, as soon as the step is done: the step, and lists of what it produced, such as every chat line the game received while it ran (command replies included) under `chat`, every conversation line and set of answers the game showed under `talk` (`<Agna> ...`, `answers: Who are you? | ...`), errors under `error`, the absolute path of each screenshot, written before `run` returns, under `screenshot`, and the step's own result under its name (`inspect`, `log`, `perf`, `pos`, `state`, `sheet`). A command step waits until the server has run it, so its replies always sit under it. Steps can also come one per line on stdin. `start --fresh` opens a fresh natural world in survival (`just play`) instead of the showroom (`just showroom`). A batch that does not finish within `--timeout` is aborted and its log shows where it stuck.

The story runs in every world whose game rule `wayfarerStory` is on, as it is by default; the showroom's save turns it off, so neither the story nor its clock runs there. Its keys are J for the journal (`key.wayfarer.journal`), M for the map (`key.wayfarer.map`) and Z, the key left of X on every layout, to hold up the ember (`key.wayfarer.ember_sense`).

`start --state <quest/step>` on either driver opens the world at a step of the story, such as `ash/the_door`; every step of a main quest is a state. Its first start grows a fresh world from the seed in `story/states.toml` and plays each earlier step's outcome without waiting for it: every talk's first answer, the first words offered for the stranger's stone, the first camp's fire, Tollbridge and the caravan's camp built at once, the clock at the hour the step leaves it at, every reward taken, every control the task panel asked for along the way learned (the journal, the inventory, the map, the compass, an answer's number, a glowing sign's right-click, the Gear page), so a state's panel asks only for what its own step asks. It keeps that world in `build/states/`, then begins the step with the team standing where it begins. Later starts copy the kept world in seconds, until what its world is made from changes: the mod's data, the story, `balance.toml` or the pack, never the textures, models, fonts, sounds or lang. A state not yet kept is built from the nearest earlier step that is, and a build keeps every step it passes, so one build to a late step makes every step before it quick. The mod's code is not part of it, since each step begins with the code that runs; after a change to code that builds an earlier step's world, such as Tollbridge's town or the caravan's camp, `--rebuild` builds the state anew. Building one from a fresh world takes one and a half to two minutes, where on the dev server a pretend player named Wayfarer walks it and leaves, so the dev client takes its place on joining. `/wayfarer story to <quest/step>` and `/wayfarer story next` move a running world on the same way, and the step `story <quest/step>` of either driver returns once it stands there. `/wayfarer story marsh <x> <z>` puts the Marsh Mothers' hall down again under that spot, to try it in a real swamp when the states' seed only makes a bog. `/wayfarer story side <quest/step>` sets a quest at one of its steps, its talk begun again and `/wayfarer step skip <quest>` finishes the step it stands at, so a side quest's late steps are tried without playing the ones before; `/wayfarer story gift <gift>` gives the team a shard's gift, to try it without its hall; `/wayfarer story choose <quest/step> <answer>` makes a choice stand as answered, such as `the_hollow/pip lost`, to try what follows another answer than a state's first.

```console
$ python3 scripts/client.py start --state ash/the_door
the client is running in state ash/the_door, from the cache, on a hidden screen (opened in 21 s, 3 log lines, 0 errors)
```

Everything the story waits on counts game ticks: a talk's lines, a hold's waves, the clock passing to dusk or dawn. Vanilla's `/tick rate <ticks a second>` runs them faster, so checking that a timed step works takes a fraction of its length (`/tick rate 80` runs a hold's waves about four times as fast), and `/tick rate 20` sets it back. Pace and feel are judged at 20, never fast-forwarded.

The client opens in the showroom dimension `wayfarer:showroom`: the Last Camp at spawn, the stranger's first and last camps just south of it at z = 24 (x = -16 and 8), each with its name sign on the north side, an item frame wall at z = -10, and the halls in alphabetical order along x at 40-block steps (hall centres at x = 48, 88, ...; z = 72; the surface at y = 1, each hall as deep as its way down needs), each with its name sign on the north side. The compass works there as a demo: it points at a waymark 750 blocks west of the camp, shows a companion circling the camp and marks the camp's grave. `/locate` finds nothing there; the natural overworld next to it (`/execute in minecraft:overworld run ...`) is kept between launches.

The workshop (`wayfarer:workshop`) sits beside the showroom in the same save: empty, flat, a smooth stone floor, noon and clear sky, wiped at every launch. Designs line up along x from the origin, 4 blocks apart, each with its name sign 2 blocks south of it, so the vanilla relative put in with `beside` stands next to the design it is judged against:

```console
$ python3 scripts/client.py run workshop 'place wayfarer:last_camp' 'spawn minecraft:villager' 'views camp' clear
```

`tour` shoots each hall from its surface cover down the shaft into the hall, and the Last Camp from every side, into one labelled contact sheet per subject in `build/client/tour/<subject>.jpg`; the hall cameras come from the generator (`mod/src/generated/dev/wayfarer/tour.json`), so every hall is framed from open air with a clear line to what it looks at. It starts the client itself and quits it when done; a whole tour takes about three minutes.

`panorama` grows the panorama's world from its seed, photographs the Last Camp from its camera at each time of day (`PANORAMA_SEED`, `PANORAMA_CAMERA` and `PANORAMA_TIMES` in `scripts/client.py`) in spectator mode under a clear sky with the fog held even across the six faces, at the Preset's Ultra with the shader and the far view fully drawn (the client's `shipped` mode), on a hidden 2,048 by 2,048 screen drawn by the graphics card (gamescope from the system, so it never takes over the user's display), reduces each face to 256 colours with `pngquant` and writes each set into its own folder in `mod/src/main/resources/assets/minecraft/textures/gui/title/background/`; run it again after a change to the land or the camp, and judge every set on the title before keeping it. The title shows one set at random each time the game starts; every dev run shows the first by name, so the gallery's title stays the same.

`bench` measures what the frame rate does where the far view works hardest: in a fresh world from the states' seed, on the hidden screen in the `shipped` mode, it first measures 32 s of the world opening, then picks each step of the Preset in the running game with the `graphics` step, puts the player at a height of 256 blocks over where the world spawned, looking down at the land, measures 32 s hovering there while the far view comes in and 32 s flying on over new land at about a horse's pace, each step in a direction of its own, and fails at once when the player did not get far, as when something stood in the way. It prints one row per step and place with the average frame rate, the 1% low and the worst frame of each half, naming at the row's end any other program that used 5% or more of the graphics card meanwhile, and quits the client when done. Alone it runs every step, the shader on for High and Ultra, against the 90 frames a second `design/production.md` R1 gives a high-end PC; `bench --minimum` runs Low and Medium with the shader off on R1's minimum machine (an RTX 2070 Super laptop card, an i7-10750H), alone and on the dev server started with `--far`, against R1's floor: the game is launched believing it has the minimum machine's 12 threads and held to the processor's slower cores (`--cpus` names others), the dev server to the rest, and the graphics card's clock is held at the minimum machine's speed: when nobody is at the PC (no graphical login session, local or over RDP, that is not idle), the bench locks it itself with `sudo nvidia-smi --lock-gpu-clocks=1100,1100` and resets it on the way out, even when stopped; when someone is, the clock is theirs, so the bench fails unless they have locked it (`nvidia-smi` cannot tell a lock from an idle card before the game runs). A whole `bench --minimum` takes about 9 minutes, most of it starting the game and the dev server.

| Step | Does |
|---|---|
| `/<command>` | Runs a command as the player |
| `assets` | Reloads every texture, model and sound, as F3+T does, and returns once the reload is done and the loading screen has faded |
| `await <text>` | Waits for a chat line or a conversation's line (`talk`) containing the text, such as a quest's reply that comes on the server's own rhythm or `answers: Who are you`; each line answers one `await`, so a second `await` of the same text waits for the next such line |
| `beside <thing>` | Puts a creature, structure, template, block or item, usually its vanilla relative, at the workshop's next free spot |
| `chat on\|off` | Shows or hides the chat on the screen; the game still receives it |
| `clear` | Empties the workshop and puts its floor back |
| `board <page>` | Opens the style board, every component of the stamp in every state, on its page: `book`, `hud`, `type` or `widgets` |
| `click <x> <y> [button]` | Moves the mouse to GUI coordinates on the open screen and clicks there as a player's mouse does, with the left button or the one named (`middle`, `right`) |
| `close` | Closes every open screen, back to the world |
| `compare <a> <b>` | Counts the pixels that differ between two screenshots and writes a picture of the difference |
| `face <x> <y> <z>` | Turns the player's view towards a block, at absolute coordinates or relative ones, as moving the mouse does, so it also steers what the player rides |
| `far` | Waits until Distant Horizons has generated and drawn the far view around the player, or on a server has fetched and drawn it (only with the far view on, in the `far` and `shipped` modes) |
| `digest` | Returns a fingerprint of what the client has been told and holds: the compass, the task panel, the banners, the journal, the inventory and where the player stands |
| `distance <chunks>` | Sets the render distance for this launch and returns once every chunk within it has arrived |
| `find <block> [radius]` | Lists the nearest positions of a block within the horizontal radius (default 32), at any height |
| `fly on\|off` | Toggles creative flight |
| `graphics <step> shaders on\|off` | Picks a step of the Wayfarer page's Preset (`low`, `medium`, `high`, `ultra`) and turns the shader on or off, as a player does in Video Settings |
| `forget` | Forgets where the journal was left, its ribbon and chosen entries, as joining a world does |
| `gui on\|off` | Shows or hides the HUD |
| `hold <mapping> on\|off` | Presses a key mapping and holds it down, or lets it go, as a player's key does: `hold key.use on` looks through a held spyglass or draws a bow, `hold key.spyglass-improvements.use on` looks through the spyglass from the charm slot |
| `hud` | Waits for the compass reading the server sends after the commands before it and returns it as JSON: its mode and markers |
| `inspect block <x> <y> <z>\|creature <type>\|item\|inventory` | Returns as JSON a block with its block entity, the nearest creature of a type with its data (a mob's also with its target and the goals it runs), the held item, or every filled inventory slot |
| `interact <creature>` | Right-clicks the nearest creature of that type within 8 blocks, as a player does |
| `join <address>` | Leaves the world and joins the server at that address, such as the dev server at `localhost:25566`, and returns once the player stands in its world |
| `key <mapping>` | Presses a key mapping (`key.inventory`) and returns once the game has taken the press and drawn a frame after it; with a screen open it presses the mapping's key on that screen, as a player's keyboard does; `key escape` closes the open screen |
| `leave` | Leaves the world for the title screen, saving it first, and returns once the title stands |
| `loading [new]` | Opens a loading screen that stands still, with the recap of the world just left or, with `new`, the line a new world shows |
| `log` | Returns the warnings and errors the game logged since the last `log`, the newest 64 kept, leaving out the known lines of other mods listed in `generator/noise.toml` |
| `panorama <name>` | Photographs the six faces of a title-screen panorama from where the player stands, each at a field of view of 90 degrees, the square middle of the window's frame scaled to 2,048 pixels, into `build/client/screenshots/panorama/<name>/`, each face once its terrain is drawn |
| `particles <all\|decreased\|minimal>` | Sets how many particles the game draws |
| `perf [seconds]` | Measures the frames drawn in the world over that many seconds (one by default), leaving out those with a screen open: the average frame rate, the 1% low (the average of the slowest hundredth) and the worst frame, with the server's tick time and which renderer draws |
| `person <skin> [pose]` | Puts a person at the workshop's next free spot: an Easy NPC body wearing a skin from `generator/skins.toml`, with its model and size, standing or in an Easy NPC pose (`sitting`, `rest`, `sleeping` and the rest) |
| `place <thing>` | Puts a structure, template, block or item at the workshop's next free spot, with its name sign |
| `pause` | Opens the pause menu, as Escape does in the world |
| `point <x> <y>` | Moves the mouse to GUI coordinates on the open screen without clicking |
| `pos` | Logs the player's position, facing and dimension |
| `press <label>` | Clicks the widget with that label on the open screen, lists included, once the screen has been drawn and the widget is enabled, waiting up to two seconds for a screen still filling in, such as the journal right after a world opens; a label that does not appear is an error |
| `quit` | Closes the client |
| `scale <n>` | Sets the interface scale (2, 3 or 4, as the art skill judges a screen at), widening the window when a scale needs more room than 1280 by 720, and returns once the game draws at that scale |
| `screen` | Logs the open screen and its widgets with labels, positions and sizes |
| `settle` | Waits until earlier commands have run on the server, the chunks around the player are loaded and the terrain is drawn |
| `shot <name>` | Saves a screenshot of a frame drawn after every step before it |
| `skin <face> [player]` | Dresses the player, or the named pretend player, in a face from `generator/faces.toml` on the client only, its texture loaded as the game loads a downloaded skin, so the glowing eyes are judged on any skin |
| `sky <ticks>` | Watches the client's clock for that many ticks and returns how far it moved, the least and most in one tick, and the speed the server set |
| `sneak on\|off` | Holds the sneak key down or lets it go, so the next `use` or `interact` is a sneaking one |
| `spawn <creature> [walking]` | Puts a creature at the workshop's next free spot, standing still, or walking with `walking` |
| `state` | Returns the world's story state: every save file of the Wayfarer mod as JSON |
| `still [ticks]` | Freezes the world and holds what moves on its own: animated textures on their first frame, no particles, the HUD's motion and every mod's server ticks; with ticks it first runs the world, hurried, to that many ticks after it opened, so each opening is shot at the same moment |
| `story <quest/step>` | Plays the story to that step, as a state is built, and returns once it stands there |
| `type <text>` | Types into the focused field |
| `use <x> <y> <z>` | Right-clicks the block there, at absolute coordinates or relative ones (`~2 ~ ~`) as in vanilla's commands, with what the player holds, on its open face nearest the player, as a player does: a tent's door, a campfire, a lectern, a sign |
| `views <name>` | Photographs everything in the workshop, creatures included, from the front, the side, above and three-quarters, each camera fitted to it at the pack's field of view, into `build/client/workshop/<name>.jpg` |
| `wait <ticks>` | Waits a fixed time; only for watching something with no end, such as an animation, never to let a step finish |
| `workshop` | Takes the player into the workshop, south of what stands there |

The driver (`mod/src/dev/java/.../Driver.java`) confirms commands with a sentinel (`execute if entity @s`, whose reply it hides), so `sendCommandFeedback` must stay on. After `just generate`, `start` copies the new resources and reloads or reopens what they change; within a batch, `just compile` (which copies the resources too) and the step `/reload` load new data into the running world, and new chunks and `/place structure` then use it.

Changed Java code goes into a running game without a restart: `python3 scripts/client.py swap` or `python3 scripts/server.py swap` compiles the Wayfarer mod and swaps every changed class that is already loaded into the client or the dev server, over a debug port (`client_swap_port` and `server_swap_port` in `mod/gradle.properties`). The JetBrains runtime from devbox takes changed method bodies and added or removed methods and fields, and HotswapAgent (`hotswap_agent_version` in `mod/gradle.properties`, in the dev runs only) gives a new static field its starting value. A class whose `@SubscribeEvent` methods changed is registered on the game's event bus again. It prints one line of JSON: what was swapped, what loads fresh when first used, what is new, which classes listen again, and what needs a restart. Mixins always need one, and so does a loaded class whose initialiser changes otherwise than by adding fields, such as a changed starting value or a removed field, or whose initialiser registers something (the mod's main class, a key mapping, the driver), since an initialiser does not run again (the swap names it under `needs a restart` instead of swapping it), and anything the game reads only at startup; changed command code takes effect after `/reload`, which registers the commands again.

The dev server is driven the same way through `scripts/server.py`: the whole pack but Distant Horizons and its SeedGen, as on the dev client (`start --far` adds them, in a world of its own, `world-far`, so a client that joins draws the far view the server sends), with the Wayfarer mod's dev code, in `build/devserver` on port 25566, so it can run beside `just test` and a dev client can join it at `localhost:25566`. It runs headless and keeps its world between launches (`start --fresh` deletes it, and `start --seed <n>` grows a fresh one from that seed, to try a place the story puts down on other land). Each step of `run` is a console command, returns the moment the server has run it, and prints one line of JSON with its replies; `await <text>` waits for a later reply containing the text, such as an asynchronous `/locate`'s, each reply answering one `await`; `state` returns the world's story state as on the client, and `story <quest/step>` plays the story to that step. `start` keeps a running server, after swapping in changed code, unless its world is to change (`--state`, `--fresh`, `--seed`), its mods or data changed, or a change needs a restart.

```console
$ python3 scripts/server.py start            # keep the running server or launch one; returns once it is ready
$ python3 scripts/server.py run 'locate structure wayfarer:hall/old' 'await The nearest wayfarer:hall/old'
{"step": "locate structure wayfarer:hall/old", "replies": ["The nearest wayfarer:hall/old is at [4608, ~, 384] (4400 blocks away)"], "seconds": 0.7}
$ python3 scripts/server.py stop             # returns once the server has exited
```

Pretend players stand in for a team on the dev server: `wayfarer pretend join <name>` brings one online through the game's own login, so every mod reacts as to a person; `wayfarer pretend leave <name>` logs it out, `wayfarer pretend respawn <name>` presses Respawn after a death, and `wayfarer pretend answer <name> <n>` picks the nth answer standing, so a team's vote can be played. They do what vanilla commands tell them (`tp`, `spreadplayers`, `execute as <name> run ...`), every message one receives comes back as a reply (`Mo hears: ...` for chat, `Mo sees the subtitle: ...` and `Mo sees the answers: ...` for a conversation), and a returning name comes back where and as it left, so one that left dead joins dead until `wayfarer pretend respawn` brings it back. They never move on their own, not even to fall, so put them on the ground with `spreadplayers`; the chunks around them load wherever a command puts them, and a change of world completes for them, as a person's would. They join the dev client's own world the same way, beside the real player and its chat.

```console
$ python3 scripts/server.py run 'wayfarer pretend join Mo' 'spreadplayers 0 0 0 4 false Mo' 'execute as Mo run say hello'
```

`scripts/drive.py` is the library both drivers and the boot test share: launching and stopping a game, crash reports, and a server's console, whose commands and replies go through `driver/console` and `driver/server.log` in the server's folder.

## Gates

The gates are `just lint`, then `just test`. Development runs neither and shoots no gallery: each change is checked in the running game as far as it needs, working and with nothing new in `log`. The `finish` skill, which the user calls before committing, runs the rest over everything uncommitted: the gates, a newcomer's walkthrough of what a player meets, and the gallery's shots for what changed. `just test` runs the mod's game tests beside the boot test, which builds the server pack, audits recipes, portals and that the Wayfarer mod loads after every mod whose files it replaces, boots the whole pack headless and checks with `/locate` that the stronghold ring sits at its radius, that every hall generates and that every cut structure is gone, its searches eight at a time through Async Locator. Among the game tests, the face test runs the eye finder over every face in `generator/faces.toml` (the default skins, the pack's people, the user's skin and community skins, fetched once into `~/.cache/wayfarer/faces/`) and prints how often the glow lands on the eyes. It fails when the game tests did not pass, crashed or never ran, and it returns at once, saying so, when nothing the server or the game tests load (the mod jar without its client code and assets, the dev code, the pack's mods and configs, the boot scripts and their sources) changed since its last pass, kept in `build/passed.json`. The boot test plays the states' seed from `story/states.toml`, so a skipped run would have checked the same world. The server pack is put together from links to `~/.cache/wayfarer`, NeoForge's server install kept there once per version. `just pack` is no gate: it exports the pack for the user to play, and runs when the user says they will play or asks for it.

What no test can see - shaders, sound, the HUD, the feel of a night, the balance - is judged in game by the user; the `playtest` skill is the checklist. Look through the driver first, so the user only judges what a screenshot cannot show.
