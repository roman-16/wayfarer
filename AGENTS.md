# Wayfarer

A personal modpack repository: it reproduces on any machine from `direnv allow` and `just`, and nothing outside it needs a manual step.

## The rules of the pack

- **The design decides.** `design/` holds what the pack is becoming, and `design/README.md` says the phase and what comes next. Build only what is Settled there; a gap found while building goes back to Open in its document, never decided in code. Design work runs through the `design` skill.
- **The lore decides.** `design/lore.md` is the source of truth. Every mechanic needs a reason there, and every quest, book and item text is written from it with the `lore` skill. A mechanic the lore cannot explain is a mechanic to drop or to give a reason first.
- **The world looks like Minecraft.** Every block, item, creature, skin, model, animation and structure is made with the `art` skill and judged in the game beside its vanilla relative (`design/style.md`, L8).
- **The interface wears the stamp.** Everything drawn over the world, the pack's HUD and screens and vanilla's and the mods' interfaces, is one design system, Ash and Ember (`design/style.md`): its tokens in `style.toml`, its sprites drawn by the generator, its fonts m5x7 and Alagard, every colour in the interface code taken from `Stamp`. Each look is made with the `art` skill and judged on the style board and where the player meets it.
- **Everything sounds like one game.** Every sound and piece the pack chooses is its own, new to the player and free to use, put to the user by ear with the `sound` skill, and heard in the game beside what plays around it, vanilla's and the pack's mods' alike, so the whole pack sounds coherent; the story's own music plays only at its moments (`design/sound.md`). Every line is read aloud in a voice designed for the pack and carries its performance as audio tags in its text (`[sighs]`, `[whispers]`), which the game never shows (`design/narration.md`). Recording waits for the user's word, so credits are spent once, on words that have settled: a change that writes or rewrites a line runs `just voice prune` in the same change, which deletes the clips of changed texts, and the line stays silent, as a new speaker stays without a voice, until the user says to record. Then the `sound` skill puts each new voice to the user by ear, `just voice record` records every silent line and checks each take against its words, and the user hears every scene it changed; `just lint` names every line and speaker still waiting.
- **There is no Nether.** Nothing may bring it back: no lit portals, no Nether structures, no recipe that needs a Nether-only item without an Overworld source. `just test` audits every recipe in every jar against `generator/nether.toml` and every structure that still generates for a lit portal; a recipe finding is fixed by an Overworld source or accepted there as unobtainable, a portal by cutting its structure in `generator/owners.toml`, never ignored.
- **Every number lives once.** Balancing numbers are in `balance.toml`, and the mod, the generator and the boot test all read them from there. Eyes, their biomes and lore are in `generator/eyes.toml`. The interface's colours, alphas and timings are in `style.toml`, which the generator draws the stamp's sprites from and the mod reads through `Stamp`. A number that tunes something is a power of two, or a sum of two such as 128 + 64 = 192; a number that means something stays as it is, such as time people read, a share like two thirds, a count the story sets, or a fact about vanilla or a mod. A system's numbers move to this when it is rebuilt.
- **Every walk is set by its quest.** Every hall and story place is put down by its quest a set distance ahead of the caravan, out of sight, on the nearest fitting ground of its act's land (Act 1's on the home land, those of Acts 2 and 3 on the outer continent beyond the sea), within `balance.toml` `[halls]` `reach_blocks`; the world grows none of them. A place's distance is the `ahead_blocks` of its own table, named after the place, which `just lint` holds the story's words to ("a morning's walk"). A walk's length is tuned against its quest's budget from the play report (`just play-report`, `design/production.md` R6), never by moving the world.
- **The world's shape is measured, not guessed.** A change to `balance.toml` `[world]`, the ring's radius or the oceans in `pack/config/tectonic.json` is measured before it is kept, with `/wayfarer shape <seed> 24576 128` on the dev server on the states' seed and the five others in `design/systems.md` M39, and held to M39's table: one outer continent with no sea inside it, the crossing, the Last Sea's coast and the floors. `/wayfarer shape <seed> 24576 128 <beyond_lift> <far_shore>` tries other numbers in the same run.
- **Every mechanic has its edge cases.** What a player may do while it runs is part of its design: walk off, go underground, sit in a tent, die, log out, untrack its quest, reload the world mid-step, or a teammate doing the opposite. A design entry, a plan and a build name each of them and what happens, and the finish walkthrough tries them.
- **Singleplayer and server behave the same.** Rules live in the mod jar (code and data) or in `pack/config`, never in a world save or a client-only setting.

## Where things go

- `mod/src/generated/`, `pack/CREDITS.md` (with `generator/credits.json`, every mod's and pack's authors, licence and page, which it fetches from Modrinth, or from the jar of a mod from CurseForge, only for one not yet in it), `pack/config/EndRemastered-NeoForge/Eyes/`, `pack/config/iris.properties`, the shader's options in `pack/shaderpacks/` (from `generator/shader.toml`), `pack/config/nohostilesaroundcampfire.json5` and `pack/config/sparsestructures.json5` are written by `just generate`. Change the generator or its sources (`story/books.toml` for the books, `story/sounds.toml` for the music and the cues, `style.toml` for the stamp, `generator/fonts.toml` for the fonts, `generator/crafting.toml` for the pack's own extra recipes, and `generator/enchantments.toml` for the icon and colour of every enchantment that Enchant Icons does not cover), never the output.
- `CHANGELOG.md` holds each version's changes and names the version: its newest dated section (`## [1.2.3] - 2026-11-19`) is the version of the tree, which the mod's build, `just` and the scripts read from it (`scripts/changelog.py`), and the game counts the changes for the update notice under the loading recap. A push to main whose newest section has no published release releases it: `.github/workflows/release.yml` runs `just lint`, builds with `just pack`, makes it the GitHub release of its version and then runs `.github/workflows/pages.yml` itself, since a release made with the workflow's token starts no other workflow; Pages serves each major's newest release at the hosted address, `https://roman-16.github.io/wayfarer/<major>/`.
- The pack carries no third party's resource or crafting tweaks, so its public repository hands out nothing as another made it; its extra recipes are its own, in `generator/crafting.toml`.
- A story person is seated and unseated by the story alone: `SmallShipsPassengerMixin` keeps Small Ships' right-click from dropping a ship's passenger or pulling one aboard, which would tip Maud and Pip into the sea on the crossing's night.
- `generator/owners.toml` gives every structure kind of every mod an owner, or cuts it; the owners spaced in `balance.toml` `[structures.every_blocks]` share one placement each. `generator/removals.toml` cuts items, creatures, enchantments, loot tables and recipes (also those that name what it cuts or a mod the pack leaves out, so they never fail to load), and names the blocks that take no root and those that keep only in a tent. A mod added to the pack has its structures and anything that breaks a rule of the road entered there, and its keys, chat messages, HUD elements, starting items and random events weighed against the design: every key it adds is entered in `pack/config/defaultoptions/keybindings.txt`, unbound unless the user keeps it because a player really needs it, such as Combat Roll's roll or a key that acts only in the mod's own screen or tooltip, so the file holds every key of the pack; and a greeting, a starting item or an event the design does not have is switched off in the mod's config.
- The title's panoramas in `mod/src/main/resources/assets/minecraft/textures/gui/title/background/<time>/`, one set for each time of day, of which the title shows one at random each time the game starts, are photographs of the world, written by `python3 scripts/client.py panorama`, never drawn by hand; the title's logo is drawn by the generator with the stamp.
- `generator/noise.toml` lists the lines other mods log on every launch or world that are known and harmless, each with its mod and why; a line of the pack's own is fixed at its cause, never listed.
- `story/voices.toml` says who reads which line and describes each voice. `story/voices/` keeps each voice's kept design, the request that made it with its seed and the preview it made, and `recordings.json`, every clip's text and how it was made; the clips are in `mod/src/main/resources/assets/wayfarer/sounds/voices/<voice>/`. `just voice` writes all of these but `story/voices.toml`, never edit them by hand, and `just voice record` also rewrites the game's index of the clips, `mod/src/generated/resources/wayfarer/voices.json`, which `just generate` writes too; the `elevenlabs` CLI from devbox reaches the rest of the account, such as its history or a quick try of a line.
- `playtests/` holds the agent's playthroughs (the `playthrough` skill), one folder per run with its friction log, shots and report, and `playtests/ledger.md` lists every run; like `exaroton/`, git ignores it. The play record every world writes (`design/production.md`, R3) is read with `just play-report <folder>`, and `just script` writes the whole story in play order into `build/script.md` for reading it through.
- `pack/` is managed by packwiz. Add a mod with `packwiz modrinth add <slug>` from `pack/`; use CurseForge only for a mod that is not on Modrinth. Every mod is `client` or `both`: singleplayer runs the server inside the client, so a `server` mod would be missing there.
- A new config for a mod starts from the default the mod writes on its first boot (`build/server/config` after `just test`, `build/client/config` after `just showroom`). Ship only the keys the pack changes when the mod fills in the rest itself, so machine-specific defaults (thread counts and the like) stay per machine.

## Reading the game and the mods

`just sources` writes Minecraft's and NeoForge's code with Parchment names and every mod decompiled with Vineflower, each with its assets and data and the jars it bundles (under `META-INF/jars/<jar>/`, where some mods keep all their code), into `build/sources/` (`neoforge-<version>/` and `mods/<jar>/`); a mod is decompiled again only when its version changes. Search it with `rg`, naming the path: `rg` skips `build/` and every other generated folder through `.gitignore`.

```console
$ just sources
$ rg "enter_when_safe" build/sources/mods
```

`just registries` writes what the running pack contains into `build/reports/`, from every mod: `registries/<namespace>/<path>.json` for every registry, a list of its entries for those made in code (blocks, items, creatures) and every entry's content for those made from data (structures, structure sets, biomes, loot tables and the rest); `tags/<namespace>/<path>.json` with every tag's members; and `recipes.json`. An entry the game cannot write back holds its error instead. It starts the dev server and stops it after, so run it while the dev server is stopped.

```console
$ just registries
$ rg --count '"trek:' build/reports/registries/minecraft/worldgen/structure.json
```

## Seeing the game

Never wait by guessing. No `sleep`, and no `wait` in a driver batch to give something time to finish: a guessed wait is either too short and flaky or too long and slow. Every step returns the moment its effect is done, by waiting on the event itself (the server's reply, a freshly drawn frame, the next compass reading) or by checking about every second until it holds, and a wait that does not finish in time fails and says where it stuck: a driver step that makes no progress for 60 s fails the batch, naming the step and the state of the game. When a step returns before its effect shows, fix the step so it waits for that effect, never the batch; `wait` is only for watching something that has no end, such as an animation. A run that must outlive an interrupted tool call, such as building a late state, starts detached (`setsid nohup python3 scripts/server.py start --state ... > /tmp/state.log 2>&1 < /dev/null & echo $! > /tmp/state.pid`) and is waited on by its exit (`tail --pid="$(cat /tmp/state.pid)" -f /dev/null`), never by a loop that sleeps.

Never wait for a failure that is already known. Code that knows something failed says so the moment it knows, in one line `<what> failed: <why>` (`camp failed: no spot ahead is out of sight, ...`), and both drivers end the batch on that line at once, with it as the error, and exit non-zero; a refusal or a note on the way (`camp: the spot at ... falls short`) is worded otherwise and lets the batch go on. Gates and builds run in the foreground with a generous timeout and return when they finish; the dev client is driven through `scripts/client.py`:

```console
$ python3 scripts/client.py start            # open the showroom, in the running client or a new one
$ python3 scripts/client.py run 'fly on' '/tp @s 48.5 12 60 0 20' settle 'shot row'
$ python3 scripts/client.py stop             # returns once the game has exited
$ python3 scripts/client.py tour [eye|camp ...]   # open the showroom, photograph every hall and the Last Camp
$ python3 scripts/client.py panorama           # photograph the title's panoramas at the Last Camp, one for each time of day
$ python3 scripts/client.py bench [--minimum]  # measure every graphics step's frames, or Low and Medium on the minimum machine
```

Each π session drives games of its own, so sessions never close each other's world: `client.py` and `server.py` give a session a game folder of its own under `build/clients/<n>/` and `build/devservers/<n>/` (claimed in its `session` file and reused by the next session once its game has quit), with its own driver channel, hidden screen, swap port (the base in `mod/gradle.properties` plus 10 per folder) and dev server port (25566 plus 10 per folder), and `start`, `run`, `swap` and `stop` reach only the session's own games; a shell outside π, and the user's own `just showroom`, `just play` and `just devserver`, use `build/client` and `build/devserver`. Every client sees the same saves, kept in `build/client/saves/` and linked into each folder (a world a session makes there moves into the shared folder at its next `start`), and the same checkpoints in `build/client/checkpoints/`; a save another session's client has open refuses to open (`start failed: "<save>" is open in session <id>'s client; ask that session to leave it`), as the dev server's `--world` does, and the showroom, play, state and workshop worlds are each folder's own. Launches go one at a time through `build/launch.lock`, a state being built is waited for, and `start` refuses when memory is short for another game (the heap in `balance.toml` `[heap]` plus 4 GB), naming the games that run and whose they are. One client stays up between batches, so a test pays for a launch only when it must. `start` first swaps changed code into the running client, then opens the world it was asked for in place: the showroom, a fresh world (`--fresh`, or `--seed <n>` to grow it from that seed), a state (`--state`), a save by its folder name (`--world <save>`, such as a playthrough's world; never `play`, `showroom` or `state`, which their own modes make anew) or the title screen (`--title`), from which a world is made or loaded as a player does, in seconds. It launches a new client only when none runs, when the pack's mods or configs, the mod's build or a file the game reads while it starts (the stamp's tokens in `style.json`) changed, when the screen or mode is another (`--visible`, `--far`, `--lean`), or when a change needs a restart, and says why. Changed textures, models, fonts, sounds or lang are reloaded in place, and changed data or story opens the world again: the mod reads its own data files (the story, `balance.toml`, the gear, the plans of Tollbridge and the rest) each time a world starts. A session's client, its dev server and `just test` run beside each other, so none is stopped for another. Between batches a hidden client draws at 5 frames a second, and after 30 minutes without a batch it quits on its own, as the dev server does, unless turns hold its world paused for a player who thinks; `stop` ends either at once. `just showroom`, `just play` and `just devserver` stop the client or dev server running in `build/client` or `build/devserver` first, since they use those folders.

The game runs on a hidden screen from `start` until `stop`: gamescope's headless screen from the system (`programs.gamescope.enable`), 1,920 by 1,080 and drawn by the graphics card like the user's own, so it never takes over the user's display and runs whether anyone is logged in or not; `start --visible` and `tour --visible` put it on the user's display, drawn by the graphics card. A hidden game is silent (master volume 0, which Reactive Music's own player follows too); a visible one plays at half volume. A launch that meets a dialog on the way into its world fails at once and names it, since nobody can click it on the hidden screen; the one-time warning that a world uses experimental settings, which every showroom save shows because of its extra worlds, is confirmed by the driver and never comes again.

`start --lean` on either driver, and `just showroom lean` or `just play lean`, load only the Wayfarer mod and what it needs (Curios and Easy NPC, from the dev classpath) with an empty mods folder, for work that does not need the rest of the pack: the client starts in about 23 s instead of 34, the dev server in about 11 s instead of 16. They play in worlds of their own (`showroom-lean`, `play-lean`, `world-lean`), since opening a world without the mods it was made with damages it.

Every dev client (`just showroom`, `just play` and the driver) runs with test settings that `scripts/instance.py` lays over `pack/config` in `build/client`: no shaders, no Distant Horizons (it and its SeedGen stay out of the mods folder), 8 chunks, in a window. `start --far` on the client driver, and `just showroom far` or `just play far`, keep Distant Horizons on, its far view cut to 1,024 blocks; `start --shipped` and `just play shipped` keep the far view, the shader, 12 chunks and the full screen as the pack ships them, and the client loads Baritone in every mode but lean, so the driver's `go` and its kin walk as a player does; the `far` step waits until it is drawn, which around a new spot takes one and a half to two minutes, hidden or visible. The shipped pack keeps its shaders, its far view and 12 chunks, so those are judged in the published pack, which the user's Prism instance fetches from the hosted address (`production.md`, R2).

gamescope comes from the system, never from devbox, since it loads the graphics driver's own libraries and devbox's packages are older than the system's. The justfile sets `JAVA_HOME` to devbox's Java for every task, and both drivers launch the game and compile through just recipes (`just showroom`, `just play`, `just devserver`, `just compile`), so a shell's own `JAVA_HOME` never matters.

`run` prints one line of JSON per step, as soon as the step is done: the step, and lists of what it produced (a line a step repeats with only its numbers changed shows once, with its count: `the way brushes Powder Snow at ...: round by ... (11 times)`), such as every chat line the game received while it ran (command replies included; the action bar's lines show under `seen` instead) under `chat`, every conversation line and set of answers the game showed under `talk` (`<Agna> ...`, `answers: Who are you? | ...`), what of the screen changed during the step under `seen`, what the player heard under `heard` (each sound as its subtitle reads, with the side it came from, how near and how often: `Zombie groans, behind-left, near (2 times)`), every hit taken under `hurt` (`3 from behind-left: a zombie`, `2: heat`, `1: a hedgehog` for a prick no cactus gave), what the player's body did on its own under `body`, the death screen's line under `death`, with where the player was each second of the 8 before it, on the ground or in the air and doing what, under `before` and the walk's last lines from Baritone under `baritone`, errors under `error`, every story check that failed while it ran under `check` (`check: cecily stands inside minecraft:oak_leaves at 13629, 66, 6181`, the same check on the same subject once per batch with its count at the end), each a finding that never stops the batch, the absolute path of each screenshot, written before `run` returns, under `screenshot`, and the step's own result under its name (`inspect`, `log`, `perf`, `pos`, `state`, `sheet`); Baritone's own lines show only when a walk fails or the player dies, a move it gives up on as taking too long or as unreachable (a jump that fell short) with the move and the blocks at its end, after which a stalled or failed jump across a gap is not tried again on that walk. A batch ends with one more line, `seen` in full: the task panel's title, objective and tasks, the compass's mode, the ember's word while it reads the way (`flaring`, `fading`, with `far` while the goal lies over 2,048 blocks off) and its marks with their names, the caravan's fire among them while its smoke stands (`smoke`), the action bar's line, the banner while one shows (`banner`: its label and title), what the crosshair is on (`an interaction box over Barrel, 2 blocks`), the body as the HUD shows it (health, food, saturation, armour, air, temperature, effects, the hand and the hotbar, the time of day as the server keeps it, whose day turns over at dawn's first light, and the weather) and what is in view, as far as light and walls let a player make it out: the creatures, the boats and carts, and the blocks a player looks for (gravestones, beds, campfires, doors, crafting tables, chests, barrels and lecterns), each with how far, which side, how far above or below and where it stands, and levers and buttons too; whatever glows, up to 96 blocks off and through rock (`The heir, 61 blocks below, glowing through what stands between at 12408, 29, 12484`); and while the ember is held up, whatever glows in its light, as far (`Spruce Fence glowing in the ember's light, 4 blocks ahead at 12410, 82, 12420`). A command step waits until the server has run it, so its replies always sit under it. Every step that clicks, in the world or on a screen (`click`, `interact`, `key`, `pitch`, `press`, `put`, `use`), waits for the server's answer to the click and a frame drawn after it, and says what screen it opened or closed; a right-click in the world first waits for the hand to settle after a swing or a weapon drawn, as Better Combat holds a right-click in a weapon's first half-swing (`use: BookViewScreen "" open`); every step that clicks with an item in hand (`eat`, `fish`, `pitch`, `put`, `use`) first steps out of the space of a person, a creature or an interaction box the player's eyes are in (`stepped out of Agna's space to ...`); every step that picks a creature by its kind (`fight`, `follow`, `inspect creature`, `interact`) passes over story people, Easy NPC bodies and figures that stand without AI, which only their name picks; and the commands the driver sends for itself (`state`, `still`, the workshop's steps) run with the owner's rights through the dev code, in a world with cheats or without, and stay out of the play record. Steps can also come one per line on stdin. A step begins only once no loading screen stands, so one after entering or leaving a tent waits for the world it goes to. A walk that stops for anything but its own end (`stopped: ...`), the player's death, a step's error (a step that does not finish within 60 s too), or the body acting during a step that is not a walk ends the batch: every step after it but `leave`, `shot`, `screen`, `pos` and `log` is `skipped`, so the next move is always decided after what stopped it. `run --shots <folder>` ends the batch with a screenshot numbered after those already in the folder (`t0042.png`), copies it there and makes a copy 1,280 wide for reading, both paths on the last line (`screenshot`, `view`); when the folder is a playthrough's `shots/`, the last line also says what the run has come to (`run`: the game time played in it, kept in the run's `run.json` from the moment its folder is named for, the play record's time before the first such batch included, and never wound back by a rewind, and its deaths and rewinds from the play record). `start --fresh` opens a fresh natural world in survival (`just play`) instead of the showroom (`just showroom`). A batch that does not finish within `--timeout` is aborted and its log shows where it stuck.

In survival the player has a body, as a player of a few hundred hours of vanilla has hands that act before they think (`Body.java`): during a batch it treads water in deep water while nothing else moves the player, swims to the nearest air it can reach through the water when the air runs low (straight up when none is found within 16 blocks), steps out of fire, runs into water within 8 blocks when it burns, backs off from a creeper that hisses or stands within reach (or raises a shield at it) until it is 8 blocks off and silent, for 13 s and 16 blocks at most, knocking it back with a blow when it stands within reach and breaking off when something else strikes the player while the creeper is more than 4 blocks away, fights what attacks the player when it holds a sword, an axe or a trident not worn to its last point (where the pack strips it bare), is not badly hurt and faces at most two (stepping away from lava within 2 blocks before it swings, taking the weapon from the hotbar, raising an off-hand shield between swings), never more than 8 blocks from where the fight began, letting a foe go that leads it further unless it has just struck from within 6 blocks (reach measured to a big creature's edge, as a bison's), and leaving one it has come no closer to for 20 s, such as a pillager on a canopy above, until it comes within reach, and walking back to that spot afterwards when the player was standing, during a hold in singleplayer fighting from where it stands and striking only what comes within reach, walks on past them when it cannot fight while walking somewhere, saying what decided it (`too hurt to fight, health 5 of 20`, `outnumbered, 3 foes at once`, `it keeps its distance, 7 blocks`), runs from them otherwise, to the nearest lit campfire, the tent or where it was going within 32 blocks, or away from them when none is that near, and once it stands at that fire or tent and still cannot fight it leaves the next move to the player, and fights on, hurt or not, what comes within 8 blocks once it stands within 6 blocks of that fire or tent or is walled in on all four sides, as in a hole, and eats from the hotbar when hungry or hurt and nothing hostile is near, leaving raw meat and fish for the fire unless it is starving and looking up at the sky first when the crosshair is on something, so a furnace or a barrel in front takes no click. It reacts a quarter of a second after a threat comes, only to what attacks the player or comes at it in sight (a monster, or any creature the game counts as one, such as the great worm), never to story people (Easy NPC bodies, and the story's figures that stand without AI, such as a memory's men in grey or a road scene's guards), teammates or animals that leave it alone, and never solves what the pack sets the player: heat, cold, the ember, hordes and holds are the player's own decisions. Each act is one line under `body`; a walk goes on after it, and one it cannot fix (health at a third, more foes than it can fight, no way up for air, fire it cannot leave) stops the walk with its reason. With turns on, the driver keeps checkpoints of the world it opened itself, at most every 2,048 ticks at a quiet moment, during a walk, a row or a wait as at a batch's end (alive, at least half health, on the ground or in a boat, dry, nothing hostile within 16 blocks, no screen, no talk for 10 s, not hurt for 5 s), and the step `checkpoint` keeps one at once, the game standing paused while `client.py` copies it; they lie in `build/client/checkpoints/<save>/`, shared by every session, each copying only what changed since the last and leaving out the far view's and Baritone's caches, the last 16 kept with the first of each game day; after 4,096 ticks without one, any quiet moment counts whatever the health, and each checkpoint's line says the health. `rewind [n]` leaves the world, puts it back as the newest checkpoint (or the nth newest) left it, drops the checkpoints after that one and opens the world again; the play record keeps what came after and notes the rewind. On the dev server, `checkpoint` and `rewind [n]` are steps of `server.py run` too, `rewind` restarting the server, so players join it again.

The story runs in every world whose game rule `wayfarerStory` is on, as it is by default; the showroom's save turns it off, so neither the story nor its clock runs there. Its keys are J for the journal (`key.wayfarer.journal`), M for the map (`key.wayfarer.map`) and Z, the key left of X on every layout, to hold up the ember (`key.wayfarer.ember_sense`).

`start --state <quest/step>` on either driver opens the world at a step of the story, such as `ash/the_door`; every step of a main quest is a state. Its first start grows a fresh world from the seed in `story/states.toml` and plays each earlier step's outcome without waiting for it: every talk's first answer, the first words offered for the stranger's stone, the first camp's fire, Tollbridge and the caravan's camp built at once, the clock at the hour the step leaves it at, every reward taken, every control the task panel asked for along the way learned (the journal, the inventory, the map, the compass, an answer's number, a glowing sign's right-click, the Gear page), so a state's panel asks only for what its own step asks. It keeps that world in `build/states/`, then begins the step with the team standing where it begins. Later starts copy the kept world in seconds, until what its world is made from changes: the mod's data, the story, `balance.toml` or the pack, never the textures, models, fonts, sounds or lang. A state not yet kept is built from the nearest earlier step that is, and a build keeps every step it passes, so one build to a late step makes every step before it quick. The mod's code is not part of it, since each step begins with the code that runs; after a change to code that builds an earlier step's world, such as Tollbridge's town or the caravan's camp, `--rebuild` builds the state anew. Building one from a fresh world takes one and a half to two minutes, where on the dev server a pretend player named Wayfarer walks it and leaves, so the dev client takes its place on joining. `/wayfarer story to <quest/step>` and `/wayfarer story next` move a running world on the same way, and the step `story <quest/step>` of either driver returns once it stands there. `/wayfarer story side <quest/step>` sets a quest at one of its steps, its talk begun again and the quest tracked by whoever ran it, and `/wayfarer step skip <quest>` finishes the step it stands at, so a side quest's late steps are tried without playing the ones before; `/wayfarer story gift <gift>` gives the team a shard's gift, to try it without its hall; `/wayfarer story deed <deed>` earns a deed for the player; `/wayfarer story gear <row>` shows a row of the Gear page and `/wayfarer story said <line>` marks a line of `story/gear.toml` said; `/wayfarer story unclaim <reward>` hands a taken reward back; `/wayfarer story choose <quest/step> <answer>` makes a choice stand as answered, such as `the_hollow/pip lost`, to try what follows another answer than a state's first.

```console
$ python3 scripts/client.py start --state ash/the_door
the client is running in state ash/the_door, from the cache, on a hidden screen (opened in 21 s, 3 log lines, 0 errors)
```

Everything the story waits on counts game ticks: a talk's lines, a hold's waves, the clock passing to dusk or dawn. Vanilla's `/tick rate <ticks a second>` runs them faster, so checking that a timed step works takes a fraction of its length (`/tick rate 80` runs a hold's waves about four times as fast), and `/tick rate 20` sets it back. Pace and feel are judged at 20, never fast-forwarded.

The client opens in the showroom dimension `wayfarer:showroom`: the Last Camp at spawn, an item frame wall at z = -10, and the halls in alphabetical order along x at 40-block steps (hall centres at x = 48, 88, ...; z = 72; the surface at y = 1, each hall as deep as its way down needs), each with its name sign on the north side. The compass works there as a demo: it points at a waymark 750 blocks west of the camp, shows a companion circling the camp and marks the camp's grave. `/locate` finds nothing there; the natural overworld next to it (`/execute in minecraft:overworld run ...`) is kept between launches.

The workshop (`wayfarer:workshop`) sits beside the showroom in the same save: empty, flat, a smooth stone floor, noon and clear sky, wiped at every launch. Designs line up along x from the origin, 4 blocks apart, each with its name sign 2 blocks south of it, so the vanilla relative put in with `beside` stands next to the design it is judged against:

`tour` shoots each hall from its surface cover down the shaft into the hall, and the Last Camp from every side, into one labelled contact sheet per subject in `tour/<subject>.jpg` in the session's game folder; the hall cameras come from the generator (`mod/src/generated/dev/wayfarer/tour.json`), so every hall is framed from open air with a clear line to what it looks at. It starts the client itself and quits it when done; a whole tour takes about three minutes.

`panorama` grows the panorama's world from its seed, photographs the Last Camp from its camera at each time of day (`PANORAMA_SEED`, `PANORAMA_CAMERA` and `PANORAMA_TIMES` in `scripts/client.py`) in spectator mode under a clear sky with the fog held even across the six faces, at the Preset's Ultra with the shader and the far view fully drawn (the client's `shipped` mode), on a hidden 2,048 by 2,048 screen drawn by the graphics card (gamescope from the system, so it never takes over the user's display), reduces each face to 256 colours with `pngquant` and writes each set into its own folder in `mod/src/main/resources/assets/minecraft/textures/gui/title/background/`; run it again after a change to the land or the camp, and judge every set on the title before keeping it. The title shows one set at random each time the game starts.

`bench` measures what the frame rate does where the far view works hardest: in a fresh world from the states' seed, on the hidden screen in the `shipped` mode, it first measures 32 s of the world opening, then picks each step of the Preset in the running game with the `graphics` step, puts the player at a height of 256 blocks over where the world spawned, looking down at the land, measures 32 s hovering there while the far view comes in and 32 s flying on over new land at about a horse's pace, each step in a direction of its own, and fails at once when the player did not get far, as when something stood in the way. With `--storm` it measures under a thunderstorm, where rain, wind and leaves fill the air with particles, instead of a clear sky. It prints one row per step and place with the average frame rate, the 1% low and the worst frame of each half, naming at the row's end any other program that used 5% or more of the graphics card meanwhile, and quits the client when done. Alone it runs every step, the shader on for High and Ultra, against the 90 frames a second `design/production.md` R1 gives a high-end PC; `bench --minimum` runs Low and Medium with the shader off on R1's minimum machine (an RTX 2070 Super laptop card, an i7-10750H), alone and on the dev server started with `--far`, against R1's floor: the game is launched believing it has the minimum machine's 12 threads and held to the processor's slower cores (`--cpus` names others), the dev server to the rest, and the graphics card's clock is held at the minimum machine's speed: when nobody is at the PC (no graphical login session, local or over RDP, that is not idle), the bench locks it itself with `sudo nvidia-smi --lock-gpu-clocks=1100,1100` and resets it on the way out, even when stopped; when someone is, the clock is theirs, so the bench fails unless they have locked it (`nvidia-smi` cannot tell a lock from an idle card before the game runs). A whole `bench --minimum` takes about 9 minutes, most of it starting the game and the dev server.

The driver (`mod/src/dev/java/.../Driver.java`) confirms commands with a message of the dev code's own (`Sync`), which the server answers once everything sent before it has run, so it drives a world without cheats as well as one with them. After `just generate`, `start` copies the new resources and reloads or reopens what they change; within a batch, `just compile` (which copies the resources too) and the step `/reload` load new data into the running world, and new chunks and `/place structure` then use it.

Changed Java code goes into a running game without a restart: `python3 scripts/client.py swap` or `python3 scripts/server.py swap` compiles the Wayfarer mod and swaps every changed class that is already loaded into the client or the dev server, over a debug port (`client_swap_port` and `server_swap_port` in `mod/gradle.properties`). The JetBrains runtime from devbox takes changed method bodies and added or removed methods and fields, and HotswapAgent (`hotswap_agent_version` in `mod/gradle.properties`, in the dev runs only) gives a new static field its starting value. A class whose `@SubscribeEvent` methods changed is registered on the game's event bus again. It prints one line of JSON: what was swapped, what loads fresh when first used, what is new, which classes listen again, and what needs a restart. Mixins always need one, and so does a loaded class whose initialiser changes otherwise than by adding fields, such as a changed starting value or a removed field, or whose initialiser registers something (the mod's main class, a key mapping, the driver), since an initialiser does not run again, and a loaded class whose lambdas are numbered anew (a lambda added before the others, or one removed), since code that holds one of its lambdas, such as Baritone's logger, calls it by its number (the swap names each such class under `needs a restart` instead of swapping it), and anything the game reads only at startup; changed command code takes effect after `/reload`, which registers the commands again.

The dev server is driven the same way through `scripts/server.py`: the whole pack but Distant Horizons and its SeedGen, as on the dev client (`start --far` adds them, in a world of its own, `world-far`, so a client that joins draws the far view the server sends), with the Wayfarer mod's dev code, in the session's dev server folder (`build/devserver` on port 25566 outside π), so it can run beside `just test` and the session's client can join it at its port, which `start` prints. It runs headless and keeps its world between launches (`start --fresh` deletes it, and `start --seed <n>` grows a fresh one from that seed, to try a place the story puts down on other land); `start --world <save>` opens another save kept in `build/devserver` by its folder name, linked into the session's folder, such as the copy of the exaroton world that `just carry` packs, and grows it anew with `--fresh` or `--seed`. Each step of `run` is a console command, returns the moment the server has run it, and prints one line of JSON with its replies; `await <text>` waits for a later reply containing the text, such as an asynchronous `/locate`'s, each reply answering one `await`; `state` returns the world's story state as on the client, and `story <quest/step>` plays the story to that step. `start` keeps a running server, after swapping in changed code, unless its world is to change (`--state`, `--fresh`, `--seed`, `--world`), its mods or data changed, or a change needs a restart.

```console
$ python3 scripts/server.py start            # keep the running server or launch one; returns once it is ready
$ python3 scripts/server.py run 'locate structure betterstrongholds:stronghold' 'await The nearest betterstrongholds:stronghold'
{"step": "locate structure betterstrongholds:stronghold", "replies": ["The nearest betterstrongholds:stronghold is at [16480, ~, -1024] (16417 blocks away)"], "seconds": 27.5}
$ python3 scripts/server.py stop             # returns once the server has exited
```

Pretend players stand in for a team on the dev server: `wayfarer pretend join <name>` brings one online through the game's own login, so every mod reacts as to a person, and `wayfarer pretend join <name> <id>` brings that player in by their id, with their own saved data, such as a friend from a world copied off the exaroton server; `wayfarer pretend leave <name>` logs it out, `wayfarer pretend respawn <name>` presses Respawn after a death, and `wayfarer pretend answer <name> <n>` picks the nth answer standing, so a team's vote can be played. They do what vanilla commands tell them (`tp`, `spreadplayers`, `execute as <name> run ...`), every message one receives comes back as a reply (`Mo hears: ...` for chat, `Mo sees the subtitle: ...` and `Mo sees the answers: ...` for a conversation), and a returning name comes back where and as it left, so one that left dead joins dead until `wayfarer pretend respawn` brings it back. They never move on their own, not even to fall, so put them on the ground with `spreadplayers`; the chunks around them load wherever a command puts them, and a change of world completes for them, as a person's would. They join the dev client's own world the same way, beside the real player and its chat.

```console
$ python3 scripts/server.py run 'wayfarer pretend join Mo' 'spreadplayers 0 0 0 4 false Mo' 'execute as Mo run say hello'
```

`scripts/drive.py` is the library both drivers and the boot test share: launching and stopping a game, crash reports, and a server's console, whose commands and replies go through `driver/console` and `driver/server.log` in the server's folder.

## Gates

The gates are `just lint`, then `just test`. Development runs neither: each change is checked in the running game as far as it needs, working and with nothing new in `log`. The `finish` skill, which the user calls before committing, runs the rest over everything uncommitted: the gates and a newcomer's walkthrough of what a player meets. `just test` runs the mod's game tests beside the boot test, which builds the server pack, audits recipes, portals and that the Wayfarer mod loads after every mod whose files it replaces, boots the whole pack headless and checks with `/locate` that the stronghold ring sits at its radius, that no hall grows on its own and that every cut structure is gone, its searches eight at a time through Async Locator. Among the game tests, the face test runs the eye finder over every face in `generator/faces.toml` (the default skins, the pack's people, the user's skin and community skins, fetched once into `~/.cache/wayfarer/faces/`) and prints how often the glow lands on the eyes. It fails when the game tests did not pass, crashed or never ran, and it returns at once, saying so, when nothing the server or the game tests load (the mod jar without its client code and assets, the dev code, the pack's mods and configs, the boot scripts and their sources) changed since its last pass, kept in `build/passed.json`. The boot test plays the states' seed from `story/states.toml`, so a skipped run would have checked the same world. The server pack is put together from links to `~/.cache/wayfarer`, NeoForge's server install kept there once per version. `just pack` is no gate: it exports the pack for the user to play, and runs when the user says they will play or asks for it. A version's section in `CHANGELOG.md` is cut only on the user's word, since pushing it to main publishes it and every friend's instance takes it on its next launch; a published change gets a new version (patch for fixes and tuning, minor for content, major for the land's shape or the story's structure, with Minecraft's and NeoForge's versions, which packwiz's installer cannot move in a running instance). The Release workflow runs `just lint` but not `just test`, whose two game servers of 8 GB each are too big for GitHub's runner, so a release is tested by the `finish` skill's gates before its commit. Every change that touches a world's saved story data adds a numbered migration to `Release.java` instead of reading an old shape in the mod.

What no test can see - shaders, sound, the HUD, the feel of a night, the balance - is judged in game by the user; the `playtest` skill is the checklist. Look through the driver first, so the user only judges what a screenshot cannot show.
