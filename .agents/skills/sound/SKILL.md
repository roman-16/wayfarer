# Sound

`design/sound.md` says what plays where, and `design/narration.md` who reads which line in which voice. The agent cannot hear: it can prove where a sound comes from and that the pack may use it, never how it sounds. So every sound, every piece and every voice is chosen by the user, by ear.

## Before choosing a sound

1. **Know what it tells the player.** Find the moment or the thing in `design/lore.md` and `design/story.md`: who makes the sound, what it means, how often the player hears it. A fact the lore does not have goes to the user first.
2. **Hear what plays around it.** The pack's sounds are its own, new to the player, not Minecraft's: a moment the player has never heard before is a moment they notice. But it sits among what the player already hears, vanilla's and the pack's mods' sounds (`design/sound.md`, A4), so a new sound matches them in loudness and does not clash in character; judge it beside them. Vanilla's events are listed in `minecraft/sounds.json` and its files kept in the game's asset objects, found through the index the dev environment downloads (`~/.gradle/caches/neoformruntime/assets/indexes/17.json`, each entry's hash naming its file under `objects/`); try one in game with `/playsound`.

## What gets sound

- **Music only for the story's moments.** A death, a hold, the morning after, a good night at the fire. Everyday play, the road, the land, the weather, ordinary days and nights, stays with Reactive Music and its own songs; the pack's pieces are rare, so they mean something.
- **A sound for what the story gives.** Getting the ember, its waking, a shard, a reward: anything the player gets that matters is heard as it arrives.
- **Every step's design names its sound.** A step that is a moment says which piece or sound it has, or that it has none, in its design.

## Where it may come from

In this order; nothing else ships.

| Source | Use |
|---|---|
| CC0 and public domain: [Freesound](https://freesound.org), [OpenGameArt](https://opengameart.org), Wikimedia Commons (Musopen's recordings) | First. Found through [Openverse](https://openverse.org), whose API needs no key: `https://api.openverse.org/v1/audio/?q=<words>&license=cc0` |
| CC BY 3.0 or 4.0 (Freesound, [Scott Buckley](https://www.scottbuckley.com.au/library/), incompetech) | With its credit line in `story/sounds.toml`, which the generator writes into the pack's credits |
| [Pixabay](https://pixabay.com) | Its own licence allows use inside the pack, never as a file on its own; record the licence page as it read on the download date |
| Minecraft's own sound events | Only where the thing is vanilla's own, a vanilla block or creature. Referenced by name in `story/sounds.toml`, never copied |

A mod's own sounds and music come with the mod under its licence and are judged by ear like the rest (Reactive Music's songpack, `design/sound.md` A4). What the pack chooses itself never is: a file whose licence is unknown, anything NC or ND, [Free To Use](https://freetouse.com) (its free licence covers only videos, and games need a paid one), music from a game, film or band, or a classical piece whose recording is not free too. A classical piece is free only when the composer died over 70 years ago and the recording is CC0 or public domain.

## Listen before the user does

The agent hears through an audio model: `just hear "<question>" [--tries <n>] [--plain] <label>=<file>...` (or `hear()` and `hear_each()` in `scripts/hear.py`) sends each clip to Qwen3.8 Omni Flash on OpenRouter, signed in through pi's own OpenRouter login, and prints its answers under the clip's label. Use it on every candidate and every cut before the user hears it, and to choose when the user leaves the choice to it.

- **One clip per call.** Beside other clips the model describes the wrong one and scores the same clip 3 in one call and 8 in the next, so `just hear` asks about every clip in its own call, at most six at a time (more at once come back "cannot hear"), each brought to one loudness first, and asks again when a call hears nothing. Never judge candidates side by side.
- **Give it the picture in words.** A judgement is only as good as what it knows. Every call carries the brief in `scripts/hear.py` (the pack, its story, its tone, its music); the question adds to that:
  - **The moment as the player lives it:** what they do and see, what happens just before and just after, the line chat shows, who hears it (one player, the team, anyone near), and how often.
  - **What plays around it, described:** the pieces and sounds of the moments around it, the one that plays now and the everyday music, each named with its instruments and mood from `design/sound.md`, never sent as clips.
  - **Everything this moment has had:** every clip ever proposed, chosen or rejected for it, named with what happened to it and the user's own words ("NO HOPE, rejected: too rushed"), found in `story/sounds.toml`, `design/sound.md` and the session's earlier rounds, so the model judges against what the user already heard and said.
  - **What to answer, in order:** first what it hears in the clip (the instruments, any voices, the tempo, how loud and dense it is and how it changes), then `SCORE: <n>/10` for the moment and one sentence why, then where to start and end it, the fades, and the level against a named relative.
- **Three tries, the median kept.** One answer is noise: `--tries 3` asks about each clip three times and prints the median of its scores, which is the score that counts.
- **Ask neutrally first.** Without the brief and the moment (`--plain`), it is asked only "What do you hear?": told it is a door, it describes a door, and it calls a second of silence "a musical note".
- **Trust the ear for character, the meter for numbers.** Loudness and length come from ffmpeg (`ebur128`, `ffprobe`), and so does whether an end fades: the model calls a faded end abrupt, so measure the last tenth of a second against the peak (`-sseof -0.1`, `volumedetect`; 24 dB under the peak or more is a fade). Where a loop holds steady comes from the short-term loudness, measured every few seconds. The model cannot know a loop repeats, so its "abrupt end" on a loop is the seamless join. It judges what is heard, what jars, what fits and where to cut.
- **What it says becomes data.** Its cuts go into the sound's `cut`, its level into `gain` (dB against the sound's loudness class, a power of two or a sum of two), and `just sounds <id>` makes the file again; then hear it again.
- **Below 8 of 10, search again.** When every candidate's median is under 8 of 10, none of them is put to the user or ships: find new candidates, ask about them, and repeat until one scores 8 or more, naming the rounds before in the question as rejected clips with their scores.
- **It advises; the user decides.** Its findings go with the options put to the user; it chooses only where the user leaves the choice to it, and the report says it did.

## Put it to the user

Before a sound or a piece ships, put it to the user with the `questionnaire` tool, one question per sound:

- **Where** it plays, told as the player lives it: what they are doing, what they see, what happens just before and after, and the step or item it belongs to.
- **What it is for**: what the player should feel or understand, and how often they will hear it.
- **Two to four options**, the recommended one first: for a free sound its page link, licence, length and what its maker says it is; for a Minecraft sound the `/playsound` line that plays it.

Only the chosen option ships. When the user says to pick, take the recommended ones and say so in the report, so the playtest judges them.

## Ship it

- **Sources live in `story/sounds.toml`.** A sound (`[effects.<id>]`) has its credit, licence, page and download date, so the proof stays with the pack, and how it is made and heard: its `kind` (`rare`, `frequent` or `loop`, the loudness classes of `design/sound.md` The mix), a `gain` in dB against that class where the ear asks for it, its `slider`, a `cut` (`start`, `length`, `fade_in`, `fade_out`, in seconds) where only part of the file is wanted, and a `reach` in blocks for a sound placed in the world. A sound that takes the place of another mod's own names its event in `replaces` (`pingwheel:ping`): the generator writes it into that mod's `sounds.json` in the Wayfarer mod, which loads after the mod, and makes it mono, since the mod plays it from a direction. A piece (`[music.<id>]`) has the same proof, and `loop = true` where it loops. Its subtitle is `subtitles.wayfarer.<id>` in the language file, written with the `lore` skill. `just generate` writes `sounds.json`, the mod's sound data and the credits from it; never edit those.
- **Files.** `just sounds [ids]` makes them from `story/sounds.toml`: it fetches each sound's preview from its Freesound page into `/tmp/`, cuts it, makes a placed sound mono (Minecraft plays a stereo file with no direction and no falloff), joins a loop's end into its start so it repeats without a seam, and matches its loudness to its class with ffmpeg's `loudnorm`: a rare sound at vanilla's level-up (-17 LUFS), a frequent one 6 dB under, a loop at -30 LUFS, a piece of music at -18; true peak -1.5 dB. Effects land in `mod/src/main/resources/assets/wayfarer/sounds/effects/<id>.ogg`, music in `sounds/music/<id>.ogg`, OGG Vorbis. A very short, punchy sound may stay under its class where its peak already touches the ceiling; judge it by ear.
- **How it plays.** `Sounds.play` plays a sound to one player, to every player or in the world at a spot, under its slider; `Sounds.loop` keeps a loop going at a place while any player is within its reach. A line's `sound` plays to every player as the line is said, and a line's `music` starts a piece once; a step's `music` loops while the step lasts and fades out when it ends. Story music plays in the Music category, and Reactive Music fades its own out while it plays (`pack/config/ReactiveMusic.json5`).

## Check it

The hidden dev client is silent, so prove a sound by its event, never by ear: the log names every sound played to a player (`sound <id> for <player>`, on the server) and every piece (`story music <id>`, on the client), and a missing file shows as a sound warning in the client log (`log`). The playtest skill's checklist names each sound for the user to judge by ear.

## Voices

`story/voices.toml` holds the cast of `design/narration.md` as data: each voice's `reads` (the speakers of `story/`, `narrator`, `pip_grown`, `book:<author>`, and `journal` and `player` for the two voices a player `chosen` from), its `description` and preview `text`, its `pitch`, and `design` where its settings differ from `[design]`; `[record]` holds the recording's model, settings and sample rate, `[check]` how each take is checked, `[gaze]` the filter that renders the gaze's copy of a line and whose lines get one, `[say]` how a word is to be said (`Malgven = "Mal-gwen"`), and `[lang]` which lines of the language file each speaker reads.

- **See what stands.** `just voice` shows the plan's credits, slots and voice changes, each voice's kept design, whether this account holds it, and what is left to record. The `elevenlabs` CLI from devbox reaches the rest of the account (`elevenlabs voices search`, `elevenlabs user subscription get`, `elevenlabs history`, `elevenlabs --help`); it keeps defaults in `~/.elevenlabs/`, outside the repository, so pass `--voice` and `--model` every time.
- **Write the performance into the line.** A line's text carries audio tags in square brackets where its delivery changes: breath and body (`[sighs]`, `[gasps]`, `[coughs]`, `[gulps]`, `[sniffs]`, `[wheezes]`, `[breathing heavily]`), laughter and tears (`[laughs]`, `[a dry chuckle]`, `[voice breaking]`, `[crying]`), volume and pace (`[whispers]`, `[shouts]`, `[quietly]`, `[slowly]`, `[pause]`, `[long pause]`), emotion and manner (`[bitter]`, `[scared, stammering]`, `[low, threatening]`, `[a faint smile in his voice]`), as many as the moment carries, one per change. Never a sound effect (wind, a door, footsteps): the world makes its own sounds. The game shows the words without the tags, so a tag never carries a fact the player must read, and the same words in one voice always carry the same tags.
- **Every speaker says their own words.** A narration that quotes someone is split into the narrator's line and the person's own; a written text read out (a note, a log, a journal page) becomes a line of its own with `voice` naming its writer, a person or a `book:<author>`, which the band shows as narration; a word the game fills in is written so its few forms can be recorded (`{direction}`, `{stone}`, `{wave}`) or so nothing varying is spoken.
- **Record when the user says so.** Recording waits for the user's word, so credits are spent once, on words that have settled. A change that writes or rewrites a line runs `just voice prune`, which deletes the clips of changed texts and rewrites the game's index; the line stays silent, and the game shows it for its reading time. When the user says to record, every new speaker gets their voice first (below), then `just voice record` records every silent line: it saves each voice it needs from its kept preview, dropping the one used longest ago when every slot is taken, records three lines at a time, has ElevenLabs' speech-to-text write down each take and records it again when it skips words or reads a tag aloud (up to `[check]` `takes`, keeping the closest and naming it), renders the gaze's copies, prunes the clips of changed texts and rewrites the game's index. `just voice record <voice|group|group:line> [--again]` records only those, and anew with `--again`. It ends naming the scenes it changed in `build/voices/scenes/<group>.ogg`, the story's lines in play order: put every scene of the story to the user to hear, and retake what they name by its `<group>:<number>`; fire lines, books and the interface's lines rest on the check. A misspoken word goes into `[say]`, which records every line with that word again.
- **A new speaker.** `just lint` names a speaker no voice reads, and they wait for the recording. Then one who never speaks in the same step as a shared voice's roles and fits it joins that voice's `reads`; any other gets a voice of their own, designed as below.
- **Describe it from the lore.** Write the description in ElevenLabs' prompt format ("Native English. Male, 60-70. Excellent quality. Persona: ... Emotion: ...", then a sentence on timbre, pacing and delivery) from `design/lore.md` and `design/story.md`: age, where they come from, what the road did to them. No effect words (reverb, echo, radio), and no "accent" where intonation is meant. The preview text, 100 to 1,000 characters, is the role's own lines from `build/script.md` in the mood it mostly speaks in.
- **Design, try, hear, ask.** `just voice design <voice> [count]` writes three previews a design into `build/voices/<voice>/`; the lines are recorded by `eleven_v4`, which renders a voice otherwise than the design model, so `just voice try <voice> <preview>...` saves the three in the account and has `eleven_v4` read their preview text into `<preview>.eleven_v4.mp3`. The user chooses a voice by ear alone, with no audio model's score: put all three to them with the `questionnaire` tool, whose question itself carries the folder's full path, who the character is (what they do in the story, their temper, how they speak), the description sent and the preview text, since the user may answer on a page that shows only the questionnaire; each option names its preview and its `eleven_v4` take, and "Design again" comes last. `just voice keep <voice> <preview>` keeps the chosen one in `story/voices/`, and `just voice drop <voice>` deletes the three from the account.
- **Young voices.** ElevenLabs refuses any description of a young voice, a child, an adolescent or a teenager, on the website too: describe a small, high, light adult voice instead, and set its `pitch` in semitones, which `try` and every recording apply, trying a few raises on the chosen take with ffmpeg for the user to choose from.
- **Another account.** With a new key in `.env`, `just voice save <voice>` saves each voice from its kept preview, or designs it again from its seed when the preview is gone. That is a new take of the same request, never the same audio: put `build/voices/<voice>/rebuilt.mp3` beside `story/voices/<voice>.mp3` to the user, and `just voice save <voice> --rebuilt` saves it once they hear the same voice. When it is too far off, `just voice design <voice> --like` designs it from its kept preview and its clips as reference audio, and the user chooses again by ear.
