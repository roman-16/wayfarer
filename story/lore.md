# Lore

The source of truth for every quest, book and item text, and the reason behind every rule of the pack. A mechanic without a reason here does not belong in the pack.

## The world

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What is the dragon? | The guardian of the End, bound there by the builders of the End cities. It can never leave the End |
| Why one eye, and how could it use it? | To guard more than one world, the dragon gave up one of its own eyes. It was set into the portal between worlds, and through it the dragon watched everything the portal touched |
| Endermen and ender pearls | Endermen carry splinters of that gaze, which is why they hate being looked at. Ender pearls are those splinters; mixed with blaze powder they fly home to the portal. That is why the vanilla eye of ender works at all |
| Why does it hunt settlements? | The Eye sees what stays still. The longer a place is lived in, the brighter it shines in its sight, and cities shine brightest |
| What happened? | The Ancient Cities were the first great civilisation and shone so brightly that the dragon reached through. They drew their power from the Nether and built their own gate to strike back (the portal-shaped frame in every Ancient City), and failed: it opened once, and what came through was silence. The sculk is that silence, spreading across the stone, and the Warden is their last guard |
| Who broke the Eye? | The survivors became the first Wayfarers. They tore the Eye out of the portal, broke it into twelve shards and gave each shard to a people at the far ends of the world. Blind, the dragon faded and the portal went dark. They swore the oath: never settle, and never let the shards come together again |
| Who built the strongholds? | The Keepers: Wayfarers who stayed to guard the empty gates, as far from everyone as they could. Their libraries hold the old records |
| Why is there no Nether? | When the Ancient Cities' gate failed, the Keepers sealed the Nether forever, so nobody could repeat it. Obsidian no longer takes a spark |
| Why is the dragon growing stronger now? | People forgot the oath. Villages became towns and towns became capitals, so the world shines again. And the Gazebound, an Illager cult that wants the dragon's sight back to rule through it, are hunting the shards. The greedy king of the Corrupted Eye was their first leader |
| Why can't the Gazebound simply take the shards? | Only an ember can feel where a shrine lies, and an ember cannot be taken: it passes only at a fire shared freely, and it goes dim in anyone who stops walking |
| Why put the Eye back together? | Because the shards are waking and calling to each other, and the stranger said so. The truth is darker (see The player). Once it is whole in your ember, the only safe end is to open the true gate and kill the dragon while it is still half-blind, so nothing is left for anyone to see through |
| Why are the shrines hidden? | The Twelve Peoples hid them so that neither the dragon nor the Gazebound could find them. Only the ember can. They set them far apart, so that no one would ever carry two shards by accident |
| Why are nights so hard? | At night the dragon's gaze presses hardest. Every fourth night it turns fully towards the world (horde nights): nobody sleeps, and the ember burns too bright to fold into a tent. Every fifth night the Eye is reflected in the moon (blood moon), and monsters walk thicker and stronger. Every twentieth night brings both |
| Why do monsters grow stronger? | The longer you carry the ember and the more of the Eye you gather, the clearer the gaze sees you |
| Why can ships only sail with four shards? | On open water a lone ember shines like a beacon, and the gaze finds every ship that carries one. Four shards together dim it enough to cross |
| Why can aircraft only fly with six? | In the air the ember flickers and the phantoms see it. Six shards steady it enough to fly, for a while |
| What is an Ember Veil? | Phantom membrane woven around the ember hides its light from the phantoms that shed it. Gaze tears remember how not to be seen and echo shards carry the deep dark's silence, and each deepens the veil |
| Why is the sky dangerous? | The sky belongs to the dragon, and phantoms are its eyes in the air. In the air the ember flickers and the gaze sees it, and the pull of the shards fades away from the ground |
| Why are gaze tears? | Phantoms weep what they have seen. Brewers call them ghast tears after a creature nobody alive has met |
| Why is a tent safe? | A Wayfarer tent is folded space that is not anywhere, and the gaze cannot see what is not anywhere. Its creatures cannot find the door; only people pass through it |
| Why is a shrine safe? | The Twelve Peoples hallowed their halls. No monster is born inside one, and the gaze does not turn its hordes on those who shelter there |

## The player

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who am I? | No one special: a traveller who shared a fire with a dying stranger |
| Why me? | Before he died he passed you the ember, a sliver of the Eye's own light that every Wayfarer has carried since the Eye was broken. The journal he left you is your quest book |
| Who was the stranger? | A Wayfarer who broke the oath. Tired of the road, he stayed with the Gazebound, who fed him and gave him a home, and his ember went dim and stopped answering him. He became their captain. Dying, he lit a fire by a road where its smoke could be seen and waited for someone kind enough to sit with him, then gave them his ember with a Wayfarer's story. No true Wayfarer would ask anyone to gather the twelve |
| Why does the ember wake only after the first day? | An ember answers only someone who keeps the oath. It wakes once you have lived a Wayfarer's first day: a tent pitched and slept in, a fire lit, everything carried |
| Why do I feel where the next eye is? | The ember pulls towards the shards it came from. Far away it is a feeling: it flares when you walk towards one and gutters when you walk away. Within about 500 blocks the shard answers, and you know exactly where its way down begins |
| Where do the shards go when I take them? | Into the ember. A shard sinks into the light it came from and never leaves it again: it cannot be dropped, lost or given away |
| Why do my friends feel the same? | When you share a fire, the ember splits. Joining a team is sharing your fire, and a shard one of you takes sinks into every ember at that fire |
| Why does a shard show me the past? | Each shard remembers a piece of what happened. The more of the Eye comes together, the more it remembers, always in the same order: the first shard you take tells the beginning, the twelfth the end |
| Why does the journal keep every page? | A Wayfarer copies every page they find into the journal, so the road remembers what the traveller forgets |
| Why do I know where the stronghold is? | Once you hold all twelve, the ember remembers where the Eye was torn out: the true gate. First a feeling, then certainty |
| How is the Eye set? | Touch the gate's frame with all twelve shards in your ember, and they leave it and set themselves |
| Why do I respawn at campfires? | The ember is bound to fire. Wherever you light one, it brings you back |
| What happens to my things when I fall? | The road keeps them under a stone where you fell, each in its place, until you come back for them |
| Why am I always hungry? | The ember burns what you eat. A Wayfarer eats twice what a settled person does |
| Why does varied food make me stronger? | The ember feeds on the world it passes through. A settled person eats the same bread every day; a Wayfarer eats the road, and the more kinds of it you eat, the stronger it keeps you |
| Why do I know in the morning that the night will be hard? | The ember feels the gaze turn before it turns. At dawn of a hard night, its carrier knows |
| Why can't I settle? | The ember marks you. Stay still too long and the gaze finds you too |
| How does it end? | When the dragon dies, the ember goes out for those who killed it and they are free. The sky opens, and the last quest is "Build a home" |

## Plotlines

1. **The Last Wayfarer:** the main story, told through the journal (the quest book).
2. **The Twelve Peoples:** each shrine tells the story of the people who guarded its shard, in the lectern book beside its pedestal (`generator/eyes.toml`).
3. **The Memories:** twelve chapters, one with each shard, in the order they are taken (`story/books.toml`). They tell the breaking, the Keepers, the Grass King, and in the last chapters the stranger's truth.
4. **The Keepers:** books in stronghold libraries, explaining why the true gate lies so far out. The last Keeper remembers the stranger at their door.
5. **The First Gate:** a visit to an Ancient City and the truth about the deep dark.
6. **The Gazebound:** Illager outposts, raids and mansions; their captains carry pages of the cult's plan.

## Quest chapters

1. The Oath: the first tent, the backpack, a campfire camp, the atlas, food; the ember wakes.
2. The Road Provides: gear, tools and gadgets that help on the road.
3. The First Eyes: eyes 1-6 on foot; sailing ships unlock at four.
4. The Sea: sailing ships, then aircraft and veils.
5. The Far Eyes: eyes 7-12.
6. The Last Road: the far stronghold.
7. The Dragon.
8. Build a home.
9. Pages: every book the player has read, to read again.

Each eye also gets a short lore quest.
