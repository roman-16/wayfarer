# Vision

What Wayfarer is, what the player does every minute, hour and act, and who they turn out to be. Every later decision is checked against this document.

## Where the pack stands

The pack ships as a journey to twelve eye shards, and it bores:

- **One verb, twelve times.** The only goal is the next shard, reached the same way each time: follow the ember for about 19 minutes on foot (a 5,000 block leg, tier one) or about 31 (8,000 blocks, tier two), take a stair down, take the shard, read two pages. About five hours of straight-line walking in all.
- **The road asks nothing.** Nothing between two shrines needs anything from the player, so better gear has nothing to answer.
- **The quest book is a checklist.** 52 of its 63 quests are "have item X", and most pay XP levels.
- **The story waits at the end of the walk.** It comes as book pages at the pedestal and one talk a day at the fire.

## Ground truths

What every decision here and in later phases has to respect.

1. **Fix the play, not the text.** Story laid on the same loop stays boring. Story is told by turning it into something the player does.
2. **Sparse world, dense road.** Vast land and rare, wide places are the pack's look, so variety travels with the player: encounters on the road, terrain that is an obstacle, nights and weather, a voice that walks along.
3. **A gate sends you somewhere.** Whatever opens a way comes from another place on the map, never from grinding in one spot, which the ember's stillness rule punishes anyway.
4. **Story and play decide each other.** Every mechanic needs a reason in the lore, and every story beat needs something to do.
5. **Engine once, content as data.** One person with agents cannot hand-build a Witcher. The quest, dialogue, encounter and voice engine is built once; every quest is then an entry in a data file.
6. **One to four players.** A team shares one ember, and singleplayer behaves like a server. Every twist and quest works for a team.
7. **What ships is a toolbox.** The ember, nights, stillness, fire, tents, the compass, the shrines and the generator each get a verdict in phase 3; none is kept for having been built.
8. **Fun is found in play.** Act 1 is built and played before the other acts are written in detail.

## Settled

### D5 The twist: the passenger, and the Eye's own

- You wake at a dying stranger's fire with no name, and he gives you his ember.
- His voice walks with you. It talks on the road, not only at fires: about the land, the fight just won, the place you skipped. You grow to like him.

  ```text
  <Wend> Birches. Everything watches you from a birch wood.
  ```

- The world treats you strangely, and the clues pile up: Endermen do not flinch from your gaze, and the peoples' guardians bow to you.
- **First reveal:** the voice is the dragon, wearing Wend's voice.
- **Second reveal:** you are the thirteenth shard, grown into a person by the gaze to walk its Eye home. That is why the voice reaches you at all. The stranger knew, and gave you the ember anyway.
- Near the end the real Wend's last whisper surfaces, and the ending asks whom you believe and what you are.

**Why:** The passenger turns the long legs into company, with the cheapest machinery there is: lines on triggers. The Eye's own gives the player a past and the shock, and explains why the voice can reach them. Both grow out of the ember and the voice at the fire.

It raises, for phase 2:

- **D5a** The lore lets the voice reach you only while you sit still at a fire. Being the Eye's own explains why it talks on the road too: it is in you, not in the ember. From when, and what is left of the fire's part?
- **D5b** In a team of four, who is the thirteenth shard?
- **D5c** How far apart the two reveals land, and which acts they close.

## Open

### D1 Pillars

Draft:

- The road is the game.
- Nothing stays still.
- Every place has a story with a turn in it.
- You are not who you think you are.

### D2 The loops

Draft:

| Loop | What the player does |
|---|---|
| Minute | Crosses terrain, forages, fights, reads the land |
| Leg (30-60 minutes) | Follows one tracked quest, meets one or two encounters on the road, passes one gate, makes one choice |
| Act | Reaches a new region, a new tier of gear or vehicle, a revelation |

An hour, as the draft imagines it (every name a placeholder):

```text
Tracked    [Main] The Salt Road - find the caravan's tracks east of the dry lake
Road       <Wend> Storm's coming in off the flats. Walk faster, or don't, I'm not your mother.
Encounter  A Gazebound patrol is questioning a herder: step in, hide, or walk on
Gate       The caravan's path climbs a basalt cliff: an iron-headed hook, or a day around
People     The caravan's elder bows to you and won't say why. The shard warms her children: take it, trade for it, or come back later
Fire       A talk at the fire; the herder you saved gives you a side quest
Journal    Quests: Main 1, Side 3 - track one and the compass shows it
```

### D3 A different verb for every main quest

Draft:

| Verb | Example |
|---|---|
| Choose | Taking the shard costs someone something, and the consequence comes later |
| Earn | A living people gives up its shard only for help |
| Follow | The ember |
| Hold | Keep a fire burning through a horde night |
| Infiltrate | A Gazebound camp holds a shard |
| Reach | A summit, the sea floor, the deep dark, each needing its gear |
| Solve | A seal opens to a word from another people's book |
| Track | A moving caravan carries a shard |

### D4 Gates

Draft: five kinds, each opened by something from another region.

| Gate | Opened by |
|---|---|
| Combat | Gear and skill enough to beat what guards it |
| Knowledge | A word, a map or a story learned elsewhere |
| People | Their trust, earned by helping them |
| Tool | A tool of a material found elsewhere, such as stone only a better pick breaks |
| Traversal | Gear for the climb, the dive, the cold or the heat |

### D6 The quest log

Draft:

- The journal lists Main and Side quests, each with its current objective.
- One quest is tracked at a time, and the compass shows only its objective.
- Phase 3 decides whether it runs on FTB Quests or in the Wayfarer mod: FTB Quests' pinned tracker lists quests but knows no positions, and the Wayfarer compass already draws markers.

### D7 What vanilla loses

Draft: anything vanilla that works against the road goes, with its reason in the lore; phase 3 gives every system its verdict. Candidates:

- The enchanting table: power comes from the road, not from a table at home.
- Villager trading halls: a settled economy is what the gaze hunts.
- XP levels as the reward for quests.

### D8 Scope

How many hours the main story runs, and how many main quests, side quests and road encounters it holds.

### D9 The ending

What the player can choose at the end, and what each choice costs.
