# Story

The story act by act: its parts, its people, the shards and every quest, each with its place, its activity and its turn. Everything here is checked against `vision.md`, and `lore.md` is written from it.

## Settled

### S1 The direction: a caravan on Wend's trail

- The story keeps its world, the voice that lies and the four doors, and gains a moving home: the caravan (D14).
- The player walks in Wend's footsteps. The halls' fates are his doing: as a Gazebound captain he stole shards, hid one and gave one away, so every empty hall tells the player something about the man whose voice they trust.
- A rival with a face: the Grass King's heir leads the Gazebound and races the player to the halls, with a point the player half agrees with.
- A clock inside the player: carrying the ember turns them into the Eye bit by bit, and seeing through stone is its first symptom (D15), so the second reveal is something the player feels coming.
- The past is played, not read, the way The Witcher 3 hands the player Ciri whenever someone tells her story.

**Why:** As the pack ships, the story's goal is a collection and only two of its people live in the present. A caravan, a rival and Wend's trail give it people now and a pressure, which is what The Witcher 3 (a person to find), Cyberpunk 2077 (a clock) and Red Dead Redemption 2 (a gang falling apart) hang their stories on.

### S2 The acts: a prologue, three acts and a finale

| Part | Where | The caravan | Opens with | Closes with |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue: The Oath | The Last Camp | Takes the player in | The player wakes at the dying stranger's fire | The ember wakes, and the caravan takes the player as its scout |
| Act 1: The home land | The land round the Last Camp, three of the twelve halls (`systems.md`, M39) | Grows: people join, and their stories begin | The ember's first pull | Malgven names the stranger: Wend, a Gazebound captain |
| Act 2: The far shore | Across the sea round the home land, two halls | Crosses the sea with the player | A night held on the water | The first reveal: the voice is the dragon, and the caravan is broken for having trusted it |
| Act 3: The far ring | Out to the ring of frames, one hall | What survived of it, or nobody | The morning after, walking out of the hollow | The second reveal: the player is the thirteenth shard |
| Finale: The frame | The nearest frame | Whoever is left | The shards the player carries, and the heir's | The four doors |

- The whole road is walked: on foot, with the paraglider and a horse, and across the sea once by boat. Each act is a land farther out, reached only through the one before it.
- The road runs about twelve hours (`vision.md`, D8): the prologue about an hour, Act 1 about four, Act 2 about four, Act 3 about two and a half, the finale about half an hour.
- Who survives the breaking depends on the player's choices, and the survivors come to the frame.

```text
End of Act 2  <Wend> Camp in the hollow, out of the wind. You'll be safe there.
              That night the gaze comes down on the hollow. By morning, nine wagons are gone.
              The voice is quiet. Then, not in Wend's voice: "I needed you alone."
```

**Why:** Three lands, each farther out and reached only through the one before it (the sea for the far shore, the hollow's breaking for the far ring), give each act a country of its own while the road stays a walk through wild land, which is the pack's way to travel. The first reveal lands about two thirds in, where BioShock's friendly voice betrays its player, and the second just before the doors, which give what follows the twist something to do. The breaking makes the lie cost people the player knows, the way Red Dead Redemption 2's gang falling apart carries its story. Twelve hours fits before the road's shape has repeated itself too often: two friends on the server stopped around the ninth hour of a thirty-hour road, still in its first act.

### S3 The order: the place carries the hall, the count carries the act

- Each hall's fate, its people and Wend's deed there belong to its place, whenever the player reaches it.
- The act's rising line, from Malgven's visits and the voice's turns to the clues to the twist, moves with how many of the act's halls the player has done, so it builds whatever the order.
- A beat bound to the count never relies on which hall came before it, but remembers what the player did there.

```text
Act 1, whichever hall comes second:
  At the fire that night a hooded traveller sits down across from you.
The cold hall, whenever the player reaches it:
  The Frost Walkers' shard lies in the ice where Wend left it, twenty winters ago.
```

**Why:** The fates stay tied to their land and peoples, as D10 and Wend's trail need, while the act still rises instead of standing still until its last hall, as it would if everything belonged to its place the way The Witcher 3's three regions do. The memories as the pack ships already follow the count.

### S4 The voice: Wend's words, the dragon's will

- The ember remembers Wend. It is the Eye's light and light is sight, so in the decades he carried it, it kept everything he saw, heard and said, and all of it came to the player with the ember. The dragon speaks to the player through the Eye in them and wears the voice it found there; that is also where it gets his stories, his camps and his leads.
- It wears that voice for two reasons. It needs the player willing, since the ember goes out in anyone who carries it unwillingly (S19), and as itself, the thing that burned Maud's town, it would never get its Eye home; Wend's is the one voice the player trusts. And it has no voice of its own: an eye only ever watched and never spoke, and the only words it has are his. Even unmasked it keeps his voice (`narration.md`); it only stops pretending to be him.
- It speaks from the first hall on. The prologue and the walk to the first hall are quiet, so its first words land as a surprise.
- Until Malgven names him (1.6), the voice speaks in chat as "The stranger", in the gold he spoke in while he was dying, since nobody knows his name; from then on it is Wend.

  ```text
  First hall  The shard sinks into the ember. A warmth, then a voice you last heard dying:
              <The stranger> Well. That's one. Don't look so surprised, I did say I'd come along.
  ```

- On the road it makes short remarks: the land, the fight just won, the place the player skipped. The first time a pull known by feeling runs out over open sea, it says so once for that quest, since a boat is what a player then needs: "It pulls out over the water. You'll want a boat." The long talks, Wend's road, leads and warnings, come when the player sits apart at a fire. The caravan's people hear none of it, and in a tent it is silent, since nothing of the gaze passes the door.
- Unmasked after Act 2, it keeps talking, honest at last, and the journal calls it the gaze. It says what it wants, mourns, bargains and sometimes helps; it tells the player the Keepers are lying, and sometimes it is right. It cannot leave, because it is in them.

  ```text
  After the breaking  <The gaze> I have no voice. I never needed one. I only looked.
                      His is the only one I have. I'm sorry it's his.
  ```

- Near the end, Wend's real last words surface from the ember's memory, under the mask.

  ```text
  Near the end  Under the gaze's voice, faint, older, his: "...don't let it look at them..."
  ```

**Why:** A voice built from the ember's memory needs no new rule: it explains why it is Wend's, how it knows his road, why it is not quite him, and where his true whisper comes from. Small talk on the road and the weighty talks at the fire follow CD Projekt's rule of light lines while walking and important ones in quiet, and a voice that stays after the mask drops, as Johnny Silverhand stays in Cyberpunk 2077, is what keeps "whom you believe" alive through Act 3.

### S5 Closing the Eye in a team: one steps forward

- The team chooses the door together, with two thirds as for every hard decision. For Close the Eye, whoever walks into the frame first is the one who steps in.
- What happens then is everyone's, as everything one player does is (D11): if Pip survived the breaking, every player plays on as Pip; if not, the world is gone for all of them.
- In singleplayer the one who steps in is the player.

```text
The frame stands open. Nobody moves.
Kai steps forward. The light takes him, and the Eye closes.
<The gaze> For the first time... it's dark. Thank you, little light.
```

**Why:** Who gives themselves up is a choice made with the feet, in the moment, between friends, which is the strongest thing co-op can give an ending, and an ending that counts for everyone keeps the world one story.

### S6 The twelve shards: Wend's trail through six halls, and the heir's five

- The Eye broke into twelve shards, one for each of the Twelve Peoples (S20). The road reaches six of their halls, which hold seven shards, since the Tide Folk's stone holds two. The other five Wend lifted for the Gazebound in his years as their finder, and the Grass King's heir carries them to the frame, so every hall the player never reaches is accounted for, and the Gate is where the player meets his half of the Eye (S49).
- Two peoples still live on the road: the Tide Folk and the Sand Singers. Four decisions cover five shards, two in Act 1 and two in Act 2; Act 3's one hall has none, since the doors are its decision. Wend's deeds grow darker act by act without ever being only bad.
- A shard in its hall hallows the land around it, so the gaze cannot see there. The living peoples live on such land, which is why they survive and why taking their shard can kill them; a shard outside a hall shines, as it did in Maud's bell.
- A shard's gift is a power of the Eye that opens what only the Eye passes (D4). Since a shard can be left behind, a gift opens side ways and shortcuts, never the only way forward.

### S7 Why the compass knows the way: someone told the player

- A goal that is not a hall comes with words: a person, a page, a sign or the voice tells the player where. Someone who knows the place names it, and the compass shows its exact mark from the start; someone who only heard of it gives a rumour, "somewhere north-east", and the compass shows a direction and a rough distance until, within about 256 blocks, the place shows itself: its smoke, its banner, its broken tower.
- The caravan follows the marks the player leaves on the road, and in the evening its smoke shows them where it camped.

```text
<Bram> They took the boy to the outpost by the twin lakes, north-east. Two days' walk.
[Side] Bram's Son   Compass ◆ north-east, 1,812 blocks
<A drover> Gazebound? There's a camp somewhere north. Don't ask me where.
[Side] The Drover's Rumour   Compass ◄ north, about 2,000 blocks
```

**Why:** Leads as the pack ships already come with a direction and a distance from whoever told them, so the compass shows what the teller knew: exactly, roughly, or, for a hall, only the ember's feeling. Every quest starts with someone speaking, which is where D12's people come in.

### S8 The doors: what each answers, opens and leaves behind

- Close the Eye means the Eye finally rests. The player steps into the frame, and the Eye, with them in it, closes for good. The dragon does not die: for the first time in its life it sleeps. The gaze ends, with no more hordes, no hearts torn open and no burning towns. The voice says goodbye once, in its own voice, grateful, and the survivors walk on without the player.

  ```text
  Close the Eye  <The gaze> For the first time... it's dark. Thank you, little light.
                 The frame goes still. Your friends are alone at the fire.
  ```

- The four doors answer the dragon's tragedy, an eye that can never look away: Blind it kills it, Give it its sight lets it look forever, Keep the oath leaves it half-blind as it is, and Close the Eye lets it stop looking.
- **What opens the closed doors.** Keep the oath needs both living peoples, the Tide Folk and the Sand Singers, to trust the player, since they are the ones who take the shards back and hide them again: the Tide Folk trust a team that left them at least one of their two shards, and the Sand Singers a team that let the worm live. Close the Eye needs the words that close an Eye, which only the Scribes' books hold and only Alruna can read: it opens if the player reached the Scribes' hall and Alruna survived the breaking, or if she taught them to read the books in her side quest.
- **The more shards are set, the more the dragon sees.** In Blind it, the dragon fights with the sight the frame was given: the player's shards and the heir's five, which lie at the frame after the Gate (S49), so it sees with at least five and at most all twelve; fewer make it blinder and easier to kill, but leave the player fewer gifts. In Give it its sight, the lands where the player left a shard stay hallowed, dark to the gaze and safe. Keep the oath and Close the Eye do not depend on the count.
- **The world after each door**, since the game plays on:

  | Door | The world after |
  |---|---|
  | Blind it | The road's rules end: no hordes, no stillness, no ember's hunger, and the sky is the player's. What D7 cut comes back: planting, breeding, beds, chests. The last quest is A Home, a bed under the player's own roof |
  | Give it its sight | The home the dragon promised: one place where nothing comes at night, and there what D7 cut comes back. Everywhere else the gaze sees all, its creatures hunt every night and towns burn |
  | Keep the oath | The road goes on as it is: the player stays a Wayfarer under its rules and cannot settle, and one day passes the ember on. The last quest is "The Road Goes On", an evening at the caravan's fire |
  | Close the Eye | The gaze is gone, so the rules end and what D7 cut comes back, as with Blind it. If Pip survived the breaking, the player plays on as Pip, grown, carrying their ember, with Pip's Road ahead, a home of Pip's own; in a team, every player does. If Pip did not survive, the world is gone for the player: they never wake again |


- **Years later** every door ends with what became of the caravan's people and of the player, told by a grown Pip, or by the narrator when Pip was lost (S53).

**Why:** The lore's oldest line about the dragon is that its eye was never allowed to close, so the merciful door is the one that closes it, saving the world and the voice the player grew to like at the price of the player alone. Opening the closed doors through trust and through Alruna lets the road decide the last choice, the count of shards makes every shard left behind matter even at the end, and a world that changes with the door makes the choice something the player goes on living in; Pip carries the merciful door only if the player kept Pip alive.

It raises:

- **Phase 2:** each door's scene and what Wend's last whisper says, written with the `lore` skill.
- **Phase 3:** the dragon fight, the ended world and playing on as Pip, settled in `systems.md` (M14).

### S9 The caravan: a burnt town looking for a place the gaze cannot see

- Most of the caravan are the survivors of Tollbridge, a town at a river crossing that grew too big and shone too brightly until the gaze found it, joined on the road by strays: a herder, a Keeper's apprentice and others. One or two come from elsewhere, such as a Gazebound deserter who served under Wend, or one of the last of a people whose shard Wend took.
- They are looking for a place the gaze cannot see, and the voice promises them one. It leads them towards it, and into the gaze at the end of Act 2. At the frame, the door Give it its sight offers the survivors that dream, at the world's cost.
- They cross the sea by ship with the player, in boats from a shipwrights' yard, in the quest that opens Act 2, and the camp is set up again on the far shore.
- Who survives the breaking is decided person by person: everyone in the caravan has a side quest of their own, and doing it, failing it and what the player did for them decide their fate.

```text
<Wend> There's a valley past the sea where nothing comes at night. I've seen it. Bring them.
Old Maud, at the fire: "Ask him again how far."
```

**Why:** A camp of strays gives every member a past of their own, as Red Dead Redemption 2's gang does, and a deserter or a people's last child brings the quarrel and the stake a single town would lack. A safe place promised by the voice makes the breaking the price of its lie and the door its last temptation, and survival decided by each person's own quest, as in Mass Effect 2's final mission, makes the side quests carry the main story.

### S11 The peoples and factions: two peoples left, one cult, Keepers at the gate

- **The Twelve Peoples:** two still live as peoples, the Tide Folk and the Sand Singers; the swamp's witches and the Grass Kingdom lost their shards to Wend and the Gazebound, and the rest are scattered or dead, and only their places remain.
- **The settled world:** the villages, towns and capitals are the people who forgot the oath. They live, and the gaze burns some of them as the story goes, the way it burned the caravan's town before the story begins.

  ```text
  The village where Hob bought iron is black timber now. A dog you knew sits in the road.
  ```

- **The Gazebound** are one cult under the Grass King's heir, who carries the five shards Wend lifted for them to the frame (S6). Malgven's turn and Cadoc's desertion are its cracks from inside.
- **The Keepers,** few and old, guard the frames at the far ring and bar the way, because they know what a thirteenth shard is. Their knowledge and trust are Act 3's gates, and the second reveal comes from them.
- **Who owns which places** (D17):

  | Places | Belong to |
  |---|---|
  | Pillager outposts, woodland mansions, Illager camps and forts | The Gazebound |
  | Villages, towns, capitals and taverns, and the ruins of the towns the gaze burned | The settled world |
  | Temples, monuments, old mines and the peoples' own places | The Twelve Peoples, living or gone |
  | Strongholds and their libraries | The Keepers |
  | Ancient Cities and the deep dark | The first civilisation, dead |
  | Campsites and old camps | Wayfarers and travellers |

**Why:** Living peoples carry D10's living fates and D4's trust gates, while the gone ones keep the mystery and Drifa's grief without crowding a sparse world. Burning towns put the lore where the player sees it, one cult gives the rival a face, and Keepers who bar the way make the second reveal something the player runs into.

### S12 The clock inside: the player shines

- The player turns into the Eye in three stages, one an act, each tied to quests (D15) and each easy to explain away until the next:

  | Act | What happens | How it is explained away |
  |---|---|---|
  | Act 1 | Endermen do not flinch from the player's gaze. They hear the calling stones before the ember feels them. Agna finds their heart slower than anyone's, at the caravan's first fire (S23) | "Wayfarers are strange." The voice laughs it off |
  | Act 2 | From the team's first hall of the act their eyes glow faintly in the dark, which their friends see, Cadoc first (2.3). At the Sand Singers' hall they first see through stone. The peoples' guardians bow to them | Nobody can, and Agna stops laughing |
  | Act 3 | They dream the dragon's dreams: the End, the void, an eye that cannot close. The Keepers know them at the door and bar the way, which is the second reveal | Nothing explains it away any more |

  ```text
  Act 1  Mother Agna, two fingers on your wrist: "Slow. Like something asleep."
  Act 2  Cadoc, at night: "Your eyes. Did you know they do that?"
  Act 3  You wake in the tent. For a moment you were looking down at the world, and it was very small.
  ```

- The cost is that the player shines. The more of the Eye is in them, the more their heart shines like a shard (S18), and the more the gaze's creatures come for them and the harder they search; friends at their side feel it too. This takes the place of monsters simply growing stronger.
- Each shard taken is therefore a gift for the player, more sight for the dragon at the end and a brighter player on the way. Only taking fewer shards slows the clock; Agna's remedy, from her side quest, dims the player's shine for a night.
- In a team the clock moves with the shards the team shares (D11), and every player takes it as their own (D5).

**Why:** Clues in plain sight that only make sense afterwards are what made BioShock's twist land, so each stage hides in an explanation until the next. A cost that grows from the gaze searching what shines gives the rising danger a reason, and gives leaving a shard a reward on the road and not only at the frame.

How brightly the player shines and what it does are settled in `systems.md` (M9).

### S13 The memories: Wend's life, played where it happened

- Wend walked the same places decades before the player and did other things there. At such a place, often an empty hall, the player touches the calling stone or what he left, the ember flares, and they wake in a dreamed copy of the place as it was then, as Wend, with his gear. They play a few minutes of what he did, and come back with what the quest needs: who, where, why.
- The past cannot be changed. Some choices are offered and refused, which shows who he was.
- The real world around the player's friends never changes while they dream.

```text
Now      The hall is empty. Drifa, behind you: "Someone took it. I want a name."
         You touch the calling stone. The ember flares.
Memory   Twenty winters ago. The hall is lit, the shard on its calling stone. Your hands are Wend's,
         and your ember still burns. The Frost Walkers block the stair.
         [Memory] The Theft - lift the shard, fight your way out through the snow
         An old woman grabs your arm: "Please." (Spare her - you can't; he didn't.)
         You bury the shard in the ice of a frozen lake to the east, and mark the place.
Now      You're back. Drifa is watching you. "Well? Who was it?"
         The man whose fire I carry.  /  I didn't see his face.
```

### S18 The gaze: it searches for its Eye

- The gaze sees only what shines: a shard, an ember, and a living heart, which shines brighter the longer it stays in one place. From the End it cannot tell one from another.
- It wants its Eye back more than anything, so it sends its creatures to whatever shines. They look into every heart for an eye, and they tear it open to look; finding none, they move on. A town that stood long and still is the brightest heap of hearts there is, so they tear it apart, and its overturned hearths burn the rest. Maud's town really held a shard, in its bell, and was hit hardest.
- Its creatures can find a shard but never carry one; only an ember can. So the gaze needs a carrier for what it finds, which is why it grew the player (D5), why the voice guides them, and why the Gazebound feed Wayfarers.
- The Gazebound serve it to use it: they believe its sight will run through them once the Eye is set. At their oath, every Gazebound lets the gaze's creatures open their chest just far enough to look; not all of them survive it, and those who do carry the scar over the heart. The gaze never searches a heart twice, so its creatures leave them alone, as monsters leave Illagers alone in Minecraft. They take it for a blessing, but it is only a searcher passing a place already searched, and the gaze does not serve them. Cadoc carries the scar too, and the caravan resents that monsters pass him by.

```text
Mother Agna, over a body on the road: "Look. They never take anything. They only open the chest."
```

**Why:** A gaze that searches explains every hunted thing at once, the towns, the Wayfarers and the still, and keeps the dragon sad rather than evil, while hearts that shine like shards make its creatures' cruelty the same mistake made over and over. That its creatures cannot carry what they find is what makes the player worth growing.

### S19 The ember: the Eye's light

- The ember is the Eye's light. When the Eye was broken, its body became the shards and its light came loose as a spark, which the first Wayfarers caught in their fire; everyone who sat at that fire carried a piece of it away, and every ember since was lit at a fire shared freely from one of theirs. Light lights light without growing less, so a shared fire lights an ember in everyone at it: the team, and every player who joins it later (D11).
- Light is sight, and it lives on what is new: a still life shows it nothing new, so it starves and dims, which is also why varied food keeps it strong. A dying ember's light seeks its body and flies home to the Eye, and the dragon sees a little more. Carrying the ember and walking is the guarding itself, which is what the oath protects, and the dragon grew stronger as Wayfarers settled and their embers went out.
- Light seeks its body, so the ember pulls towards the shards, and a shard can be lifted only by the light it came from. The Gazebound therefore need a living ember: Wend lifted shards for them while his burned, and the heir holds the five he gave them; what he hid lies where he hid it, and the heir wants the player's.
- Wend gave the player the ember so its light would not go home. His ember had been dark for twenty years and he was dying; a dying ember's light flies home to the Eye, and only the Eye's own body could light it again. So he gave it to the one thing the gaze grew to carry its Eye home, and bet that the player would choose otherwise.
- The ember finds halls, not shards. On every calling stone the first Wayfarers set a calling stone, kin to their waystones, which only an ember hears, so Wayfarers could find the halls and look after them. Far off the ember only feels whether the player walks towards the stone or away, flaring and guttering; within about 256 blocks it knows the stone's place; and the stone stays when the shard is gone, so in an empty hall it shows what happened, because it saw.
- The player is the thirteenth splinter. When the Eye was broken, a splinter fell unnoticed; it grew over the centuries, as a shard grew the Sand Singers' worm, and the gaze shaped it into a person, because only a heart that keeps walking can carry the ember home. That is why the ember woke in them: the light found its body. With every shard they take, more of the Eye comes together in them, which is the clock inside (S12) and what Close the Eye spends.

**Why:** One thing, the Eye's light, explains the pull, the carrying, the oath, the stillness, the splitting at a fire and why the gaze needs the player, so the rules of the road are the story's rules too. A calling stone lets an empty hall still be found and still tell its tale.

### S20 The breaking: why the Eye was broken

- The first Wayfarers were the survivors of the Ancient Cities, the first great civilisation.
- The Eye sat in the portal between worlds, and through it the dragon watched everything that stood still and sent its creatures there; that is how the Cities fell. The survivors tore the Eye out of the portal, and the dragon went blind.
- The Eye cannot be destroyed: fire, sea and hammer only broke it. Whole, it could be set again in any frame, so they broke it into twelve shards and sent each to one of twelve peoples at the far ends of the world. The Twelve Peoples hid the shards in halls, and the Wayfarers walked, keeping the ember.

The player learns it from Alruna in the Keepers' burnt reading-house (1.5), from the Keepers' book she carries (S26).

**Why:** The breaking is the oldest thing the player learns and the root of every rule, so it has to answer why the Eye was not simply destroyed: it could not be.

### S21 The dragon and the silence

1. Beyond all the worlds lies the silence, a nothing that wants everything to stop: sound, movement, life. Where it touches, things go quiet and die, and the sculk is the silence growing.
2. The End is the last shore before it; the void under the End's islands is the silence.
3. The builders of the End cities, an old people long gone, made the dragon to guard that shore and bound it there, so it could never leave its post.
4. The silence also seeps into any world wherever life stands still too long, because a place that stays still grows thin. So the dragon gave up one of its eyes, which its makers set into a portal that touched every world, and through it watched them all.
5. Where a place stood still too long, it sent its creatures to break it up and keep life moving: cruel, but it kept the silence out.
6. The Ancient Cities saw only a monster sending its creatures at them, and decided to kill it. The only way to the End was the dragon's own portal, which it watched, so they built a gate of their own, powered by the Nether's fire, to break through.
7. Their gate opened past the End, straight onto the silence. It poured into the Cities and they fell silent: the deep dark, whose blind Warden, hunting by sound, is the Cities' last guard.
8. The survivors tore the Eye out of the portal and broke it (S20). The dragon went blind, the silence slowly spreads with nothing watching, and the dragon wants its Eye back to watch again, or so it says.

This pack is about the dragon and the gaze, and the silence stays in the background. It is hinted at along the way: in the deep dark, its sculk and its Warden, in an old book, in a line from the dragon, which may let slip a little of its side, and in one line at the very end, after whichever door, that says something else is still coming. A little explanation is fine; the whole story is not told. No door ends the silence, which leaves it for a part two.


**Why:** A guard with a cruel way of guarding is what makes the dragon sad rather than evil, and knowing it keeps every later decision consistent, but told in full in this pack it would pull the story away from the dragon and the gaze. Hints along the way and one line at the end leave the door open for a part two.

It raises, for phase 2: where the hints fall, and how much the dragon lets slip.

### S23 The prologue in detail

The prologue step by step, as the slice builds it (`slice.md`, step 2). Every line is a placeholder for the `lore` skill, and the voice stays silent throughout (S4).

**P1 The Last Fire** (the Last Camp; Talk, then Build):

1. The world's first players wake together at dawn on the ground by the Last Camp's lit fire, remembering nothing before it. The stranger sits across the flames, propped against a log, grey and short of breath, with a scar over his heart: the Gazebound's mark (S18), which Cadoc explains in 1.3.
2. He speaks first, in chat, with answers to click (M3). He says plainly that he is dying and why: he is old, and finding the player and bringing them to his fire took the last of him. He knows the player's face and will not say why. He presses a cold coal into their hand and names it, "That's an ember", and only then does the compass name it. He gives three rules, each with its reason in his words, walk, never stay, sleep only in a tent by a fire, and dies with his charge cut off (S22), slumping where he sits.
3. After his death the story holds a silence of about sixteen seconds, the fire and the music only, then the narrator says that he is dead and gives the reason to bury him; the task to bury him shows once that is said. The player buries him: a right-click on him, the screen dims, and a grave stands by the camp with a blank stone. Whoever buried him chooses its words from numbered answers, a few ready words, their own (only then the stone's writing screen opens, with a task saying what to do there) or none, and the journal keeps them.
4. His pack is the first reward, waiting in the journal for each player to claim: a backpack, a tent, a bed of any colour and a little food. Nothing else comes with a new world. Silent Gear's blueprints come later, as Hob's patterns at the caravan's first fire (M18).

The Last Camp is his fire, burning, his tent and his things, with no grave until the player buries him and no letter, since he says it himself. It stays as it is for good: the last memory is played there (S13).

```text
You wake on cold ground beside a fire. You don't remember anything before this fire.
An old man sits across the flames, propped against a log. His face is grey, and his breath rattles.
<The stranger> I'm dying, that's all. I'm old, and I walked further than I had left in me.
<The stranger> That's an ember. Mine. It's been cold for years.
<The stranger> Never stay long in one place. Stay, and something finds you.
<The stranger> Don't let it... the shards. I took them. Don't let it have...
The fire pops. The old man is dead.
What do you write on his stone?  [Here rests a good man, who gave all he had.] [Rest well, old friend.] [Gone, but never forgotten.] [Leave it bare.]
Journal: I buried him by his fire. On his stone I wrote: "Rest well, old friend."
```

**P2 The First Day** (wherever the player makes camp; Build):

1. "Not here": the player walks at least 512 blocks from the Last Camp, any way they like, and for a reason of their own, which the narrator gives in their thoughts: he knew their face and they don't; all he left is a cold coal and three rules, and the coal wakes on new ground, so maybe it remembers what they can't. The objective names what the walk is for, new ground for the ember, and the journal keeps it as "I" ("He knew who I was, and the ember is all I have of that, so I took it somewhere new"). When they are far enough the land is new to them, and the coal is a little less cold.
2. They make their first camp: the tent pitched, a campfire crafted from logs, sticks and coal or charcoal and lit within 4 blocks of it, and a rest by the fire, which counts the moment a player stands near it. In a team, two thirds of the team have done so.
3. Once the camp is made, the afternoon passes at the fire in half a minute (M20) and dusk falls. Its lines wait until a player sits by their fire, so one who went into the tent is asked to come out to it, and "You sit by your fire" is always true.
4. The ember wakes: it has seen new land (S19). It leans in the player's palm towards the smoke, which ties it to the compass that has stood at the top of the screen from the start, and the ember sense wakes with it.
5. Smoke and a red glow rise over the land ahead, and P3 opens. The player sees the town, so the compass shows its exact mark.

The sun eases as on every day out (M6), and the road's rules teach themselves when tried, through the first-time hints: a sapling that withers, a chest that will not stand.

```text
He knew your face. You don't. All he left you is a cold coal and three rules.
It wakes on new ground, he said. Maybe it remembers what you can't.
[Main] The First Day - find new ground for the ember: 512 blocks to go.
You sit by your fire. The shadows stretch. The ember in your hand grows warm, then glows.
Over the ridge ahead, smoke. Under it, the sky is red.
[Main] Ash - the smoke over the ridge   Compass ◆ 320 blocks
```

**P3 Ash** (the burnt town; Investigate, then Hold; the gate is Strength):

1. The quest puts the town down at dusk 256 to 384 blocks past the player's camp (`balance.toml` `[tollbridge]`), on the way they walked, out of sight behind the land and close enough that its smoke shows over it, already burning, with fires that do not spread. It is a town that grew too big: about a hundred blocks across and about thirty buildings round the bell tower's square, from the inn, the smithy and the market to houses of every size. It grows on its land the way a village does: streets leave the square and bend with the ground, every building stands on its own footing beside its street with its door towards it, and a path joins each door to the street. Its site keeps 64 blocks clear of any other place and prefers land beside a river, and where it finds one the town stands on both banks with the toll bridge across; elsewhere it stands without. About thirty-two dead lie in it, each with a face of their own: most indoors, where they hid, some at the doors they tried to get out of, fewer in the streets where they fled. It stays afterwards as a ruin of the settled world: Maud's town.
2. The player investigates by night with the ember sense (M10): bodies with their shirts torn open over the heart, purses still on their belts, emeralds in an open strongbox, each a line in the journal. The task panel says to hold up the ember and then to right-click a glowing sign to look closer. The cellar door is always the last sign: it glows, whole, only once three of the town's four are found, and finding it ends the search. The town's barrels hold what it left, since its killers took nothing: food and seeds in the houses, the market and the inn, coal, flint and a little iron at the smithy, wool at the weaver's, the emeralds in the strongbox and a few at the toll house, each player finding their own share (Lootr), with hay stacked in the barns (`balance.toml` `[tollbridge.loot]`).
3. The survivors, Maud, Pip, Agna and Hob, hide in the cellar of the stone bell tower, and each gives their name there, since the player knows none of them; their talk begins once the player comes down to their fire, not at the door. Resting at their fire makes it where the player wakes if they fall that night, and Hob hands them a blade from his forge. The rest takes half a minute at the fire, counted in the task panel, and the blade waits in the journal once it is done.
4. The gaze's creatures come back, drawn by the ember that has just woken: the player shines (S12) before anyone knows what that means.
5. The hold: four waves at the cellar door, with the survivors as their target. Hob shuts the door as it begins, and the first wave comes half a minute after "Outside, something scrapes at the stones". The waves are small, 2, 3, 4 and 5 creatures for each player of the team (`balance.toml` `[hold]`), and they are the whole fight: while the hold lasts nothing else spawns within its reach, and what already stood in the town when it began joins the first wave. Each creature of a hold is outlined in dark red through walls for the whole team until it falls, and a wave ends only once its creatures are dead, or after its time; while no one of the team stands alive at the town, the hold waits for them. A grave stands on open ground beside where a player fell, and right-clicking it gives their things back. Each wave makes for the bell tower's door and beats on it; left to them, the door cracks and gives way, and they come down the stairs into the cellar. If one falls, the step is tried again from the cellar, with the failed try's waves gone (D6). The night eases towards midnight while the hold lasts; after the last wave it passes to dawn in half a minute, and what is left crumbles in the sun.
6. In the morning the survivors come up out of the cellar and beg the one with the ember to lead them; the talk begins once the player stands with them by the bell. Maud sits by the fallen bell and asks what to do with it; she takes it whatever the player says, and remembers what they said, which comes back in The Bell (1.5).
7. Make for the caravan: the survivors' first camp goes up out of sight ahead, at its smallest, the fire, Maud's tent and the bell's cart, the healers' tent and Hob's wagon (M5). The player walks in by daylight, the afternoon passes at the fire, everyone has a word, Agna binds a cut on the player's arm and finds their heart slow, and Maud makes them the caravan's scout. They sleep in their tent by its fire, and the next morning Act 1 opens with the ember's first pull (1.1).

### S25 The road's curve: one peak an evening, the act's end biggest

The road is about 6 evenings of two hours, about 12 hours of main road, with the side quests on top as longer or extra evenings (D8). It has the same shape at every scale:

- **An evening** opens at the caravan, rises to one peak, a fight, a revelation or a hard decision, and closes at the fire. The next evening's peak is of another kind: after a fight comes a revelation or a decision.
- **An act** rises to its last evening, which holds both its biggest fight and its revelation. The evening after an act ends opens the quietest: new land, and time to walk it.
- **Side quests fill the valleys:** beside a main quest that fights, the evening offers one that talks or searches, and the other way round.
- **A red moon comes on the climaxes:** the Bell, the Hollow and the frame in the finale, so a red sky always means the worst night of its part of the road. Act 3's climax is the Keepers naming the player, which needs no moon. Every other hard night is a hold its quest calls or the caravan's own (M6), and an evening has at most one.
- **A story piece plays** at every hold (the battle piece), at every death the player watched, at each act's revelation at the fire, as each memory's own short theme, and on each act's first morning; outside a hold, an evening has at most two (`sound.md`), and the last evening, which ends the road, has the finale's own.

One peak an evening, of a kind the last evening did not have, keeps a group's weekly session from feeling like the one before, and a red moon kept for the worst nights stays an omen instead of weather.

### S28 1.2 The Deserter in detail

**1.2 The Deserter** (a ford below a Gazebound outpost; Hold; Strength):

1. About 1,024 blocks out, the quest puts the ford down out of sight on flat land where no snow lies, since the cold is 1.3's gate: it cuts a stream across the way, a knee-deep creek about 8 blocks wide with gravel banks and a ford of gravel, running far enough either side to read as one the land always had, and on the high ground across it a Gazebound outpost. The creek is the ford by design: the rivers a player sees are Streams Reflowing's streams, which the quest cannot read before the land is made, and the river biome is rare in this world, so on six worlds measured no river was ever found.

   **Out of the cold.** Before it chooses, the quest checks the way: the land from the edge of the caravan's camp, 256 blocks out, to the ford, sampled every 64 blocks, and the ford's own spot; the camp's own ground is where the team already stands.    ```text

- Escort: the escorted person follows whoever is nearest; dying or logging out leaves them where they stand until someone comes back; a boat carries them along (they are put beside the player); their deadline counts dawns, which pass for everyone.
- A team splits: any player can finish any step; talks start with whoever comes near.
- C1's village burned (W1) or gone: Wil stands at the nearest village standing.
- C2's outpost: the ford's hold rules (waves grow with the team; dying keeps the wave count).
- C3 with no snow on this shore within reach: the grounds are put on the coldest ground found, with the snow brought (as 1.3).

**Where it lives.** People: Wil, Brannoc, Cecily; Pages: the Keeper's note, the Tide Folk's remedy; Words: the old script.

**Sound.** None of their own.

**Looks.** Wil, Brannoc and Cecily as skins with the `art` skill; the Keepers' ruin and the cairn ring as structures.

**Why:** Mass Effect 2's loyalty missions are each a companion's own story, and the final mission reads them back; here each one is short, uses a verb the act already teaches (track, raid, escort, solve, carry), and its ending is one bit the Hollow reads.

### S48 Act 3 in detail

As `acts.md` builds Act 3 (steps 17 to 21). Every line is a placeholder for the `lore` skill. Act 3's one hall, the Scribes', is put down ahead by its quest, as every hall is (M40). From 2.6 on the voice is labelled "The gaze" (S4).

**3.1 The Morning After** (opens at the hollow at dawn).

1. **The wounded.** Three of the caravan lie hurt in the ash; under the held-up ember each shows where they lie. Right-clicking one carries them to the wagons ("Carry the wounded: 1/3").
2. **The dead.** Three graves are dug where the tents stood, one for each lost household; right-clicking each mound closes it ("Close the graves: 1/3").
3. **The gaze speaks.** At the cold fire, those left load what is left and follow the player; nobody says where. The gaze, openly for the first time: "I have no voice. I never needed one. I only looked. His is the only one I have. I'm sorry it's his."
4. **Out of the hollow.** The ember pulls on, out into the far ring, by feeling. Act 3's first morning is this walk out: new land, quiet, and what is left of the caravan behind.

**3.2 The Dreaming** (a fire's story, the first evening on the road from the hollow).

At the fire the player nods off and wakes with a start: in the dream they looked down at the world from very high, and it was very small, and everything in it stood still. Agna, if she lives: "I've nothing for that." If she died at the hollow, nobody says anything. The gaze: "That was me. That's what I see. All of it."

**3.3 The Scribes' Books** (the Scribes' hall, mountains; it opens the morning after 3.2).

1. The pull, by feeling, up to the Scribes' hall near a peak, which the quest puts down ahead on the nearest mountain of the far ring (M40).
2. Their books on the shelves; under the ember one glows. Touching the stone wakes a vision, not a memory of Wend's own but a sight of the hall's past as its stone saw it: Wend, young, reading at their lectern by candlelight, and turning a page, and stopping.
3. The book he stopped at speaks of a thirteenth shard, "the one that walks". If Alruna lives, she reads it and finds the words that close an Eye; if she died but taught the player the old script (C5), the player reads them. Either way `close_words` is learned, which opens Close the Eye.
4. The shard: take it (The Flare: a burst of the ember's light that crumbles the gaze's creatures) or leave it.

**3.4 The Rider** (the evening after the Scribes' hall).

1. At the caravan's fire a rider in grey comes out of the dark at a gallop, snatches Pip (if Pip died at the hollow, Wil, Cecily or another survivor) and rides off.
2. **The chase.** The rider flees on horseback; the compass marks him. He can be caught on foot or on a horse: getting within 4 blocks of him, or bringing his horse down, ends it. If the team falls far behind he slows, so he is always caught in the end.
3. Caught, he drops the child and says: "He doesn't want the child. He wants you at the gate." He rides off; the child walks back with the player (an escort).

**3.5 The Keepers' Door** (it opens once the child is back with the caravan).

1. The compass marks the nearest stronghold of the far ring; until the player is inside a hall, the compass marks its way in, never its stone or its frame deep under it. In its hall of the frame wait the Keepers, old Wayfarers who would not walk; their watch stands at the stair. The Keepers keep their hall: once they wait there, no monster lives or spawns within 32 blocks of the frame and its spawners are gone (`[gate]` `quiet_blocks`), and the oldest stands on free floor in sight of it.
2. **Sneak**: the Keepers' watchers (people with lanterns) see a player within 8 blocks who is not crouching; seen, the watch calls, and the Keepers come. Either way they catch the player at the frame (sneaking only decides whether they come quietly).
3. The oldest Keeper looks at the ember, and then at the player's eyes: "The thirteenth." The second reveal: the Eye's last piece is in the player, and was put there when Wend gave them the ember; that is why a frame takes what is brought. The gaze, quietly: "Yes."

**Edge cases.**
- A companion lost at the hollow: every line of theirs in Act 3 has a `when` for the living, and an unsaid line is skipped; a person the quest needs to speak (Agna in 3.2) is replaced by narration.
- The chase: the rider never leaves the overworld and never crosses deep water.
- The Keepers' Door: the sneak cannot fail the quest; being seen only changes the line the Keepers greet the player with.

**Why:** Act 3 is the shortest act and the straightest: the wounded carried, a dream, one climb to the books that name a thirteenth, one chase that says who waits at the gate, and the Keepers who barred their door to Wend and now name the player, so the second reveal comes while the first still stings.

### S49 The finale in detail

**F1 The Gate** (opens with 3.5, at the stronghold's frame).

1. The heir's band comes down the stronghold's stair, the heir among them with the five shards he holds (S6): a hold of the act's size at the frame (named fights scale with the team, M37).
2. Malgven is with them. If the team ate or kept her bread and sent her away at neither visit (1.4, 2.5), she breaks Wend's seal on the letter she carried for thirty winters, reads it, and turns on her band: "You ate my bread, that first night. He did too, once." (or, kept: "You kept my bread. He'd have eaten it."), and the hold ends before its first wave ("Her staff cracks across the stair, and the band breaks back the way it came."). Otherwise she raises her staff with the rest, and the hold runs its waves, which come up the stronghold's passages to the hall of the frame.
3. Broken, the band falls back up the stair, and the heir with it, without his shards: his five lie on the floor before the frame, dropped where his men broke.
4. A memory, at the frame: the night of the prologue, from Wend's side. The player plays him dying at the Last Camp, finding a face by the road, and giving it the ember; his real last whisper surfaces under the mask: "...don't let it look at them..." (S4).

**F2 The Doors** (at the frame, with the shards the team carries and the heir's five).

The team chooses (two thirds, M3) among the doors that are open (S8): Blind it (always), Give it its sight (always), Keep the oath (both living peoples, the Tide Folk and the Sand Singers, trust the player), Close the Eye (`close_words`). The choice is said at the frame as a talk with the gaze.

- **Blind it**: setting the shards, the team's and the heir's five, opens the portal (the gate as it is built); the dragon fights with the sight the shards gave it (M14).
- **Give it its sight**: the shards go into the frame and the Eye opens on the world; the gaze promises a home and keeps it (S8's world after).
- **Keep the oath**: the shards, the heir's five with them, go back to the Tide Folk and the Sand Singers, who hide them again; the road goes on.
- **Close the Eye**: whoever steps into the frame first is the one (S5); the Eye closes.

**F3 After.** Each door's world (S8) and its last quest: A Home after Blind it, Pip's Road after Close the Eye with Pip alive, The Promised Home after Give it its sight, The Road Goes On after Keep the oath. Once a last quest's closing lines have been said, Years later (S53) and the credits turn on the ending's dark screen (M14).

**The Promised Home's home.** Home is where the team's people last made camp: the caravan's last camp while anyone of it lives, and when the Hollow took everyone, the last fire the team made itself, its tent beside it, which on the road to the frame lies near the frame. The quest then reads "Go home, to the last fire you made.", and the walled camp and its ring of stones (S53) stand there. **Why:** after the Hollow the team's own fire is the only home it has, and sending it back to a camp nobody is left in, about 9,700 blocks behind the frame on the states' seed, put an hour of empty walking before the ending, past the road's twelve hours (D8). **Edge cases:** whoever of the team rested by the fire counts, and the caravan's own fire never does; home is fixed the moment the door is chosen, so a fire made after it moves nothing; a team that never made camp by a fire of its own goes home to the caravan's last camp.

**Edge cases.** A team that never learned `close_words` sees Close the Eye closed, with the line that says what would open it; the same for Keep the oath, which names the people who would not hide the shards. Dying in the hold sends the player back to the stronghold's entrance; the hold keeps its wave, and the heir's five stay with his band until it breaks. A team that already set the shards into the frame by hand before F2 has chosen Blind it.

**Why:** The last act of the story is the oldest one of the game: the frame, the Eye, the dragon. The doors are the doors S8 settled, opened by what the road did; the heir's five at the frame put the whole Eye within reach at the moment the player learns what it would cost, and F1 gives the man whose voice the player carried the last word that was really his.

### S53 Years later: what became of them, told by a grown Pip

Every door ends the same way: once its last quest's closing lines have been said, the screen goes dark and the epilogue turns, leaf by leaf, years after the door. Pip, grown, tells children at a fire what became of the caravan's people and of the player, the way Tollbridge once told its children Wayfarer stories. When the hollow took Pip, the narrator tells it and says who is missing, Pip first. Then the credits turn. Every line is written with the `lore` skill.

```text
You wake in a bed under your own roof. Nobody is watching. Nothing is coming.
Years later. A house by a river, and children round its fire who have never seen anything come out of the dark.
Pip      "My mam said Wayfarers weren't real. Then one came down into our cellar, with fire in their hand."
Pip      "Hob made ploughs after. He said a plough was harder to get right than a sword."
Pip      "The hollow took Maud and Cadoc. I still set their places at the fire."
Pip      "Our Wayfarer built a house, and stayed. In the old stories, none of them ever stayed."
A girl at the fire asks if the Wayfarers in the stories were real.
Pip      "I was there."
Wayfarer › Made by Roman. › … › Thank you for walking the road.        [ Walk on ]
```

- **What it tells, door by door.** Each door opens on its own scene: Blind it, a house by a river; Give it its sight, the walled last camp and its ring of stones; Keep the oath, a fire at a river crossing, where Pip holds the ember if the player promised it ("If it comes to that, it's yours.") and someone else does if not; Close the Eye with Pip alive, the house the player built as Pip; with Pip lost, a town that never burned, where someone tells the children about the Wayfarer who walked into the frame.
- **The caravan's people.** One leaf for each person who lived through the hollow, saying what became of what they wanted, as far as the door allows (`lore.md`, The ending): Maud, Hob, Agna, Cadoc, Drifa, Alruna, and Bram and Davy when C4 brought them. Those the hollow took share one leaf, Pip named first and the rest by name; a person who left before the hollow or never joined has none.
- **Pip's own answers come back.** Under Keep the oath and Close the Eye a child asks Pip "Are you a Wayfarer?", and Pip answers with what the player told Pip in the cellar (P3): "I suppose I am." or "I don't know what I am."
- **Alone and in a team.** A line that names the player says "our Wayfarer" when one player walked the world and "our Wayfarers" when more did, counted from everyone who has ever joined it; the narrator says "you", which fits both.
- **On the dark screen.** A spoken leaf shows its speaker's name in gold above the line in quotes; a narrated one shows the line alone. Each stays as long as it takes to read (`style.toml` `[motion]` `read`, at least `linger`), and a click or a key turns it sooner. The credits follow on the same screen and end on its button: Walk on, or Start a new road in the world that is gone.
- **Where it lives.** The Afterwards ribbon of the journal keeps The ending, which plays the epilogue and the credits again. In the world that is gone it turns after the pages for every people met and before the sculk's line and the credits, every time the world is opened.

**Edge cases.**
- **Offline when it plays, or left halfway:** it opens by itself only for those online as the last quest's lines end, and only once; The ending on the Afterwards ribbon, whose gold dot a player who joins later finds, plays it again.
- **Death:** a player dead when it opens reads it, and finds the death screen again when it closes.
- **A screen in the way:** it waits behind the band's own rules for the closing lines, then opens over whatever screen stands; Escape does nothing, and the last leaf waits for its button.
- **Pip alive but Pip's Ember never asked:** it counts as not promised. Under Close the Eye after "It goes in the ground with me.", Pip says so, and carries the ember anyway.
- **The server stopped during the closing lines:** it does not open again by itself; The ending plays it.

**Sound.** The Long Way Home (`sound.md`) begins with the first leaf of Years later, once, and fades over three seconds when the button is pressed.

**Why:** Fallout's and Dragon Age's slides tell what became of the peoples, which the Afterwards pages do; the eight at the fire, whom the player sat with every evening for twelve hours, are told nowhere else. Pip first called the player a Wayfarer "like in the stories", so Pip telling it to children makes the player's road the next story Tollbridge tells, and a narrator who misses Pip keeps the hollow's heaviest loss from being undone.
