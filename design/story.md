# Story

The story act by act: its parts, its people, the shards and every quest, each with its place, its activity and its turn. Everything here is checked against `vision.md`, and `lore.md` is written from it.

## Settled

### S1 The direction: a caravan on Wend's trail

- The story keeps its world, the voice that lies and the four doors, and gains a moving home: the caravan (D14).
- The player walks in Wend's footsteps. The halls' fates are his doing: as a Gazebound captain he stole shards, hid one and gave one away, so every empty hall tells the player something about the man whose voice they trust.
- A rival with a face: the Grass King's heir leads the Gazebound and races the player to the halls, with a point the player half agrees with.
- A clock inside the player: carrying the ember turns them into the Eye bit by bit, and seeing through stone is its first symptom (D15), so the second reveal is something the player feels coming.
- The past is played, not read, the way The Witcher 3 hands the player Ciri whenever someone tells her story.

**Why:** As the pack ships, the story's goal is a collection and only two of its people live in the present. A caravan, a rival and Wend's trail give it people now and a pressure, which is what The Witcher 3 (a person to find), Cyberpunk 2077 (a clock) and Red Dead Redemption 2 (a gang falling apart) hang their stories on.

### S2 The acts: a prologue, three acts and a finale

| Part | Where | The caravan | Opens with | Closes with |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue: The Oath | The Last Camp | Takes the player in | The player wakes at the dying stranger's fire | The ember wakes, and the caravan takes the player as its scout |
| Act 1: On foot | The near ring, four of the twelve halls | Grows: people join, and their stories begin | The ember's first pull | Maren names the stranger: Wend, a Gazebound captain |
| Act 2: The sea | Across the water, four halls | Crosses the sea with the player | Ships | The first reveal: the voice is the dragon, and the caravan is broken for having trusted it |
| Act 3: The sky | The far ring of frames, four halls, out to the Last Sea | What survived of it, or nobody | Flight | The second reveal: the player is the thirteenth shard |
| Finale: The frame | The nearest frame | Whoever is left | The shards the player carries | The four doors |

- An act runs about 8-10 hours, and Act 1 is what phase 4 builds and plays first.
- Who survives the breaking depends on the player's choices, and the survivors come to the frame.

```text
End of Act 2  <Wend> Camp in the hollow, out of the wind. You'll be safe there.
              That night the gaze comes down on the hollow. By morning, nine wagons are gone.
              The voice is quiet. Then, not in Wend's voice: "I needed you alone."
```

**Why:** Three distances and three ways to travel give each act its own land and its own way across it, as The Witcher 3's three acts do. The first reveal lands about two thirds in, where BioShock's friendly voice betrays its player, and the second just before the doors, which give what follows the twist something to do. The breaking makes the lie cost people the player knows, the way Red Dead Redemption 2's gang falling apart carries its story.

### S3 The order: the place carries the hall, the count carries the act

- Each hall's fate, its people and Wend's deed there belong to its place, whenever the player reaches it.
- The act's rising line, from Maren's visits and the voice's turns to the clues to the twist, moves with how many of the act's halls the player has done, so it builds whatever the order.
- A beat bound to the count never relies on which hall came before it, but remembers what the player did there.

```text
Act 1, whichever hall comes second:
  At the fire that night a hooded traveller sits down across from you.
The cold hall, whenever the player reaches it:
  The Frost Walkers' shard lies in the ice where Wend left it, twenty winters ago.
```

**Why:** The fates stay tied to their land and peoples, as D10 and Wend's trail need, while the act still rises instead of standing still until its fourth hall, as it would if everything belonged to its place the way The Witcher 3's three regions do. The memories as the pack ships already follow the count.

### S4 The voice: Wend's words, the dragon's will

- The ember remembers Wend. It is the Eye's light and light is sight, so in the decades he carried it, it kept everything he saw, heard and said, and all of it came to the player with the ember. The dragon speaks to the player through the Eye in them and wears the voice it found there; that is also where it gets his stories, his camps and his leads.
- It wears that voice for two reasons. It needs the player willing, since the ember goes out in anyone who carries it unwillingly (S19), and as itself, the thing that burned Tamsin's town, it would never get its Eye home; Wend's is the one voice the player trusts. And it has no voice of its own: an eye only ever watched and never spoke, and the only words it has are his. Even unmasked it keeps his voice (`narration.md`); it only stops pretending to be him.
- It speaks from the first hall on. The prologue and the walk to the first hall are quiet, so its first words land as a surprise.
- Until Maren names him (1.11), the voice speaks in chat as "The stranger", in the gold he spoke in while he was dying, since nobody knows his name; from then on it is Wend.

  ```text
  First hall  The shard sinks into the ember. A warmth, then a voice you last heard dying:
              <The stranger> Well. That's one. Don't look so surprised, I did say I'd come along.
  ```

- On the road it makes short remarks: the land, the fight just won, the place the player skipped. The long talks, Wend's road, leads and warnings, come when the player sits apart at a fire. The caravan's people hear none of it, and in a tent it is silent, since nothing of the gaze passes the door.
- Unmasked after Act 2, it keeps talking, honest at last, and the journal calls it the gaze. It says what it wants, mourns, bargains and sometimes helps; it tells the player the Keepers are lying, and sometimes it is right. It cannot leave, because it is in them.

  ```text
  After the breaking  <The gaze> I have no voice. I never needed one. I only looked.
                      His is the only one I have. I'm sorry it's his.
  ```

- Near the end, Wend's real last words surface from the ember's memory, under the mask.

  ```text
  Near the end  Under the gaze's voice, faint, older, his: "...don't let it look at them..."
  ```

**Why:** A voice built from the ember's memory needs no new rule: it explains why it is Wend's, how it knows his road, why it is not quite him, and where his true whisper comes from. Small talk on the road and the weighty talks at the fire follow CD Projekt's rule of light lines while walking and important ones in quiet, and a voice that stays after the mask drops, as Johnny Silverhand stays in Cyberpunk 2077, is what keeps "whom you believe" alive through Act 3.

### S5 Closing the Eye in a team: one steps forward

- The team chooses the door together, with two thirds as for every hard decision. For Close the Eye, whoever walks into the frame first is the one who steps in.
- What happens then is everyone's, as everything one player does is (D11): if Pip survived the breaking, every player plays on as Pip; if not, the world is gone for all of them.
- In singleplayer the one who steps in is the player.

```text
The frame stands open. Nobody moves.
Kai steps forward. The light takes him, and the Eye closes.
<The gaze> For the first time... it's dark. Thank you, little light.
```

**Why:** Who gives themselves up is a choice made with the feet, in the moment, between friends, which is the strongest thing co-op can give an ending, and an ending that counts for everyone keeps the world one story.

### S6 The twelve shards: Wend's trail through twelve halls

- Four peoples still live: the swamp's witches, the Tide Folk, the Sand Singers and the Grass Kingdom. Six decisions cover seven shards, two in each act, and Wend's deeds grow darker act by act without ever being only bad.
- A shard in its hall hallows the land around it, so the gaze cannot see there. The four living peoples live on such land, which is why they survive and why taking their shard can kill them; a shard outside a hall shines, as it did in Tamsin's bell.
- A shard's gift is a power of the Eye that opens what only the Eye passes (D4). Since a shard can be left behind, a gift opens side ways and shortcuts, never the only way forward.

### S7 Why the compass knows the way: someone told the player

- A goal that is not a hall comes with words: a person, a page, a sign or the voice tells the player where. Someone who knows the place names it, and the compass shows its exact mark from the start; someone who only heard of it gives a rumour, "somewhere north-east", and the compass shows a direction and a rough distance until, within about 256 blocks, the place shows itself: its smoke, its banner, its broken tower.
- The caravan follows the marks the player leaves on the road, and in the evening its smoke shows them where it camped.

```text
<Bram> They took the boy to the outpost by the twin lakes, north-east. Two days' walk.
[Side] Bram's Son   Compass ◆ north-east, 1,812 blocks
<A drover> Gazebound? There's a camp somewhere north. Don't ask me where.
[Side] The Drover's Rumour   Compass ◄ north, about 2,000 blocks
```

**Why:** Leads as the pack ships already come with a direction and a distance from whoever told them, so the compass shows what the teller knew: exactly, roughly, or, for a hall, only the ember's feeling. Every quest starts with someone speaking, which is where D12's people come in.

### S8 The doors: what each answers, opens and leaves behind

- Close the Eye means the Eye finally rests. The player steps into the frame, and the Eye, with them in it, closes for good. The dragon does not die: for the first time in its life it sleeps. The gaze ends, with no more hordes, no hearts torn open and no burning towns. The voice says goodbye once, in its own voice, grateful, and the survivors walk on without the player.

  ```text
  Close the Eye  <The gaze> For the first time... it's dark. Thank you, little light.
                 The frame goes still. Your friends are alone at the fire.
  ```

- The four doors answer the dragon's tragedy, an eye that can never look away: Blind it kills it, Give it its sight lets it look forever, Keep the oath leaves it half-blind as it is, and Close the Eye lets it stop looking.
- **What opens the closed doors.** Keep the oath needs at least two of the four living peoples to trust the player, since they are the ones who take the shards back and hide them again; leaving them a shard or helping them in side quests earns it. Close the Eye needs the words that close an Eye, which only the Scribes' books hold and only Ilse can read: it opens if the player reached the Scribes' hall and Ilse survived the breaking, or if she taught them to read the books in her side quest.
- **The more shards are set, the more the dragon sees.** In Blind it, the dragon fights with the sight the player gave it: fewer shards make it blinder and easier to kill, but leave the player fewer gifts. In Give it its sight, the lands where the player left a shard stay hallowed, dark to the gaze and safe. Keep the oath and Close the Eye do not depend on the count.
- **The world after each door**, since the game plays on:

  | Door | The world after |
  |---|---|
  | Blind it | The road's rules end: no hordes, no stillness, no ember's hunger, and the sky is the player's. What D7 cut comes back: planting, breeding, beds, chests. The last quest is "Build a home" |
  | Give it its sight | The home the dragon promised: one place where nothing comes at night, and there what D7 cut comes back. Everywhere else the gaze sees all, its creatures hunt every night and towns burn |
  | Keep the oath | The road goes on as it is: the player stays a Wayfarer under its rules and cannot settle, and one day passes the ember on |
  | Close the Eye | The gaze is gone, so the rules end and what D7 cut comes back, as with Blind it. If Pip survived the breaking, the player plays on as Pip, grown, carrying their ember, with "Build a home" ahead; in a team, every player does. If Pip did not survive, the world is gone for the player: they never wake again |


**Why:** The lore's oldest line about the dragon is that its eye was never allowed to close, so the merciful door is the one that closes it, saving the world and the voice the player grew to like at the price of the player alone. Opening the closed doors through trust and through Ilse lets the road decide the last choice, the count of shards makes every shard left behind matter even at the end, and a world that changes with the door makes the choice something the player goes on living in; Pip carries the merciful door only if the player kept Pip alive.

It raises:

- **Phase 2:** each door's scene and what Wend's last whisper says, written with the `lore` skill.
- **Phase 3:** the dragon fight, the ended world and playing on as Pip, settled in `systems.md` (M14).

### S9 The caravan: a burnt town looking for a place the gaze cannot see

- Most of the caravan are the survivors of Tollbridge, a town at a river crossing that grew too big and shone too brightly until the gaze found it, joined on the road by strays: a herder, a Keeper's apprentice and others. One or two come from elsewhere, such as a Gazebound deserter who served under Wend, or one of the last of a people whose shard Wend took.
- They are looking for a place the gaze cannot see, and the voice promises them one. It leads them towards it, and into the gaze at the end of Act 2. At the frame, the door Give it its sight offers the survivors that dream, at the world's cost.
- They cross the sea by ship with the player: finding ships for everyone is the quest that opens Act 2, and the camp is set up again on the far shore.
- Who survives the breaking is decided person by person: everyone in the caravan has a side quest of their own, and doing it, failing it and what the player did for them decide their fate.

```text
<Wend> There's a valley past the sea where nothing comes at night. I've seen it. Bring them.
Old Tamsin, at the fire: "Ask him again how far."
```

**Why:** A camp of strays gives every member a past of their own, as Red Dead Redemption 2's gang does, and a deserter or a people's last child brings the quarrel and the stake a single town would lack. A safe place promised by the voice makes the breaking the price of its lie and the door its last temptation, and survival decided by each person's own quest, as in Mass Effect 2's final mission, makes the side quests carry the main story.

### S11 The peoples and factions: four peoples left, one cult, Keepers at the gate

- **The Twelve Peoples:** about four still live as peoples in their land; the rest are scattered or dead, and only their places remain.
- **The settled world:** the villages, towns and capitals are the people who forgot the oath. They live, and the gaze burns some of them as the story goes, the way it burned the caravan's town before the story begins.

  ```text
  The village where Hob bought iron is black timber now. A dog you knew sits in the road.
  ```

- **The Gazebound** are one cult under the Grass King's heir. Maren's turn and Corin's desertion are its cracks from inside.
- **The Keepers,** few and old, guard the frames at the far ring and bar the way, because they know what a thirteenth shard is. Their knowledge and trust are Act 3's gates, and the second reveal comes from them.
- **Who owns which places** (D17):

  | Places | Belong to |
  |---|---|
  | Pillager outposts, woodland mansions, Illager camps and forts | The Gazebound |
  | Villages, towns, capitals and taverns, and the ruins of the towns the gaze burned | The settled world |
  | Temples, monuments, old mines and the peoples' own places | The Twelve Peoples, living or gone |
  | Strongholds and their libraries | The Keepers |
  | Ancient Cities and the deep dark | The first civilisation, dead |
  | Campsites and old camps | Wayfarers and travellers |

**Why:** Living peoples carry D10's living fates and D4's trust gates, while the gone ones keep the mystery and Nell's grief without crowding a sparse world. Burning towns put the lore where the player sees it, one cult gives the rival a face, and Keepers who bar the way make the second reveal something the player runs into.

### S12 The clock inside: the player shines

- The player turns into the Eye in three stages, one an act, each tied to quests (D15) and each easy to explain away until the next:

  | Act | What happens | How it is explained away |
  |---|---|---|
  | Act 1 | Endermen do not flinch from the player's gaze. They hear the calling stones before the ember feels them. Agna finds their heart slower than anyone's, at the caravan's first fire (S23) | "Wayfarers are strange." The voice laughs it off |
  | Act 2 | At the Jungle Folk's hall they first see through stone. Their eyes glow faintly in the dark, which their friends see. The peoples' guardians bow to them | Nobody can, and Agna stops laughing |
  | Act 3 | They dream the dragon's dreams: the End, the void, an eye that cannot close. The Keepers know them at the door and bar the way, which is the second reveal | Nothing explains it away any more |

  ```text
  Act 1  Mother Agna, two fingers on your wrist: "Slow. Like something asleep."
  Act 2  Corin, at night: "Your eyes. Did you know they do that?"
  Act 3  You wake in the tent. For a moment you were looking down at the world, and it was very small.
  ```

- The cost is that the player shines. The more of the Eye is in them, the more their heart shines like a shard (S18), and the more the gaze's creatures come for them and the harder they search; friends at their side feel it too. This takes the place of monsters simply growing stronger.
- Each shard taken is therefore a gift for the player, more sight for the dragon at the end and a brighter player on the way. Only taking fewer shards slows the clock; Agna's remedy, from her side quest, dims the player's shine for a night.
- In a team the clock moves with the shards the team shares (D11), and every player takes it as their own (D5).

**Why:** Clues in plain sight that only make sense afterwards are what made BioShock's twist land, so each stage hides in an explanation until the next. A cost that grows from the gaze searching what shines gives the rising danger a reason, and gives leaving a shard a reward on the road and not only at the frame.

How brightly the player shines and what it does are settled in `systems.md` (M9).

### S13 The memories: Wend's life, played where it happened

- Wend walked the same places decades before the player and did other things there. At such a place, often an empty hall, the player touches the calling stone or what he left, the ember flares, and they wake in a dreamed copy of the place as it was then, as Wend, with his gear. They play a few minutes of what he did, and come back with what the quest needs: who, where, why.
- The past cannot be changed. Some choices are offered and refused, which shows who he was.
- The real world around the player's friends never changes while they dream.

```text
Now      The hall is empty. Nell, behind you: "Someone took it. I want a name."
         You touch the calling stone. The ember flares.
Memory   Twenty winters ago. The hall is lit, the shard on its pedestal. Your hands are Wend's,
         and your ember still burns. The Frost Walkers block the stair.
         [Memory] The Theft - lift the shard, fight your way out through the snow
         An old woman grabs your arm: "Please." (Spare her - you can't; he didn't.)
         You bury the shard in the ice of a frozen lake, a day east, and mark the place.
Now      You're back. Nell is watching you. "Well? Who was it?"
         Tell her it was Wend  /  Say you didn't see
         [Main] Under the Ice - the frozen lake, a day east
```

The memories, met out of order, place by place (S3):

| In his life | What he did | Where it is played | What it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Young, a Wayfarer | Walked happily and made his first camp | His first camp | Who he was before |
| Young | Hid a shard in a young town's bell, to keep it from the Gazebound | The First Village's hall | Why Tamsin's town burned |
| Serving the Gazebound, his ember still burning | Stole the Frost Walkers' shard, and her people died | Nell's hall | Who took it, and where it is |
| The night he gave in | Took the Gazebound's bread, and stopped walking | His last camp | Why he broke the oath |
| A captain | Fought the Sand Singers' worm and fled | The Sand Singers' hall | What the worm is |
| Old, his ember dark | Raised Maren in the grass halls | His room in the mansion | Why Maren turns |
| Old | Read in the Scribes' books what a thirteenth shard is | The Scribes' hall | What the player is |

### S18 The gaze: it searches for its Eye

- The gaze sees only what shines: a shard, an ember, and a living heart, which shines brighter the longer it stays in one place. From the End it cannot tell one from another.
- It wants its Eye back more than anything, so it sends its creatures to whatever shines. They look into every heart for an eye, and they tear it open to look; finding none, they move on. A town that stood long and still is the brightest heap of hearts there is, so they tear it apart, and its overturned hearths burn the rest. Tamsin's town really held a shard, in its bell, and was hit hardest.
- Its creatures can find a shard but never carry one; only an ember can. So the gaze needs a carrier for what it finds, which is why it grew the player (D5), why the voice guides them, and why the Gazebound feed Wayfarers.
- The Gazebound serve it to use it: they believe its sight will run through them once the Eye is set. At their oath, every Gazebound lets the gaze's creatures open their chest just far enough to look; not all of them survive it, and those who do carry the scar over the heart. The gaze never searches a heart twice, so its creatures leave them alone, as monsters leave Illagers alone in Minecraft. They take it for a blessing, but it is only a searcher passing a place already searched, and the gaze does not serve them. Corin carries the scar too, and the caravan resents that monsters pass him by.

```text
Mother Agna, over a body on the road: "Look. They never take anything. They only open the chest."
```

**Why:** A gaze that searches explains every hunted thing at once, the towns, the Wayfarers and the still, and keeps the dragon sad rather than evil, while hearts that shine like shards make its creatures' cruelty the same mistake made over and over. That its creatures cannot carry what they find is what makes the player worth growing.

### S19 The ember: the Eye's light

- The ember is the Eye's light. When the Eye was broken, its body became the shards and its light came loose as a spark, which the first Wayfarers caught in their fire; everyone who sat at that fire carried a piece of it away, and every ember since was lit at a fire shared freely from one of theirs. Light lights light without growing less, so a shared fire lights an ember in everyone at it: the team, and every player who joins it later (D11).
- Light is sight, and it lives on what is new: a still life shows it nothing new, so it starves and dims, which is also why varied food keeps it strong. A dying ember's light seeks its body and flies home to the Eye, and the dragon sees a little more. Carrying the ember and walking is the guarding itself, which is what the oath protects, and the dragon grew stronger as Wayfarers settled and their embers went out.
- Light seeks its body, so the ember pulls towards the shards, and a shard can be lifted only by the light it came from. The Gazebound therefore need a living ember: Wend carried shards for them while his burned, what he left lies where he left it, and the heir wants the player's.
- Wend gave the player the ember so its light would not go home. His ember had been dark for twenty years and he was dying; a dying ember's light flies home to the Eye, and only the Eye's own body could light it again. So he gave it to the one thing the gaze grew to carry its Eye home, and bet that the player would choose otherwise.
- The ember finds halls, not shards. On every pedestal the first Wayfarers set a calling stone, kin to their waystones, which only an ember hears, so Wayfarers could find the halls and look after them. Far off the ember only feels whether the player walks towards the stone or away, flaring and guttering; within about 256 blocks it knows the stone's place; and the stone stays when the shard is gone, so in an empty hall it shows what happened, because it saw.
- The player is the thirteenth splinter. When the Eye was broken, a splinter fell unnoticed; it grew over the centuries, as a shard grew the Sand Singers' worm, and the gaze shaped it into a person, because only a heart that keeps walking can carry the ember home. That is why the ember woke in them: the light found its body. With every shard they take, more of the Eye comes together in them, which is the clock inside (S12) and what Close the Eye spends.

**Why:** One thing, the Eye's light, explains the pull, the carrying, the oath, the stillness, the splitting at a fire and why the gaze needs the player, so the rules of the road are the story's rules too. A calling stone lets an empty hall still be found and still tell its tale.

### S20 The breaking: why the Eye was broken

- The first Wayfarers were the survivors of the Ancient Cities, the first great civilisation.
- The Eye sat in the portal between worlds, and through it the dragon watched everything that stood still and sent its creatures there; that is how the Cities fell. The survivors tore the Eye out of the portal, and the dragon went blind.
- The Eye cannot be destroyed: fire, sea and hammer only broke it. Whole, it could be set again in any frame, so they broke it into twelve shards and sent each to one of twelve peoples at the far ends of the world. The Twelve Peoples hid the shards in halls, and the Wayfarers walked, keeping the ember.

**Why:** The breaking is the oldest thing the player learns and the root of every rule, so it has to answer why the Eye was not simply destroyed: it could not be.

### S21 The dragon and the silence

1. Beyond all the worlds lies the silence, a nothing that wants everything to stop: sound, movement, life. Where it touches, things go quiet and die, and the sculk is the silence growing.
2. The End is the last shore before it; the void under the End's islands is the silence.
3. The builders of the End cities, an old people long gone, made the dragon to guard that shore and bound it there, so it could never leave its post.
4. The silence also seeps into any world wherever life stands still too long, because a place that stays still grows thin. So the dragon gave up one of its eyes, which its makers set into a portal that touched every world, and through it watched them all.
5. Where a place stood still too long, it sent its creatures to break it up and keep life moving: cruel, but it kept the silence out.
6. The Ancient Cities saw only a monster sending its creatures at them, and decided to kill it. The only way to the End was the dragon's own portal, which it watched, so they built a gate of their own, powered by the Nether's fire, to break through.
7. Their gate opened past the End, straight onto the silence. It poured into the Cities and they fell silent: the deep dark, whose blind Warden, hunting by sound, is the Cities' last guard.
8. The survivors tore the Eye out of the portal and broke it (S20). The dragon went blind, the silence slowly spreads with nothing watching, and the dragon wants its Eye back to watch again, or so it says.

This pack is about the dragon and the gaze, and the silence stays in the background. It is hinted at along the way: in the deep dark, its sculk and its Warden, in an old book, in a line from the dragon, which may let slip a little of its side, and in one line at the very end, after whichever door, that says something else is still coming. A little explanation is fine; the whole story is not told. No door ends the silence, which leaves it for a part two.

**Why:** A guard with a cruel way of guarding is what makes the dragon sad rather than evil, and knowing it keeps every later decision consistent, but told in full in this pack it would pull the story away from the dragon and the gaze. - What Wend really meant, "don't let it look at them", surfaces only at the frame, as his real last whisper (S4, F1). Until then the player follows a real deathbed wish that the voice has finished its own way.

**Why:** A story needs its goal said in the first hour, as The Witcher 3 says "find Ciri"; a dying man's unfinished charge gives it there, and the burnt town makes it personal before the voice ever speaks. The voice's lie then grows out of a real wish, which makes it easy to believe and makes the truth at the frame a turn rather than a contradiction.

### S23 The prologue in detail

The prologue step by step, as the slice builds it (`slice.md`, step 2). Every line is a placeholder for the `lore` skill, and the voice stays silent throughout (S4).

**P1 The Last Fire** (the Last Camp; Talk, then Build):

1. The world's first player wakes at dawn on the ground by the Last Camp's lit fire, remembering nothing before it. The stranger sits across the flames, propped against a log, grey and short of breath, with a scar over his heart: the Gazebound's mark (S18), which Corin explains in 1.3.
2. He speaks first, in chat, with answers to click (M3). He says plainly that he is dying and why: he is old, and finding the player and bringing them to his fire took the last of him. He knows the player's face and will not say why. He presses a cold coal into their hand and names it, "That's an ember", and only then does the compass name it. He gives three rules, each with its reason in his words, walk, never stay, sleep only in a tent by a fire, and dies with his charge cut off (S22), slumping where he sits.
3. The narrator says that he is dead and gives the reason to bury him. The player buries him: a right-click on him, the screen dims, and a grave stands by the camp with a blank stone. Whoever buried him chooses its words from numbered answers, a few ready words, their own (only then the stone's writing screen opens, with a task saying what to do there) or none, and the journal keeps them.
4. His pack is the first reward, waiting in the journal for each player to claim: a backpack, a tent, a bed of any colour and a little food. Nothing else comes with a new world. Silent Gear's blueprints come later, as Hob's patterns at the caravan's first fire (M18).

The Last Camp is his fire, burning, his tent and his things, with no grave until the player buries him and no letter, since he says it himself. It stays as it is for good: the last memory is played there (S13).

**P3 Ash** (the burnt town; Investigate, then Hold; the gate is Strength):

1. The quest puts the town down at dusk past the player's camp, on the way they walked, out of sight behind the land and close enough that its smoke shows over it, already burning, with fires that do not spread. It is a town that grew too big: about a hundred blocks across and about thirty buildings round the bell tower's square, from the inn, the smithy and the market to houses of every size. It grows on its land the way a village does: streets leave the square and bend with the ground, every building stands on its own footing beside its street with its door towards it, and a path joins each door to the street. Its site keeps 64 blocks clear of any other place and prefers land beside a river, and where it finds one the town stands on both banks with the toll bridge across; elsewhere it stands without. About thirty-two dead lie in it, each with a face of their own: most indoors, where they hid, some at the doors they tried to get out of, fewer in the streets where they fled. It stays afterwards as a ruin of the settled world: Tamsin's town.
2. The player investigates by night with the ember sense (M10): bodies with their shirts torn open over the heart, purses still on their belts, emeralds in an open strongbox, each a line in the journal.
3. The survivors, Tamsin, Pip, Agna and Hob, hide in the cellar of the stone bell tower, and each gives their name there, since the player knows none of them. Resting at their fire makes it where the player wakes if they fall that night, and Hob hands them a blade from his forge.
4. The gaze's creatures come back, drawn by the ember that has just woken: the player shines (S12) before anyone knows what that means.
5. The hold: four waves at the cellar door, with the survivors as their target. Each wave makes for the bell tower's door and beats on it; left to them, the door cracks and gives way, and they come down the stairs into the cellar. If one falls, the step is tried again from the cellar, with the failed try's waves gone (D6). The night eases towards midnight while the hold lasts; after the last wave it passes to dawn in half a minute, and what is left crumbles in the sun.
6. In the morning the survivors beg the one with the ember to lead them. Tamsin sits by the fallen bell and asks what to do with it; she takes it whatever the player says, and remembers what they said, which comes back in The Bell (1.10).
