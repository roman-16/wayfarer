# Lore

The source of truth for every quest, book and item text, and the reason behind every rule of the pack. A mechanic without a reason here does not belong in the pack.

## The world

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What is the dragon? | The guardian of the End, bound there by the builders of the End cities. It can never leave the End |
| Why one eye, and how could it use it? | To guard more than one world, the dragon gave up one of its own eyes. It was set into the portal between worlds, and through it the dragon watched everything the portal touched |
| What does the dragon want? | To see. It cannot stop looking, and it was never cruel for the sake of it; an eye that was never allowed to close wants its sight back more than anything |
| Endermen and ender pearls | Endermen carry splinters of that gaze, which is why they hate being looked at. Ender pearls are those splinters; mixed with blaze powder they fly home to the portal. That is why the vanilla eye of ender works at all |
| Why does it hunt settlements? | The Eye sees what stays still. The longer a place is lived in, the brighter it shines in its sight, and cities shine brightest |
| What happened? | The Ancient Cities were the first great civilisation and shone so brightly that the dragon reached through. They drew their power from the Nether and built their own gate to strike back (the portal-shaped frame in every Ancient City), and failed: it opened once, and what came through was silence. The sculk is that silence, spreading across the stone, and the Warden is their last guard. The silence is not the gaze's, which is why the Warden does not fear fire |
| Who broke the Eye? | The survivors became the first Wayfarers. They tore the Eye out of the portal, broke it into twelve shards and gave each shard to a people at the far ends of the world. Blind, the dragon faded and the portal went dark. They swore the oath: never settle, never shine, and never let the shards come together again |
| Why are there twelve strongholds? | When the Eye was torn out, the portal broke into twelve frames. The Keepers, Wayfarers who would not walk, carried the frames out to a ring past the edge of every map, as far from everyone as they could, and stayed to guard them. Their libraries hold the old records |
| Which is the true gate? | Any of the twelve frames. Each was part of the portal, and each takes the whole Eye. The ember leads to the nearest |
| Why does a frame refuse while Gazebound stand in the hall? | The Keepers warded every frame against the Gazebound: it will not take the Eye while one of them stands in its hall |
| Why is there no Nether? | When the Ancient Cities' gate failed, the Keepers sealed the Nether forever, so nobody could repeat it. Obsidian no longer takes a spark |
| Why is there netherite and crimson iron in the deep dark? | The Ancient Cities drew their power from the Nether, and what they drew up still lies in the deep stone around them |
| Who are the Gazebound? | An Illager cult that wants the dragon's sight back to rule through it. They are the heirs of the Grass King |
| Who was the Grass King? | The king of the grasslands, whose people were one of the Twelve Peoples. He kept his people's shard in his crown instead of hiding it, and it spoke to him in his dead father's voice. For one summer he saw everything that stood still in his kingdom and ruled like a god. His own people tore it from his crown and sealed it under the savanna, and drove out his heirs and everyone who followed them |
| Where do the Illagers come from? | They are the Grass Kingdom's outcasts and their children. Their outposts, raids and mansions are the Gazebound's |
| Why is the dragon growing stronger now? | People forgot the oath. Villages became towns and towns became capitals, so the world shines again. And the Gazebound are hunting the shards |
| Why can't the Gazebound simply take the shards? | Only an ember can feel where a shrine lies, and an ember cannot be taken: it passes only at a fire shared freely, and it goes dim in anyone who stops walking. Every ember they took from a dead hand went out, so they stopped hunting Wayfarers and began to feed them instead |
| Why put the Eye back together? | Because the stranger asked it, and the voice at the fire wants it. The truth is darker (see The stranger and The voice at the fire). Once it is whole in your ember, the only safe end is to set it in a frame and kill the dragon while it is still half-blind, so nothing is left for anyone to see through |
| Why are the shrines hidden? | The Twelve Peoples hid them so that neither the dragon nor the Gazebound could find them. Only the ember can. They set them far apart, so that no one would ever carry two shards by accident, and hid every stair under a cover that looks like nothing to anyone without an ember |
| What are the Twelve Peoples' places? | Each people left something of theirs near its shrine: a ship, a house, a temple, a grave. Its book names the place, and what they could not carry is still there for the one who took their shard |
| Why are nights so hard? | At night the dragon's gaze presses hardest. Every fourth night it turns fully towards the world (a Sleepless Night): nobody sleeps, the ember burns too bright to fold into a tent, and its horde comes. Every fifth night the Eye is reflected in the moon (a Red Dawn), and monsters walk thicker and stronger. Every twentieth night brings both |
| Why do monsters crumble at dawn? | The gaze presses hardest at night, and by day it looks away. Whatever it sent that still stands under the open sky has nothing holding it together and crumbles to dust; in caves and shadow its creatures wait for night. Illagers are people, and the sun does nothing to them |
| Why does a fire keep monsters away? | Fire is the ember's kin, a light of the Eye's own kind, and the gaze's creatures cannot bear to be seen by it. On an ordinary night none is born in a fire's circle, none steps into it, and none will go for someone standing in it |
| Why does the circle fail on hard nights? | On a Red Dawn the Eye looks down from the moon, and its creatures are not afraid of being seen. On a Sleepless Night the gaze itself turns towards the world, and its horde walks straight through the fire. Illagers never fear a fire: they are people |
| Why do monsters grow stronger? | Every day you carry the ember and every shard you take, the gaze sees you more clearly |
| Why can ships only sail with four shards? | On open water a lone ember shines like a beacon, and the gaze finds every ship that carries one. Four shards together dim it enough to cross |
| Why can I only fly with six? | In the air the ember flickers and the phantoms see it. Six shards steady it enough to fly, for a while, on an aircraft or on wings |
| What is an Ember Veil? | Phantom membrane woven around the ember hides its light from the phantoms that shed it. Gaze tears remember how not to be seen and echo shards carry the deep dark's silence, and each deepens the veil |
| Why is the sky dangerous? | The sky belongs to the dragon, and phantoms are its eyes in the air. In the air the ember flickers and the gaze sees it, and the pull of the shards fades away from the ground |
| Why are gaze tears? | Phantoms weep what they have seen. Brewers call them ghast tears after a creature nobody alive has met |
| Why is a tent safe? | A Wayfarer tent is folded space that is not anywhere, and the gaze cannot see what is not anywhere. Its creatures cannot find the door; only people pass through it. Nothing of the gaze passes that door, not even its voice |
| Why is a shrine safe? | The Twelve Peoples hallowed their halls. No monster is born inside one, and the gaze does not turn its hordes on those who shelter there |
| What are waystones? | The first Wayfarers set these stones along their roads. A stone folds the road back to any other stone you have touched, the way a tent folds space, so it only carries you along roads you have already walked |

## The player

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who am I? | No one special: a traveller who shared a fire with a dying stranger |
| Why me? | Before he died he passed you the ember, a sliver of the Eye's own light that every Wayfarer has carried since the Eye was broken. The journal he left you is your quest book, and the letter on his lectern tells you only what you need for the first day |
| Why does the ember wake only after the first day? | An ember answers only someone who keeps the oath. It wakes once you have lived a Wayfarer's first day: a tent pitched and slept in, a fire lit, everything carried |
| What does my compass show before the ember wakes? | A sleeping ember still knows its own: the embers split from it at your fire, and the stone where you fell. From the first day the compass shows your friends and your grave; the shards wait until it wakes |
| Why do I feel where the next eye is? | The ember pulls towards the shards it came from. Far away it is a feeling: it flares when you walk towards one and gutters when you walk away. Within about 500 blocks the shard answers, and you know exactly where its way down begins |
| Where do the shards go when I take them? | Into the ember. A shard sinks into the light it came from and never leaves it again: it cannot be dropped, lost or given away, not even by dying |
| Why do my friends feel the same? | When you share a fire, the ember splits. Joining a team is sharing your fire, and a shard one of you takes sinks into every ember at that fire. The voice at the fire speaks to everyone at it |
| Why does a shard show me the past? | Each shard remembers a piece of what happened. The more of the Eye comes together, the more it remembers, always in the same order: the first shard you take tells the beginning, the twelfth the end. The memories are the first Wayfarers speaking as one; they tell the truth, but they judge the stranger only by the oath |
| Why does the journal keep every page? | A Wayfarer copies every page they find into the journal, and writes down what the road taught them, so the road remembers what the traveller forgets and the next one to carry the journal learns faster. That is The Road So Far, Words of the Road and At the Fire |
| Why do I know where the stronghold is? | Once you hold all twelve, the ember remembers where the Eye was torn out. First a feeling, then certainty |
| How is the Eye set? | Touch a frame with all twelve shards in your ember, and they leave it and set themselves |
| Why do I respawn at campfires? | The ember is bound to fire. Wherever you light one, it brings you back |
| What does resting at a fire do? | Standing still at a lit fire for half a minute warms the ember. Until tomorrow's dusk it burns the fire instead of your food, so you eat like a settled person. Resting is also when the voice speaks |
| What happens to my things when I fall? | The road keeps them under a stone where you fell, each in its place, until you come back for them. The compass marks the stone |
| Why am I always hungry? | The ember burns what you eat. A Wayfarer eats twice what a settled person does, unless the ember is warm from a fire. It burns on the water too: steering a boat or ship costs food |
| Why does varied food make me stronger? | The ember feeds on the world it passes through. A settled person eats the same bread every day; a Wayfarer eats the road, and the more kinds of it you eat, the stronger it keeps you |
| Why do I know in the morning that the night will be hard? | The ember feels the gaze turn before it turns. At dawn of a hard night, its carrier knows. The warning comes from the gaze itself |
| Why can't I settle? | The ember marks you. Stay within about 256 blocks of one place and on the second day the ember dims; on the third the gaze finds you, and its horde comes that night and every night you stay, through fire and tent alike. Walk on and the ember brightens. Nights in a tent count as staying, and a shrine still shelters you |
| Why do I see monsters through stone? | From the seventh shard the Eye in your ember is whole enough to see with. Stand still in the dark and you see what the gaze sees: everything that walks within about 32 blocks. The Grass King saw like that, for one summer |
| What are leads? | Places someone told you the way to: the voice, Maren, or a people's book. A lead always comes with a direction and a distance, the journal keeps it until you find it, and the compass marks the ones you choose to follow. Leads are never needed to go on; they pay in story and supplies |
| How does it end? | When the dragon dies, the ember goes out for those who killed it and they are free. The road's rules end with it: you eat like a settled person, no night is sleepless, the moon stays white once the dragon is dead, the ember never dims, monsters stop growing stronger, and the sky is yours. Fire still brings you back when you fall; it remembers you. The last quest is "Build a home" |

## The stranger

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who was the stranger? | Wend, a Wayfarer who broke the oath. Tired of the road, he stayed with the Gazebound, who fed him and gave him a hall and a name. His ember went dark in his ninth winter, and he became their captain anyway, because he still carried one. He stayed twenty winters |
| Why did he give me the ember? | The Gazebound's plan: a dying captain lights a fire where its smoke is seen, gives his ember to whoever sits with him, and the Gazebound follow that smoke to the gate. Wend used their plan for his own end: he chose someone kind to carry the Eye to the gate, and asked Maren to help them finish it |
| What did he leave behind? | Two camps: his first, from his joyful young years on the road, and his last, where he stopped walking and ate the Gazebound's bread. His room in the grass halls, with a ledger the Gazebound kept on him. A letter for Maren, to open at the gate |
| Who is Maren? | A Gazebound evoker whom Wend raised in the grass halls. He taught her the road, so she can follow smoke. The captains send her after your smoke. She visits your fire, leaves food, and at last tells you who he was. At the gate she opens his letter and turns on her own band |
| Why can't anyone hurt Maren at a fire? | She comes as a guest and leaves in smoke before anything can happen; nothing fights at a fire shared freely |

## The voice at the fire

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who speaks at the fire? | The dragon, reaching through its own Eye as the Eye comes back together in your ember. From the second shard it is whole enough to speak |
| Why does it sound like the stranger? | It watched Wend sit still in the grass halls for twenty years and learned his face and his voice. It wears the voice you trust, the way a shard once spoke to the Grass King in his dead father's voice |
| Why does it speak only while I rest at a fire? | The gaze sees what stays still. It can reach you only when you are still, and a fire is where you are still |
| Why never in a tent? | Nothing of the gaze passes a tent's door |
| Why does it know where the Gazebound are? | It sees what the gaze sees |
| Why does it help me? | You are carrying its eye home. It wants you alive and walking until the Eye is whole, and it wants the Eye set |
| What does it want at the end? | To see again. When the mask is off it offers you a home that nothing will ever come to in the night, if you bring it its eye. It cannot keep that promise: it cannot look away |
| Why does it change its name? | The journal and the chat call it what you believe it is: the stranger, then Wend once Maren names him, then the gaze once the eleventh memory tells the truth |

## Plotlines

1. **The Last Wayfarer:** the main story, told through the journal (the quest book).
2. **The Twelve Peoples:** each shrine tells the story of the people who guarded its shard, in the lectern book beside its pedestal, and names a place of theirs nearby (`generator/eyes.toml`).
3. **The Memories:** twelve chapters, one with each shard, in the order they are taken (`story/books.toml`). They tell the breaking, the Keepers, the Grass King, and in the last chapters the stranger's truth and the voice's.
4. **The Voice at the Fire:** the voice in your ember, from the second shard to the End (`story/fire.toml`).
5. **Maren:** her visits, her leads, the gate and the home (`story/fire.toml`).
6. **The Keepers:** books in stronghold libraries, explaining why the frames lie so far out. The last Keeper remembers the stranger at their door.
7. **The First Gate:** a visit to an Ancient City and the truth about the deep dark.
8. **The Gazebound:** Illager outposts, raids and mansions; their captains carry pages of the cult's plan.

## Quest chapters

1. The Oath: the first tent, the backpack, a campfire camp, food, sharing a fire; the ember wakes.
2. The Road Provides: tools, weapons, armour and jewellery a Wayfarer makes from parts, in rows from flint to crimson steel and, after the dragon, the metals of the End; and the gadgets that help on the road, the atlas among them.
3. The First Eyes: eyes 1-6 on foot; sailing ships unlock at four.
4. The Sea: sailing ships, then aircraft and veils.
5. The Far Eyes: eyes 7-12.
6. The Last Road: the far stronghold, the Gazebound at the gate.
7. The Dragon.
8. Build a home.
9. The Road So Far: what happened, as you learned it (`story/notes.toml`).
10. Words of the Road: every word the road has explained (`story/notes.toml`).
11. At the Fire: every talk at the fire, and every lead until it is found (`story/fire.toml`).
12. Pages: every book the player has read, to read again.

Each eye also gets a short lore quest.
