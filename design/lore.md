# Lore

The source of truth for every quest, book, item and chat text, and the reason behind every rule of the pack. A mechanic without a reason here does not belong in the pack. It is written from `story.md`, which holds the story act by act; every name is a placeholder until the `lore` skill writes the real one.

## The dragon and the Eye

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What is the dragon? | The guardian of the End, made by the builders of the End cities, an old people long gone, and bound there so it could never leave its post |
| What does it guard? | The silence (see The silence). The pack never tells this whole; the player meets it only in hints |
| Why one eye? | From the End the dragon could not see the worlds, so it gave up one of its eyes, and its makers set it into a portal that touched every world. Through that Eye it watched them all |
| Why did it send its creatures at the still? | Where life stood still too long it sent its creatures to break it up and keep life moving. Cruel, but it was guarding |
| What happened to the Eye? | The Ancient Cities, the first great civilisation, saw only a monster sending its creatures at them. They built a gate of their own, powered by the Nether's fire, to break through to the End and kill it. The gate opened past the End, straight onto the silence, and the Cities fell silent. Their survivors tore the Eye out of the portal, and the dragon went blind |
| Why was the Eye broken and not destroyed? | It cannot be destroyed: fire, sea and hammer only broke it. Whole, it could be set again in any frame, so they broke it into twelve shards and sent each to one of twelve peoples at the far ends of the world |
| What does the dragon want now? | Its Eye back. It is an eye that was never allowed to close, and it says it wants to watch again |
| What is the gaze? | The dragon's sight through what is left of its Eye. It sees only what shines: a shard, an ember, and a living heart, which shines brighter the longer it stays in one place. From the End it cannot tell one from another |
| Why do its creatures tear people open? | The gaze searches for its Eye, so it sends its creatures to whatever shines. They look into every heart for an eye and tear it open to look; finding none, they move on. They never take anything; they only open the chest |
| Why do towns burn? | A town that has stood long and still is the brightest heap of hearts there is, so the gaze's creatures tear it apart, and its overturned hearths burn the rest. A town that holds a shard is hit hardest |
| Why can its creatures not bring a shard home? | A shard can be lifted only by the light it came from, the ember. The gaze can find but never carry, so it needs a carrier |
| Why is the dragon stronger now? | People forgot the oath: villages became towns and towns became capitals, so the world shines again. And Wayfarers settled, their embers went out, and every ember's light that goes out flies home to the Eye |
| Why are there twelve strongholds? | When the Eye was torn out, the portal broke into twelve frames. The Keepers carried them to a ring past the edge of every map and stayed to guard them |
| Which is the true gate? | Any of the twelve frames. Each was part of the portal, and each takes the Eye with however many shards it holds |
| Why does a frame refuse while Gazebound stand in its hall? | The Keepers warded every frame against the Gazebound |
| Why will a sealed hall not break? | The peoples warded their halls with the Keepers' craft: inside a ward nothing can be broken or built, until its seal opens |
| Why do monsters crumble at dawn? | The gaze presses hardest at night and looks away by day; whatever it sent that still stands under the open sky has nothing holding it together. In caves and shadow its creatures wait for night |
| Why do they walk in rain and storms? | Under cloud and rain the sky is dark enough for them to dare the day, as in shade; on the sea, that is when what lives below comes up by day |

## The silence

The truth behind the scenes, which this pack only hints at: in the deep dark, its sculk and its Warden, in an old book, in what the dragon lets slip, and in one line at the very end.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What is the silence? | A nothing beyond all the worlds that wants everything to stop: sound, movement, life. Where it touches, things go quiet and die; the sculk is the silence growing |
| Where is it? | Beyond the End, the last shore before it; the void under the End's islands is the silence. It also seeps into any world where life stands still too long, because a place that stays still grows thin |
| What is the deep dark? | The Ancient Cities, where their gate let the silence in. The blind Warden, hunting by sound, is their last guard, which is why it does not fear fire |
| Why is there netherite and crimson iron in the deep dark? | The Cities drew their power from the Nether, and what they drew up still lies in the deep stone around them |
| Why does some stone wear out any lesser pick? | The peoples built their gates from deep stone the Cities' Nether-fire hardened. Only metal from the same deep places cuts it; a softer pick blunts itself to nothing |
| Why is there no Nether? | When the Cities' gate failed, the Keepers sealed the Nether forever, so nobody could repeat it. Obsidian no longer takes a spark |
| Why do the volcanic and basalt lands look as they do? | That is where the Nether once pressed up, before the Keepers sealed it |

## The ember and the shards

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What is the ember? | The Eye's light. When the Eye was broken, its body became the shards and its light came loose as a spark, which the first Wayfarers caught in their fire |
| Who were the first Wayfarers? | The survivors of the Ancient Cities. The Twelve Peoples hid the shards in halls, and the Wayfarers walked, keeping the ember |
| How did others come to carry it? | Everyone at the first Wayfarers' fire carried a piece of it away, and every ember since was lit at a fire shared freely. Light lights light without growing less, so a shared fire lights an ember in everyone at it |
| Why must a Wayfarer keep walking? | Light is sight, and it lives on what is new. A still life shows it nothing new, so it starves and dims, and varied food keeps it strong for the same reason |
| Why do Wayfarers keep it alive? | A dying ember's light seeks its body and flies home to the Eye, and the dragon sees a little more. Carrying the ember and walking is the guarding itself |
| What is the oath? | Never settle, never shine, and everything carried. The ember answers only those who keep it |
| Why can the Gazebound not take an ember? | It passes only at a fire shared freely, and it goes out in anyone who carries it unwillingly or settles. Every ember they took by force went out |
| Why does the ember pull? | Light seeks its body. It does not feel shards from afar; it hears calling stones |
| What does the ember show when I hold it up? | It is the Eye's light, and light is sight: held up, it shows for a moment what has passed by, tracks and traces and what was dropped |
| Why does a fire fill the Flare again? | The Flare spends the ember's light all at once, and a fire, the ember's kin, fills it again |
| What is a calling stone? | The calling stone of every hall. The first Wayfarers set it there, kin to their waystones, so Wayfarers could find the halls and look after them. Only an ember hears it: from afar it only flares when I walk towards the stone and gutters when I walk away; within about 256 blocks it knows its place |
| Why does an empty hall still call? | The stone stays when the shard is gone, and it shows what happened there, because it saw |
| What does a shard do in its hall? | It hallows the land around it, so the gaze cannot see there. The living peoples live on such land |
| And outside a hall? | It shines, and draws the gaze |
| What are a shard's gifts? | Each shard taken gives a power of the Eye, such as seeing tracks glow or seeing in the dark |
| Where do the shards go when taken? | Into the ember, and they never leave it, not even by dying |
| Must every shard be taken? | No. A frame takes the Eye with however many shards it holds, and the Keepers' records say so. Every shard taken is a gift for the carrier and more sight for the dragon at the end |
| What are the waystones? | Stones the first Wayfarers set along their roads. A stone folds the road back to any other stone touched, the way a tent folds space, so it carries a Wayfarer only along roads already walked |
| What does a circle with a line through it mean? | A Wayfarer's sign for a hall looked after: "looked after, walk on". The calling stones were set so Wayfarers could find the halls and look after them, and one who looked in cut the sign on the way down and left the shard where it lay, since a shard in its hall keeps its land dark |
| What is a Wayfarer's own stone? | The first Wayfarers each carried a small stone cut from their waystones. Set down and touched, it holds the place, and the road folds back to it; like everything, it is carried |

## The player

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who am I? | No one, as far as I know: I woke by a dying stranger's fire with no name, and he gave me his ember |
| What am I really? | The thirteenth splinter. When the Eye was broken, a splinter fell unnoticed. It grew over the centuries, the way a shard grew the Sand Singers' worm, and the gaze shaped it into a person, because only a heart that keeps walking can carry the ember home |
| Why did the ember wake in me? | It had been dark for twenty years, since he stopped walking. One day's walking with me gave it new land to see, and it woke, as it would have in nobody else: the light found its body |
| What is happening to me? | With every shard I take, more of the Eye comes together in me. Endermen stop flinching from my gaze, I hear the calling stones, my heart slows; later I see through stone, my eyes glow in the dark and the peoples' guardians bow; at last I dream the dragon's dreams |
| What does it cost? | I shine. The more of the Eye is in me, the more my heart shines like a shard, and the more the gaze's creatures come for me and the harder they search. Only taking fewer shards slows it; Agna's remedy dims me for a night |
| Why do I go after the shards? | The stranger died asking me not to let "it" have them, the shards he took; the burnt town showed me what he meant. At the first hall his voice finished the sentence: bring them to a frame and put the dragon's eye out for good, so no town burns again |
| Why was the stranger dying? | He was old, and he spent the last of his strength looking for me and carrying me from the road to his fire: "I walked further than I had left in me" |
| Why did the stranger give me the ember? | His ember had been dark for twenty years and he was dying. A dying ember's light flies home to the Eye, and only the Eye's own body could light it again, so he gave it to the one thing the gaze grew to carry its Eye home, and bet I would choose otherwise |
| What is the voice? | The dragon, speaking through the Eye in me. It speaks from the first hall on: short words on the road, long talks when I sit apart at a fire. Nobody else hears it, and it is silent in a tent |
| What do I call it? | The stranger, as the journal does, since I do not know his name |
| Why does it sound like the stranger? | The ember remembers everything he saw, heard and said, and the voice wears what it found there: his voice, his stories, his camps |
| Why not as itself? | It needs me willing, since the ember goes out in an unwilling carrier, and Wend's is the voice I trust. And it has no voice of its own: an eye only ever watched. Unmasked, it keeps his voice and stops pretending to be him |
| What are the memories? | The ember remembers Wend's life. Where he once did something, touching the calling stone or what he left wakes me in a dream of the place as it was, as him, and I live what he did. I cannot change it |
| In a team? | The story is one, and it speaks to "you"; every one of us takes it as our own |

## The road

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Why is the day long and the night short? | The day is for the road: the sun slows towards evening while I am out. Night falls once we are back at the caravan's fire |
| Why does a fire keep monsters away? | Fire is the ember's kin, a light of the Eye's own kind, and the gaze's creatures cannot bear to be seen by it. On an ordinary night none is born in a fire's circle, none steps into it, and none goes for someone standing in it |
| Why does the circle fail on hard nights? | On a Sleepless Night the gaze has found us, and its horde walks straight through the fire. Under a red moon the Eye looks down from the moon, and its creatures are not afraid of being seen. Illagers never fear a fire: they are people |
| When does the gaze find the caravan? | When it stays too long in one place, or when something we carry shines too brightly |
| Why can I not sleep but in a tent by a fire? | Sleep is the stillest a heart gets. A tent hides me from the gaze, and a lit fire keeps its creatures off |
| Why is a tent safe? | A tent is folded space that is not anywhere, and the gaze cannot see what is not anywhere. Its creatures cannot find the door, and nothing of the gaze passes it, not even its voice |
| Why can I keep things only in a tent? | Everything carried, the oath says. A Wayfarer cannot set a hoard down in the world; a tent folds its store along |
| Why can I not settle? | A heart that stays shines. Stay within about 256 blocks of one place and on the second day the ember dims; on the third the gaze finds me |
| Why do seeds not take? | Roots are stillness, and the ember withers whatever a Wayfarer plants, so the gaze never finds it growing |
| Why will animals not breed near me? | They feel the gaze in the ember |
| Why am I always hungry? | The ember burns what I eat: a Wayfarer eats twice what a settled person does, unless the ember is warm from a fire. It burns on the water too |
| Why does varied food make me stronger? | The ember lives on what is new, and that is true of food as much as sights |
| Where do I wake after a fall? | The ember is bound to fire and to its kin: it brings me back to the nearest warmth it knows, a friend's ember, or else the last fire I sat by |
| What happens to my things when I fall? | The road keeps them under a stone where I fell, until I come back; the compass marks the stone |
| Why is ore thin in plain stone? | The Ancient Cities and the peoples mined the land bare long ago. What is left lies in their old mines and in the deep stone around the Cities |
| What is frost iron? | Iron from the deep stone at an Ancient City's edge, gone cold where the silence came through the Cities' gate: it never warms in the hand and never rusts, and no common fire melts it, only a smith's forge working through a night. The Frost Walkers dug it at a City's edge and stopped at its wall. It is the first metal from the deep places a Wayfarer can hold |
| Why do villagers trade so little? | Towns that grow burn, so they keep their stores, fear strangers and have few children |
| Why does my gear never break? | A Wayfarer throws nothing away. Worn out, a tool is still carried, no better than a hand, until a fire or a smith mends it |
| Why can only a smith set an enchantment? | The old enchantments are written in the smiths' script; only a smith who knows it can set one into steel |
| Why do the gaze's creatures come to noise? | The gaze only sees, but its creatures hear: what they hear, they come to look at, and then they see what shines |
| How does the compass know the way? | To a hall, the ember feels its calling stone, warmer and colder. To anything else, someone told me: a person, a page, a sign or the voice. If they knew the place, I know it too; if it was only a rumour, I know the way roughly, until the place shows itself |
| What does the journal hold? | Everything I know so far, as I would tell a friend: the story, the people, the words of the world, every book and talk. A word I have never heard is not in it |
| Why is the sea dangerous? | On open water a lone ember shines like a beacon, and what lives below comes up to look |
| Why is the sky dangerous? | The sky is the dragon's, and phantoms are its eyes in the air; in the air the ember flickers and they see it |
| What is the Last Sea? | The edge of the world past the far ring, where the land gives out. Its cold grows deadly the farther out a boat goes, and nothing the dragon sent there ever came back. Whoever falls on it, the road keeps their things on the nearest shore |

## The caravan

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who are they? | The survivors of Tollbridge, a town at a river crossing that grew too big and shone too brightly until the gaze found it, joined on the road by strays |
| Why do they follow me? | I carry an ember and can feel the way. I am their scout, and they follow the marks I leave on the road |
| What are they looking for? | A place the gaze cannot see. The voice says it knows one, past the sea |
| Why do I never see them travel? | They break camp after I set out and make camp before I come back; in the evening their smoke shows me where |
| Who are its people? | Old Maud, the elder, who carries the town's bell; Cadoc, a Gazebound deserter who served under Wend; Drifa, last of the Frost Walkers; Bram, a herder; Alruna, a Keeper's apprentice; Mother Agna, the healer, who knows clothes against the cold and the heat, food and remedies; Hob, the smith, who knows metal; Pip, a child who wants to be a Wayfarer and knows every horse in the stories, whose mother died the night Tollbridge burned and whom Agna took in at dawn. Maud knows tents and the camp |
| Why does Hob mend only halfway at first? | His hammer cracked the night Tollbridge burned. With the Frost Walkers' frost iron he makes one worth the name, and from then on restores anything fully, forges frost iron and sets the old enchantments |
| Why does their camp never burn? | A fire shared freely does not turn on the ones who share it: no spark of it catches their tents, and the sky's fire passes them by |

## The people

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who was the stranger? | Wend, a Wayfarer who broke the oath. Young, he walked happily and looked in on the Diggers' hall every few winters, leaving its shard where it lay. Then he hid a shard in a young town's bell to keep it from the Gazebound, served them as their finder while his ember still burned, bought the swamp's witches' sight with a word he broke, stole the Frost Walkers' shard, and one winter night took their bread and stopped walking. His ember went dark in his ninth winter in their grass halls; he became their captain anyway, raised Malgven, and read in the Scribes' books what a thirteenth shard is |
| Who is Malgven? | A Gazebound evoker Wend raised from a child; it was her hand that held out the bread the night he gave in. She follows the smoke of my fire, visits it as a guest, leaves bread as she once did for him, keeps her name back in his words, "Names are for people who stay", names Wend, and at the frame opens his letter and turns on her own band |
| Who left the Cache? | A Wayfarer nobody can name, who signed their journal only with the circle mark. They found a place they loved, built a cabin for one winter and stayed; the ember grew small, and one winter it went out. Their stone of one's own waited, wrapped in cloth, for someone who walks |
| Why can no one hurt Malgven at a fire? | She comes as a guest and leaves in smoke before anything can happen; nothing fights at a fire shared freely |
| Why did Wend sink the Frost Walkers' shard in the ice? | To keep it from the Gazebound he served. As their finder he led them to the hall, and the Frost Walkers died on its stair; he told his masters the stone was empty, and sank the shard in a frozen lake a day east, where only he knew, as he had hidden one in a young town's bell years before |
| Who is Drifa? | The last of the Frost Walkers. Twenty winters ago, a girl out hunting, she came home to her people dead on their hall's stair, grey-coat tracks in the snow and the hall empty; she never saw who took the shard. Her people's calling stone shows the past, but only to a Wayfarer's fire, so she has lived on in their winter house and waited for one. She wants a name |
| Who is Edda? | The last shipwright on the home land's coast. Nobody crosses the sea, and her husband tried three summers ago: he made landfall on the far shore, at a bay where people fish by night and nothing comes for them, and the sea took him on the way home. His wreck and his log lay on the beach near her yard, and she would build no hull for sailing until someone read the log to its end |
| Why has nobody crossed the sea? | Something lives below the open water and comes up at night to look at a light; boats that go out past the last headland do not come back. The coast's people fish within sight of land |
| Who is Cadoc? | A Gazebound deserter, an Illager born to the Grass Kingdom's outcasts, with their scar over his heart. He served under the stranger for six winters and will not say his name; he knows Malgven from the stranger's company and will not say hers either, and she lets him be; the heir hunts him, and his brother still serves at the outpost above the ford where the caravan found him |
| Who is Morwen? | The Tide Folk's elder. Her people keep two shards on one stone under their fire pit, their own and the Drowned City's, and the guardians of their bay bow to anyone who carries an ember. She will not stop a Wayfarer who takes them, only say what it costs |
| Who were the Drowned City's last people? | The few left when the sea took the most beautiful city on the coast. They climbed to their hall in the red canyons, lifted their shard and carried it to the one bay the eye could not see, the Tide Folk's, and swore the Tide Folk to keep it |
| Who is Sefa? | The Sand Singers' singer. She sings the worm's song, and the song of the man with a coal in his hand who fought it and ran, which is the funniest they have |
| Who was the girl in grey? | Malgven, as a child. Thirty winters ago, at Wend's last camp, she held out the Gazebound's bread with both hands, a tiny eye stitched on her coat, and he took it and stopped walking |
| Who waits in the Jungle Folk's hall? | The Grass King's heir, with Wend's maps of the halls. He found the way in and cannot lift the shard, since only an ember wakes a stone, so he waits for one |
| Who is the Grass King's heir? | The leader of the Gazebound, of the Grass King's line. He wants a world that is seen, with no crime and no war, as his ancestor's kingdom had for one summer, at the price of everyone being watched |
| Who was the Grass King? | The king of the grasslands, one of the Twelve Peoples. He kept his people's shard in his crown instead of hiding it, and for one summer he saw everything that stood still in his kingdom. His own people tore it from his crown and sealed it, and drove out his heirs and everyone who followed them |

## The peoples and factions

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who were the Diggers? | A people of the forests who followed ore down. Their tunnels run under half the world, and many of its old mines are theirs. Over their hall the shard kept their forest dark to the gaze, but they dug on, past the edge of that ground and down into the dark where the gaze's creatures wait out the day, and the dark took them one drift at a time. Their tunnels are full of those creatures still |
| Who are the swamp's witches? | The Marsh Mothers' heirs, a living people of the swamps, and people, not monsters: no witch comes with the night. They keep a hut on stilts over a black pool because their mothers did, and have forgotten why: it stands over the Marsh Mothers' stair down to their hall. Their brews heal because the pool's water carries the shard's sight, and their water once showed far places |
| What did Wend bargain with the witches? | As the Gazebound's finder, with his ember still burning, he asked their black water where the halls lay, and it showed him; his maps of the halls began there. He paid with his word that no Gazebound would ever come into the swamp. The next autumn they came and burned two huts looking for the hall, and never found it. The witches curse his name, and the debt is owed by whoever carries his ember |
| Who are the Tide Folk? | A living people of a sheltered bay who fish by night. Their shard keeps the eye off their boats and the Drowned City's keeps the deep things out of the bay; the bay's guardians bow to an ember |
| Who are the Sand Singers? | Nomads of the desert who dug a hall under the dunes. A great worm came up out of the deep sand and swallowed their stone; they sing to it, and it keeps the raiders off their ground |
| What is the worm? | A worm that swallowed the Sand Singers' shard and grew on it. It sees, it rises to a horn's song, and it can be hurt only while it is up |
| Who were the Jungle Folk? | A people of the jungle whose shard taught them how red dust carries a thought from stone to stone. They built locks that never slept, and the eye never found the way in, so it found them one by one. Their stone sleeps until someone writes their word: wake |
| Who are the Twelve Peoples now? | Four still live in their land: the swamp's witches, heirs of the Marsh Mothers; the Tide Folk; the Sand Singers; and the Grass Kingdom. The rest are scattered or dead, and only their places remain |
| What are the peoples' places? | What each people left near its hall: a winter house, a ship, a temple, a grave. What they could not carry is still there, and some of it, such as the Frost Walkers' snowshoes, opens the land |
| Who are the settled? | The villages, towns and capitals: the people who forgot the oath. The gaze burns them, one by one |
| What do the settled remember? | Wayfarers only as a story told to children: walkers who carried fire and never stayed. They have no name for what burns their towns; they say "they", or "the night" |
| Who are the Gazebound? | An Illager cult under the Grass King's heir that serves the gaze to use it: they believe its sight will run through them once the Eye is set. Their outposts, camps, forts and mansions stand across the world |
| What is the Gazebound's mark? | An open eye: the eye they serve, on their banners and cut wherever they have been |
| What do the Gazebound ask on the road? | After an ember: "Seen anyone with a glowing coal in their hand?" They are looking for whoever carries an ember |
| Where do the Illagers come from? | They are the Grass Kingdom's outcasts and their children |
| Why do monsters leave the Gazebound alone? | At their oath, every Gazebound lets the gaze's creatures open their chest just far enough to look. Not all survive it; those who do carry the scar over the heart, and the gaze never searches a heart twice. They call it a blessing |
| Why do the Gazebound feed Wayfarers? | Only an ember can lift a shard, and an ember cannot be taken. So they feed Wayfarers, follow their smoke, and wait |
| Why do they raid a village after I kill a captain? | His band takes his death out on the next village the killer walks into, to show the settled what sheltering a Wayfarer costs |
| Who are the Keepers? | Wayfarers who would not walk, few and old, guarding the frames at the far ring. They know what a thirteenth shard is, and they bar the way |
| How did a shard get into Maud's bell? | Forty-five summers ago, young and walking, Wend lifted the First Village's shard from its empty hall to keep it from the Gazebound searching the plains. On the ruined green, founders from the river crossing were casting the crossing's first bell from the dead village's bronze; he dropped the shard into their melt. Maud, nine, carried charcoal for that fire. The crossing had stood as a hamlet for centuries; with its bell it became a town, Tollbridge, and grew, and the bell shone, until the gaze burned it |
| What was the reading-house? | The one house the Keepers kept on the near lands, in the old script, for any Wayfarer who came to learn the oath: copies of the far ring's records, kept by one old Keeper and an apprentice. It stood longer than anything of theirs outside the ring, nobody came in the last Keeper's lifetime, and three nights before the team found it the night came for it. Ansel held its door; Alruna lived in its vault |
| What is the old script? | The writing of the first Wayfarers, kept by the Keepers. The marks that crawl over an enchanting table are its letters: the smiths' script took a handful of its words for setting enchantments into steel and kept nothing else, so only the Keepers and their apprentices still read it |

## The ending

| Door | What it answers | What it gives | What it costs | The world after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blind it | I believe Wend | The Eye set and the dragon killed while it is half-blind; the fewer shards set, the blinder it fights | The voice I grew to like dies with it | The road's rules end, and planting, breeding, beds and chests come back: "Build a home" |
| Give it its sight | I believe the voice | The home it promised, where nothing comes at night | Every lit window shines in its sight again, and it cannot look away; only the lands where I left a shard stay dark | What the road forbade comes back only in that home; everywhere else its creatures hunt every night |
| Keep the oath | I refuse what I am | Every shard given back to the peoples who trust me, and the road | No home: one day I pass the ember on | The road goes on |
| Close the Eye | I accept what I am | I step into the frame, and the Eye, with me in it, closes for good. The dragon does not die; for the first time it sleeps, and the gaze ends | Myself | The rules end as with Blind it. If Pip lived, I walk on as Pip, grown, carrying my ember; if not, the world is gone for me |
